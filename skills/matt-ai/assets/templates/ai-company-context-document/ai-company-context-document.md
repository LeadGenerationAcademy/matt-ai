# AI Company Context Document

**AI Company Context Document**

**What and why**

The AI Company Context Document is for you to fill out once, and then be able to give it to AI, like ChatGPT and Claude, and have it fully understand who you are, what you do, and who you are targeting. This will help a lot when crafting copy for your ads, landing pages, and email flows. It makes sure that AI is using information that’s actually true to your business and copy that sounds like it’s actually coming from you.

In the Framework section below, I’ve given you the exact template for you to fill out. For each section of the template, try to fill out as many as you can, the more the better, but if you can’t finish filling out a section, do more research, or move onto the next section.

#### **Global Copywriting Rules**

- Write for one exact ICP, not a general audience.

- Every piece should communicate one main idea.

- Every piece should have one clear purpose and next step.

- Lead with the most important point. Do not waste the opening.

- Write like one person speaking directly to another person.

- Use plain English and short paragraphs.

- Remove repetition, filler, and unnecessary explanations.

- Be specific. Use real problems, circumstances, outcomes, numbers, examples, and proof.

- Use the same language the ICP uses to describe its situation.

- Preserve the author’s actual tone.

- Do not use generic AI language, corporate language, or fake enthusiasm.

- Do not invent facts, stories, examples, results, guarantees, or claims.

- Make the offer, outcome, and next step easy to understand.

- Answer the questions and objections preventing the reader from taking that next step.

- Keep the ICP, offer, big idea, terminology, claims, and expectations consistent.

- The subject line or opening must accurately match what follows.

- Every piece should make sense without requiring missing context.

- Use only as many words as required to make the point properly.

- End once the point has been made.

#### **Pre-Call Flow**

#### **What and Why**

People mostly miss sales calls because they still need more information.

The Pre-Call Flow answers their questions and objections before the call. We will also do this through the thank-you page, FAQ videos, and retargeting ads, but the emails do most of the work.

Keep the booking window at 72 hours or less. Someone who books 72 hours in advance can receive all 18 emails. Someone who books 24 hours in advance will only receive six.

These emails often receive open rates above 70%. You will discuss the same subjects on the sales call anyway, so you might as well answer them beforehand.

As long as the emails are useful, prospects will be happy to receive them.

#### **Framework**

- Create 18 emails.

- Answer one FAQ or objection per email.

- Send one email every four hours between booking and the call.

- Keep each email between 300 and 1,000 words.

- Treat all 18 emails as one connected series.

- Arrange them by importance because most prospects will not receive all 18.

- Structure each answer using:

  - Theory or overview

  - Example

  - Action steps

- Reference the upcoming call differently in each email.

- Change the introduction and sign-off throughout the series while keeping them serious.

- Create a subject line based on the FAQ using seven words or fewer.

- Create preview text that builds curiosity using five words or fewer.

- End every email with links to the one-pager and three-minute Loom.

- Follow the Global Copywriting Rules.

#### **Workflow**

1.  Someone books a call and enters the Pre-Call Flow.

2.  They receive one email every four hours.

3.  The number of emails they receive depends on how far away the call is.

4.  The emails stop when the call takes place.

5.  Once the call recording is created, remove them from the Pre-Call Flow.

#### **Steps**

1.  Export your sales-call transcripts.

2.  Identify the 18 most common FAQs and objections.

3.  Arrange them by importance.

4.  Answer each using theory or overview, an example, and action steps.

5.  Add the FAQs, answers, proof, one-pager, and Loom link to the AI Company Context Document.

6.  Give the AI Company Context Document to AI with the prompt below.

7.  Review all 18 emails as one complete series.

8.  Add them to your email software and test the flow.

#### **No Show Flow**

#### 

#### **What and Why**

A percentage of prospects will not show up for their scheduled sales call.

These people normally fall through the cracks because nobody consistently follows up with them. The No-Show Flow automates that follow-up and gives them another opportunity to book.

The flow can begin when someone manually marks the prospect as a no-show inside the CRM.

You can also automate it based on the call recording. If the recording is under five minutes, it likely means the prospect never attended.

Every email should give them more information, answer a question or objection, and encourage them to book another call.

#### **Framework**

Create 24 emails sent over eight weeks:

- One email per day for the first two weeks

- Three emails per week for the next two weeks

- One email per week for the final four weeks

Each email should:

- Answer one FAQ or objection.

- Include concrete proof.

- Stay between 300 and 1,000 words.

- Reference the missed scheduled call near the beginning.

- Reference it again when asking them to rebook.

- Change the beginning, ending, and sign-off throughout the series.

- Include a clear CTA and link to book another call.

- Follow the Global Copywriting Rules.

Treat all 24 emails as one connected series.

Keep the language serious and professional. Do not shame them for missing the call.

1.  The prospect misses the scheduled call.

2.  Mark them as a no-show inside the CRM.

3.  Alternatively, trigger the flow when the call recording is under five minutes.

4.  Send one email per day for two weeks.

5.  Send three emails per week for two weeks.

6.  Send one email per week for four weeks.

7.  If they book another call, remove them from this flow and place them into the Pre-Call Flow.

8.  If they do not book, the flow ends after eight weeks.

#### **What and Why**

The 90-Day Winback Flow reactivates prospects 90 days after their original sales call.

A lot can change in 90 days. Their circumstances may be different, and your company should have new information, proof, and insights to share.

Every email should look like a short email you manually wrote to one person. It should not look like a marketing email.

The CTA should send them to:

- A one-pager containing the call-booking link

- A three-minute Loom reviewing the one-pager

1.  Record the video on your phone.

3.  Keep it natural and unedited.

4.  Save it somewhere easy to find on your phone.
