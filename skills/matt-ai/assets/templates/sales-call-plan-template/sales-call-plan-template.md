# Sales Call Plan Template

**Sales Call Plan**

You don't need a sales script, but you do need a plan: a standardized series of steps you take on every call.

As the founder taking every call yourself, you can often get away with winging it because you carry all the knowledge and authority.

Once you hire your first sales rep, that's no longer true. You need a set system to plug them into.

The goal of this document is to build that standardized Sales Call Plan, covering:

- Your Intro

- Five Questions

- Demo

- Objection Handling

- Closing

This allows every rep to run the same process on every call.

This document is split into seven sections/tabs:

- Intro

- Five Questions

- Demo

- Objection Handling

- Closing/Next Steps

- Full Example

- Your Full Sales Call Plan

Each of the five sales call plan sections/tabs gives you the instructions and my Example, followed by space to write your own version.

The Full Example tab is where I will have my full Sales Call Plan script onto one page.

Your Full Sales Call Plan tab is where you’ll paste in all the five sections that you’ve written for yourself and have it all on one page.

**Intro**

**Instructions**

Create a 30-second highlight reel for yourself. The purpose is to take command of the call right away, build authority, and then start asking questions, because the one who is asking the questions is the one who is in control.

**Matt's Example**

Hey, so my name is Matt. Over the last 12 years, I've sold three marketing agencies that have done \$72.5 million in total revenue combined.

Now, I work with about 80 clients at a time to help them acquire more customers. My main belief and reason that I do it like this is because I believe people need to learn to do it themselves primarily. I'd be doing people a disservice by doing it for them.

**Transition:** So, before we get started, what would be an ideal outcome for this meeting?

**Five Questions**

**Instructions**

Set yourself up as the authority, and flip the script to where they are qualifying themselves to you instead of the other way around. This is best done by asking a series of five questions.

**Ask These 5 Questions**

1.  What would need to happen for it to be clear that implementing our solution was successful and justified the investment?

2.  Why can't you do this today?

3.  Why now? What happens if you wait?

4.  What is it about \[feature/solution\] that you believe will be right for you?

5.  The investment range to work with us is anywhere from X to Y depending on your requirements… any issues with those initial numbers?

**Matt's Example**

This isn't a good fit for everyone and I turn away about 60% of people on these calls.

So, do you mind answering a few questions to see how you compare to our best clients?

- The investment range to work with us is anywhere from X to Y depending on your requirements… any issues with those initial numbers?

**Transition:** Okay, from what you've told me, I think we can help. Would you mind if I share my screen and show you exactly how this works?

**Demo**

**Instructions**

Outline a complete demo of your product or service. This should be between 5-7 minutes on a 30 minute call. This should either likely be a pre-made Miro board or pre-made slides. Make sure you pre-emptively overcome objections using this so you don't have to overcome them later. Also, make sure that you write down every single possible thing you need to cover to ensure consistency.

**Objection Handling**

**Instructions**

There will almost always be 6-12 main objections that you will hear over and over again. Your job now is to list out all of the objections, and then list three options on how to overcome each. These options could be examples, metaphors, stories, etc. When you do this over and over again with each call, you will learn to overcome these objections so effortlessly it is not even funny.

**Matt's Example**

**Objection \#1: Price**

**Metaphor:** The way I see it, this program is basically a toll road. You pay to get on it, and in exchange, you skip all the traffic sitting on the free roads. That traffic is the four years of trial and error most people go through trying to figure this out on their own.

**Example:** A lot of the people I talk to have already paid an agency for three to six months, got a handful of bad leads, and walked away with nothing they could run themselves. This costs a fraction of that, and at the end, the system is yours.

**Other:** Let me ask you this. What is one new client worth to you over a year? Now, how many clients would you need from this to cover the investment? For most people, it's one.

**Objection \#2: Will it work for me?**

**Metaphor:** Think of the program as a set of train tracks. If you stay on the tracks, the train gets to the station, and it really doesn't matter who's driving. The tracks are the steps, and all you have to do is stay on them.

**Example:** I've worked with about 1,800 people at this point. The ones who got results weren't all the same type of person, but they all did the steps every single day. That's the only thing they had in common.

**Other:** What specifically makes you think you'd be the exception? Walk me through it, because if there's a real reason, I want to know about it before you spend a dollar.

**Objection \#3: Will it work for my service?**

**Metaphor:** The way your customer buys is basically a staircase with four steps. They see your ad, they book a call, they show up, and they pay. It doesn't matter if you sell roofing or software, your customer still has to climb the exact same four steps to get to you.

**Example:** In the program, we walk through roofing companies, residential HVAC, auto insurance and agencies. Completely different businesses, but every single one of them runs through the same four phases: clicks, calls, shows and closes.

**Other:** Walk me through how a customer finds you and buys from you right now. Let's go step by step, and I'll show you exactly where each one fits into clicks, calls, shows and closes.

**Objection \#4: Do I have to do it all?**

**Metaphor:** At the start, you're the prototype. Every closer you hire later is basically going to be a copy of how you ran the calls, which is why you have to be the one who does it first.

**Example:** I make people take their own sales calls from Facebook ads at the start. Later on, they hire closers, and because they've done the calls themselves, they actually know how to build the sales process those closers plug into.

**Other:** You do it yourself first, you write down exactly how you did it, and then you hand that off. You're not doing it all forever. You're doing it long enough to know what you're handing off.

**Objection \#5: Not sure I have time for it**

**Metaphor:** A business running on referrals is a roof with a leak in it. Every slow month, you're running around with buckets trying to catch the water. You can keep doing that every time it rains, or you can take the time to actually fix the roof.

**Example:** The program asks for one hour a day. What would normally take four years of trial and error gets condensed into about four months.

**Other:** How many hours a week are you spending right now chasing referrals, or fixing things that aren't working? Now compare that to one hour a day on something that actually fixes the problem.

**Transition:** So, is there anything else stopping you from getting started today?

**Closing/Next Steps**

**Instructions**

After all objections are handled, give them a clear closing message that tells them how they will get an ROI as fast as possible (through quick wins in onboarding), what access they will get, how to pay, what to do between now and onboarding call, and what will happen on the onboarding call. If they are unwilling to pay on the call or five minutes after, make sure to book another meeting.

**Matt's Example**

- ROI the whole thing with the first few days with the personal, messaging and systems

- Access to modules, Matt AI, group chat, community events, AI tools, assets, and book our one on one calls

- I will send a payment link to email and phone. Pay it and you will get everything automatically in terms of access

- Between now and the onboarding call, make sure you finish the systems, messaging and personal modules. It will likely take 1-2 hours but will be the most important thing you've done in the last 12 months. On the onboarding call, we will build you a 14 day sprint.

- If they don't want to close right now, book a new meeting.

- If they buy transition: Perfect. I'm sending the payment link to your email and phone right now. Once that's done, you'll get access to everything automatically, and we’ll get you onboarded right away.

- If they don’t buy transition: No problem. Let's get a time booked now to finish this. Does \[day\] at \[time\] work for you?
