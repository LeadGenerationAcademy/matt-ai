# **Pillar: Accumulate \$1 billion in net worth**

## **Vision**

A billion, and most of it in companies I actually own rather than things I bought with the money from them.

None of it is cash sitting anywhere and I'm not going to pretend it is. At that number almost all of it is equity in businesses with people working in them, which means the number only exists because somebody who owes me nothing would pay it. That's the part I care about. Revenue is something you say about yourself. A valuation is someone else agreeing with you.

What I picture is looking at it on a normal Tuesday, seeing that four or five of my own companies are nearly all of it, and knowing not one of them falls apart if I don't show up.

## **What does this represent to me**

Ten million is a comfortable life. I already know what that looks like and I could probably get there without changing anything. It's boring.

I want to find out how far this actually goes. Not how far is sensible. How big it gets before I run out of whatever it is I have.

Three agencies was small. It felt big at the time and it isn't. Plenty of people have done it and most of them are nobody now. I don't want to be a guy who did one good thing in his twenties and spent the rest of it talking about it.

There's no noble version of this. I'm not doing it for my family and I'm not doing it so I can give it away later. I want to build something big enough that it's obvious, own all of it, and still be working on it at sixty.

Dying at eighty having run a decent business and had a nice life is the bad outcome. That's the whole thing. I don't want a nice life.

## **What Do I Achieve**

1.  Something I built is worth a billion to people who have no reason to be nice to me.

2.  I never take another meeting just because of who the guy is.

3.  Every company I own becomes optional, so I can fix them properly instead of protecting them.

4.  Whatever I build after this only has to be interesting to me.

5.  Nobody gets a vote. No investors, no board, nobody I owe anything to.

## **Who Do I Become**

1.  Someone who worked for twenty years without checking the scoreboard every month.

2.  A guy who can watch something he owns lose half its value and not change a single thing he's doing, because the valuation was never the point.

3.  I stop getting excited about a good month. A month stops meaning anything.

4.  I say no and then shut up. No explanation after it.

5.  The same to everybody regardless of what they're worth. Not there yet.

## **What Do I Think**

1.  I stop running numbers in my head during the day. The math is finished and there is nothing left to check.

2.  I think about what I actually want to exist in the world instead of what the market will pay for.

3.  I think in decades. A bad year stops being something I have to survive and becomes something I barely register.

4.  I think about which of my businesses I would still run if they paid me nothing, and I get rid of the rest.

5.  I think seriously about where all of this ends up when I am finished with it, because at that size it is going somewhere whether I decide or not.

## **What Do I Say**

1.  "No." Then nothing.

2.  "It's not about the money," and for once that's true, because right now it is.

3.  "That's a good number and I'm still not selling."

4.  "I'll fund it." To people nobody else will touch.

5.  I stop telling people what I'm worth. Right now I bring it up more than I'd like to admit.

## **How Does It Feel**

1.  The businesses feel like things I picked rather than things I'm carrying.

2.  I don't open a revenue dashboard on a Saturday. Eventually I stop wanting to.

3.  I sleep without going through tomorrow first.

4.  Quieter than I expect. I think hitting it will feel like something and I don't think it will.

5.  Probably boring, which is why there'll be other things by then.

## **What Does It Mean**

1.  Twenty years in a room instead of doing something easier was the right call.

2.  I never have to defend how I live to anybody again.

3.  The businesses I still own are the ones I actually want to own.

4.  Money stops being a factor in decisions for the people around me.

5.  The rest of my time is mine.

## **What Are The Causes**

1.  Four hours a day, every day, for twenty years, without arguing with myself first about whether I feel like it.

2.  Businesses that make real cash, not a number I can only get at by selling.

3.  One company that gets big enough to make the three agencies look like practice.

4.  Owning all of it. No raise, no investors, nothing given away early for money I didn't need.

5.  Putting it back into the few things that compound instead of starting something new every time I get bored.

# **Pillar: Build one company that is the best in the world at what it does**

## **Vision**

One company that people inside the industry agree is the best. Not the biggest, not the most followed. The best at the actual work.

The way you'd know is boring. Competitors send us the clients they can't help. People who leave to start their own thing end up running a worse version of what we do and everybody including them knows it. When somebody serious has a real problem in our category we're the only name that comes up, and when a competitor loses that deal they aren't surprised.

It doesn't need to be a household name. Most of the best companies in the world at any specific thing are invisible to normal people. I need the couple of hundred people who actually understand this work to have no argument about it.

## **What does this represent to me**

You can make a lot of money running something average. I've watched it happen and some of those guys are doing better than me. Right channel, right couple of years, a market that was easy for a while. Money on its own doesn't prove much.

Nobody is the best in the world at something by accident.

The agencies were fine. They made money and the clients were mostly happy. They weren't the best at anything, and if I'm honest part of why I sold is that I didn't want to find out whether I could make them that.

So this is the one where I find out. There's no way to fake it and no way to get there early. You have to stay in the same place long enough to run out of excuses, and I've never done that.

**What do I achieve**

1.  The best people in the industry want to work here. A couple of them take a pay cut for it.

2.  We stop bidding on work we shouldn't win.

3.  I can send a client to a competitor without thinking about it, because there's nothing over there.

4.  Clients stay for years and stop looking around.

5.  People copy how we do it and it doesn't matter, because the thing that makes it work isn't the part they can see.

**Who do I become**

1.  Someone who is actually good at the work rather than good at talking about the work.

2.  The guy who kills a product line that makes money because it isn't good enough. Never done this.

3.  I stop comparing us to competitors, because there isn't anything to compare to.

4.  Patient with the work. Not patient with people who don't care about it.

5.  Someone who can be told the work isn't good enough without getting defensive. Currently bad at this.

**What do I think**

1.  Is this the best it can be or is it just good enough to ship.

2.  About the craft instead of the multiple.

3.  I stop thinking about what competitors are doing at all.

4.  Ten years out on product decisions. What does this have to be in 2045.

5.  About who I'm training to be better at this than I am.

**What do I say**

1.  "That isn't good enough." Often, and to people who worked hard on it.

2.  "No" to clients we can't be excellent for, including when we need the money.

3.  "Go look at how they did it," about somebody better than us, without it bothering me.

4.  I stop explaining why we're different. It's obvious or it isn't.

5.  "We got that wrong." Publicly. It costs nothing because everyone already knows the rest is right.

**How does it feel**

1.  Calm before a big pitch, because there's no version where we're the worse option.

2.  I stop feeling whatever it is I feel when a competitor posts a win.

3.  Boring in the good way. Same work, better every year.

4.  Frustrating most of the time. The gap between good and best is a hundred small annoying things and none of them are interesting.

5.  Proud of the work itself rather than what the work earned, which I don't think I've ever had.

**What does it mean**

1.  The money came out of the work instead of the other way round.

2.  Nobody can say I got lucky.

3.  I picked one thing and stayed with it, which I have never once done.

4.  Everyone who worked here got better at their craft for having been here.

5.  It still works when I'm not in the building, because what's good is the system and not me.

**What are the causes**

1.  Twenty years on the same problem instead of five years on four different ones.

2.  Hiring people better than me at their part and then not overruling them.

3.  Putting money back into the product when taking it out would be easier and nobody would notice.

4.  Killing the parts of the business that make money and aren't excellent.

5.  Being honest with myself about whether the work is actually good, which is the part I'm worst at.

**Goal: Build a \$200,000 MRR Skool Community With No Sales Calls or One-on-One Calls**

## **SMART**

**S:** \$200,000 in monthly recurring revenue from the Skool community, with nobody taking a sales call or a one-on-one call to produce any of it.

**M:** Monthly recurring revenue read off Skool's billing on the 1st of the month.

**A:** At \$1,500 a month that is about 133 active members. The traffic already exists on YouTube. What has to change is the path from watching a video to joining, and how good the material is once somebody is inside.

**R:** It is the income I described in my pillars in its cleanest form. It pays monthly, it needs nobody in a room with me, and it runs off content I am already making.

**T:** 31 August 2027.

# 

## **Vision**

A hundred and thirty people inside one community, paying every month, almost none of whom I have ever spoken to.

Somebody watches a two-hour video on YouTube, decides for themselves, and joins. No application, no calendar link, no closer. It happens while I am asleep and the notification is the first I know about it.

I log in once a day, answer questions in the feed, run one call a week for everybody at once, and that is the entire job.

### 

## **What does this represent to me**

Every business I have built before this needed somebody selling. The agencies needed me on calls, and once I hired closers I was managing closers instead. This would be the first thing I own where the selling is done by the work itself.

I also want to find out whether the material is good enough to sell without a human in the loop. A sales call covers for weak content. This does not.

### 

## **What do I achieve**

1.  \$200,000 arriving every month with nothing on my calendar to produce it.

2.  An audience that buys from watching rather than from being pitched.

3.  A business I can run from anywhere with an internet connection.

4.  Proof that the material stands up on its own.

5.  Income that has nothing to do with HyperOptimal.

### 

## **Who do I become**

1.  Someone who builds things that work without him in the room.

2.  Someone who publishes whether or not it is convenient that week.

3.  Someone who answers questions in a feed instead of on a call.

4.  Someone who fixes the content when sales drop instead of hiring another closer.

5.  Someone whose calendar is empty on purpose.

### 

## **What do I think**

1.  I think about what to make rather than who to call.

2.  I think about the members already inside rather than the ones who have not joined.

3.  I judge a piece of content by whether anybody joined after it.

4.  I stop treating my calendar as something other people fill.

5.  I think about making the thing better rather than selling it harder.

### 

## **What do I say**

1.  "I do not take calls." To everybody, including people with money.

2.  "Watch the video. If it makes sense, join."

3.  "I have never spoken to most of my customers."

4.  "The content is the sales process."

5.  I stop explaining why there is no sales team.

### 

## **How does it feel**

1.  Nothing lands on my calendar that I did not put there myself.

2.  The revenue arrives on the 1st whether the week went well or not.

3.  Quieter than any business I have run.

4.  Strange at first. I built three agencies on calls and this one runs without them.

5.  The work feels like making things again.

### 

## **What does it mean**

1.  The material is good enough to stand on its own.

2.  I do not have to be present for the business to make money.

3.  The years I spent on calls taught me enough to never need another one.

4.  I reach far more people than I ever could one at a time.

5.  Whatever I build after this can work the same way.

### 

## **What are the causes**

1.  Publishing long-form YouTube every week without missing.

2.  Making the free material better than what most people charge for.

3.  Building the community so members get answers without me.

4.  Removing every step between watching the video and joining.

5.  Four Deep Work Hours a day going into the community rather than into client work.

**Goal: Get My Purple Belt In Jiu-Jitsu**

## **SMART**

**S:** Purple belt, awarded by my coach. I have been a blue belt for about eighteen months.

**M:** The belt. There is no arguing about whether you have it.

**A:** Blue to purple takes most people at the gym two to three years, and most of them train twice a week. Four sessions a week plus competing is what makes twelve months realistic. That is around 200 mats.

**R:** It is the next step in the jiu-jitsu pillar, and it is the belt where almost everybody stops.

**T:** 31 October 2027.

# 

## **Vision**

Four sessions a week for another year, at the level where I finally stop getting carried by being big.

By the end of it I can control blue belts without working for it, I am not getting smashed by every purple in the room, and I have stopped losing to the same two positions I have been losing to since I started.

Purple is where you stop being a guy who does jiu-jitsu and start being a jiu-jitsu guy. The belt is just the moment somebody else says it out loud.

### 

## **What does this represent to me**

Eighteen months in, I know exactly how little eighteen months buys you.

When I started I assumed being 6'5" and 230 would count for something. It counts for about six seconds. A guy 70 pounds lighter who has been doing this for four years takes my back, and there is no version of that I can talk my way out of afterward.

That was the reason I started and it is still the reason. I am the one everybody in my house depends on. I have handled the money side of that. The other side is that if something happens with my family standing there, it is me and whatever I can actually do, and I want to keep closing the gap between what I think that is and what it really is.

Blue is also where everyone quits. It is never dramatic. They get busy, then they get injured, then they come back less, and then they stop coming. I have watched about eight people do it since I started and every one of them thought they would be back.

### 

## **What do I achieve**

6.  I get the purple belt from somebody whose opinion is actually worth having.

7.  I put another 200 sessions behind me, on top of the ones I already have.

8.  I compete at least three times before the promotion.

9.  I stop losing from the two positions that have beaten me all year.

10. I get past the belt where most people stop.

### 

## **Who do I become**

6.  I become someone who kept going after the novelty was completely gone.

7.  I become a real problem for an untrained man instead of a guy who assumes he would be.

8.  I train at the same rate when the business is busy, which is the thing I am worst at right now.

9.  I become consistent over years instead of in bursts, which I have never managed outside of work.

10. I get comfortable being the worst guy in the round and stop choosing who I roll with.

### 

## **What do I think**

6.  I stop keeping score against the other blue belts.

7.  I think about the positions I am bad at instead of the ones I enjoy drilling.

8.  I think in years now. Purple is not the end of anything, it is year three of ten.

9.  I think about my family when I do not want to go, and that gets me out the door more reliably than anything else I have tried.

10. I stop planning my week in the middle of rounds.

### 

## **What do I say**

6.  "I train four times a week," and it is the only thing in my calendar that nothing moves.

7.  "Can't, I've got jiu-jitsu," to a client, without apologizing for it.

8.  "He's better than me and he's ten years younger," which costs me nothing to say now.

9.  "Show me that again," to a kid half my age, in front of the whole room.

10. "I'm not quitting at blue," mostly to myself, in the car, on the nights I nearly do.

### 

## **How does it feel**

6.  It feels less exciting than the first year, and that is exactly the test.

7.  I am injured a lot and I train anyway, just differently.

8.  The drive there is still the hardest part, and I have never once regretted going after I am on the mat.

9.  It is one hour, four times a week, where nothing else exists.

10. The belt itself feels like almost nothing, because I will have known where I am for months.

### 

## **What does it mean**

6.  If somebody puts hands on my wife or my kids while I am standing there, I am closer to knowing what happens next than I was a year ago.

7.  Their safety leans a little less on somebody else arriving in time.

8.  I can physically back up the life I built instead of only paying for it.

9.  My kids see a man who trains, not a man who says he used to.

10. I did not quit where everybody quits, so black belt is now an actual plan instead of something I say.

### 

## **What are the causes**

6.  I train four sessions a week for a year, including the injured weeks, the travel weeks, and the weeks the business is on fire.

7.  I roll with purples and browns instead of picking the rounds I can win.

8.  I compete while I am still losing, because that is the only place I find out what actually holds up.

9.  I drill the two positions I avoid, which is the entire reason I still lose from them.

10. I never let the business be the reason I miss, and it will offer itself as the reason every single week.

# **The Five Pillars**

1.  Reach 12% body fat at 215 pounds, with weightlifting and mixed martial arts targets on top of it

2.  Build the business community to \$2 million per month and make it the most revered, sought-after group in the world

3.  Grow HyperOptimal to \$100 million in ARR while staying extremely profitable, fully bootstrapped and free from investors

4.  Reach 10 million subscribers, listeners and readers, and become the most famous business thinker on the planet

5.  Accumulate \$1 billion in net worth

## **Where They Overlap**

The community, HyperOptimal and the audience are not three pursuits. They are three outputs of the same four hours. The hour I spend on a video is the reason somebody joins the community, and the community is the reason anybody trusts the software.

The body is the same self-mastery as the work, pointed somewhere else. If I am skipping the gym I am about to start skipping the four hours, and that has been true every single time.

The billion is not a sixth thing to chase. It is what the first four leave behind over twenty years if I keep taking money out instead of putting all of it back in.

Two of these cannot be worked on directly. Most revered group in the world and most famous business thinker on the planet are not tasks. They are what happens if I am right in public, repeatedly, for long enough, which means the only thing I can actually schedule is being right and being public.

## **Vision**

Five pillars, one day, four hours.

Right now they look like five projects fighting over the same calendar. They are not. Once I saw that the community, the software and the audience all come out of the same block of work, the whole thing stopped being a scheduling problem.

The day is not complicated. I get up early, I weigh 215 and I can see what I am supposed to see, I do four hours on the thing that matters before most people have opened their email, and I train in the afternoon. The community does \$2 million a month and I am not on a call with any of them. HyperOptimal does \$100 million a year and there is nobody on a cap table to explain a decision to. A video goes out, ten million people see it, and I did not pay for one of them.

Then I close the laptop and none of it needs me tomorrow either.

I have sold three agencies. I know exactly what it looks like to build something valuable that still owns your calendar. That is the thing I am trying not to do again.

## **What Do I Achieve**

1.  The community does \$2 million a month and I have not taken a one-on-one consulting call in years.

2.  HyperOptimal does \$100 million a year, fully bootstrapped, no investors, nobody to explain a decision to.

3.  Ten million people read, watch or listen, and I did not pay for any of them.

4.  215 pounds at 12%, with lifting numbers and mixed martial arts I would put on the record.

5.  A billion in net worth, which makes everything above it optional.

## **Who Do I Become**

1.  Somebody who does the four hours on the days he feels terrible, which is most of what this actually is.

2.  Someone who says the same thing to ten million people that he says in a room with two, because at that size the gap becomes the whole problem.

3.  Someone who still trains in the afternoon after the work is already done and it is 45 degrees outside.

4.  Someone who can be wrong in public about something he taught and correct it the same week.

5.  Someone who stops needing the community to grow every single month before he can feel fine about the day.

## **What Do I Think**

1.  I think about which of the five is furthest behind and put the hours there, instead of doing the one I enjoy most.

2.  I stop thinking about revenue during the day. It is being produced by things that already run without me.

3.  I think about the guy who joined this morning and whether what I am about to publish is going to work for him or just sound good.

4.  I think in twenty-year blocks about the billion and four-hour blocks about everything else.

5.  I think about what happens to all of it if I stop, and I build so that the answer is not much.

## **What Do I Say**

1.  "I do not do one-on-one anymore," and there is no number that changes it.

2.  "That was wrong, here is the corrected version," in public, without dressing it up first.

3.  "Go do the four hours for thirty days and then come back to me."

4.  "I am not raising," to every person who offers to put money into HyperOptimal.

5.  I stop announcing what I am about to build and put it out when it exists.

## **How Does It Feel**

1.  Boring in a way I did not expect. The day is the same day over and over, and the sameness is the entire point.

2.  The gym stops feeling like a separate obligation and becomes the second half of the work.

3.  There is no Sunday night anymore. The week does not start, because it never stopped.

4.  Quieter than the numbers suggest. Ten million people is an abstraction. The room is still me and a timer.

5.  Flat, some days. Hitting a target mostly means you now have a bigger one, which is how a hundred million turned into a billion.

## **What Does It Mean**

1.  The System works, and I can point at five separate parts of my life as proof instead of arguing about it.

2.  Everything I teach, I did first. Nobody gets to take that apart.

3.  The businesses are not holding me up. I am choosing them.

4.  The years I spent in a room instead of doing something easier bought something real.

5.  Whoever runs this after me does not have to take my word for any of it.

## **What Are The Causes**

1.  Four Deep Work Hours every day on the 10X Project before anything else in the day gets touched.

2.  Three gym sessions a week that happen whether the week went well or not.

3.  One 2.0 You Hour every day, which is where all five of these actually get built.

4.  Publishing on a schedule for years, past the point where it stops feeling like it is working.

5.  Taking money out of the businesses and leaving it somewhere boring instead of putting all of it into the next idea.

## **What Are The Standards**

1.  The four hours are not negotiable based on how the morning went.

2.  The food plan holds when I am traveling, which is the only time it actually gets tested.

3.  Anything I said would be done today gets done today, not moved onto tomorrow's War Map.

4.  Every hour gets recorded, including the bad ones. Especially the bad ones.

5.  Nothing gets taught in the community that I have not already done myself.

# **The Five Pillars**

6.  Build the company to \$100 million a year and still own every share of it

7.  Be in better physical condition at 60 than the men around me are at 35

8.  Raise three kids who choose to be around me when they are grown and have every option not to be

9.  Get my parents, my brother and my sister to the point where money is never a factor in a decision any of them make again

10. Give away \$50 million while I am alive and can see the impact it has

## **Where They Overlap**

The \$100 million and the \$50 million are the same pillar looked at from opposite ends. There is no version where I give away that kind of money without building the thing first, and there is no version where I build it and keep all of it that I would be happy about at 60.

My family and the company pull against each other and that is the part I have to be honest about. Every hour that goes into \$100 million is an hour that does not go into the kids, and the standard answer to that is that you do it for them, which is mostly a lie people tell themselves. What is actually true is that the business has to be built so it does not need me at 6pm. That is a design problem, not a motivation problem, and it is the single most important thing on this page.

The body is the same discipline as the work with a different target. Every stretch where I have stopped training has been followed by a stretch where I stopped doing the mornings, and I have never once seen it go the other way.

My parents and my siblings are the reason I cannot sell early and take a comfortable number. A comfortable number covers me. It does not cover six other people for the rest of their lives, and it does not cover their kids.

## **Vision**

One company, built so it runs without me, funding everything else on this page.

\$100 million a year and my name on all of it. No partner I had to bring in during a bad year, no investor who owns a piece of a thing he never worked a day on. There are people running the departments who are better at their jobs than I ever was, and my job is picking them and then leaving them alone.

I am at the school at 3pm. Not sometimes, as a treat, and not as a thing I move a meeting for. I am there because the business was built to allow it from the beginning, which is a decision I make now, at \$3 million, not later when it is supposedly convenient.

I train five days a week at 60 and I am not fragile. My parents live somewhere they picked rather than somewhere they could afford. My brother stopped working a job he hates. And somewhere in there, fifty million dollars went to places I chose, while I was still alive to watch what it did.

My dad worked for the same company for 31 years and got a clock. That is the entire reason any of this is on the page.

## **What Do I Achieve**

6.  The company does \$100 million a year and I still own all of it.

7.  Nobody in my family ever has to consider money when they make a decision, including my parents and my brother and my sister.

8.  Fifty million dollars goes out the door to things I chose, while I am alive and can see what it did.

9.  I am 60 years old and physically capable of anything I want to do.

10. My kids are grown and they call me because they want to, not because it is Sunday.

## **Who Do I Become**

6.  Somebody who built a company that does not collapse when he is at a school play.

7.  A man who is the same person at home as he is in a meeting, which is not currently true.

8.  Someone who can hand a department to a better operator and not go around him three weeks later.

9.  Somebody who gives money away without needing anybody to know he did it.

10. A man who trains at 60 because he never stopped, not because he started again.

## **What Do I Think**

6.  I think about which of these five is furthest behind and I put my hours there instead of into the one I enjoy.

7.  I think about whether this company could run for a month without me, and I fix whatever the answer says is broken.

8.  I think about my dad's clock when I want to take an easier option.

9.  I think in decades about the money and in days about my kids, because the kids are the only one of these with a deadline I cannot move.

10. I stop thinking about what the business did this month, because by then it is being run by people who are better at it than I am.

## **What Do I Say**

6.  "I'm out at 3," and I stop explaining why.

7.  "That's your call, not mine," and then I stay out of it, which is the hard half.

8.  "I'm not selling," to people who came with a real number.

9.  "We're not raising," and I will still be saying it in twenty years.

10. "Put it in my parents' name," and I never tell them what it cost.

## **How Does It Feel**

6.  Boring most days, in the way that means it is working. The same morning over and over.

7.  Uncomfortable every time I hand something over, because I am still faster at it than the person I gave it to.

8.  Physically better than it has any right to feel at 60.

9.  Quieter around money than I expect. Giving away fifty million turns out to be a series of ordinary afternoons.

10. Settled in a way I have never felt, because nobody I love is one bad month away from anything.

## **What Does It Mean**

6.  My kids grow up with a father who was actually there, not one who provided from another room.

7.  My parents get to stop. Thirty-one years and a clock ends with me.

8.  The money came out of something I built, so nobody gets to say I got lucky or that somebody handed it to me.

9.  I proved the business could be big and still be mine, which almost everybody at my level gives up on.

10. The fifty million does something while I can still watch it, instead of becoming a paragraph in a will.

## **What Are The Causes**

6.  Four Deep Work Hours every morning before anybody in the company can reach me.

7.  Hiring people better than me at their part and then not overruling them, which is the actual constraint on all of this.

8.  Building the org chart for a \$100 million company now, at \$3 million, so the structure is already there when the revenue shows up.

9.  Three training sessions a week for thirty years without a gap I have to explain to myself.

10. Leaving the office at 3pm starting this week, so the business gets built around it instead of the other way around.

## **What Are The Standards**

6.  The four hours happen regardless of how the morning went or what came in overnight.

7.  I leave at 3pm. If the business cannot handle that, the business is the problem and I fix the business.

8.  I do not overrule a department head in front of his team, ever.

9.  Anything I said I would do today gets done today and does not move to tomorrow's War Map.

10. I never put money into an expansion that requires me to be in two places at once.

# **My Average Wednesday**

I wake up before the alarm, which I stopped finding impressive a long time ago. It is dark in Dubai and it stays dark for another hour, and that hour is the entire reason the rest of the day works.

The scale says 215. It has said 215 for about two years. The only remotely interesting thing about that number now is how boring it has become to me.

Twenty minutes on the treadmill, no phone, no music. I used to think the walking was for my body. It is not, really. It is the twenty minutes where I work out what I am actually going to do with the four hours, so that by the time I get off it there is nothing left to decide.

Numbers into the spreadsheets. Then I am working before six.

It is Wednesday, so the first hour is the Anti-Worry Hour. Most of the list is small and stupid. A renewal, a form, a thing that needs a signature. But there is one on there that has been sitting for four months because it involves a conversation I do not want to have, and I do it at 6:20 in the morning, badly, and then it is done and I get most of an hour back in my head that I did not have yesterday.

Hours two, three and four are HyperOptimal, and today that means the pricing page. A hundred million a year in recurring revenue and the pricing page is still the highest leverage surface in the company. Nobody believes that until they see what one line does to conversion at that volume.

Hour two is bad. I write four versions of the same headline and every one of them is worse than what is already live. I sit there for most of the hour producing nothing, which I have learned not to panic about.

Hour three is not bad. The thing opens up about fifteen minutes in and I look up and the timer has gone.

By late morning there are four notes on the wall and an X through Wednesday on the calendar, and everything after this point is extra. That is the part that took me twenty years to understand. Not that I get more done. That by eleven in the morning nothing else can ruin the day.

Somebody on the team sends a screenshot of a churn number and asks if he should be worried. He should not be worried. I tell him why in four sentences and go back to what I was doing.

The 2.0 Hour is in the afternoon. Today it is the Self Mastery List, because I have caught myself checking messages between hours again, which is exactly how this starts coming apart. I write down what actually happens in that moment and what I am going to do instead, and I will probably have to write it again in six months.

Evening is not work. It is dinner at home and twenty pages before bed, the only reading I do that is not for anything.

Desk cleared, tomorrow laid out, phone left outside the bedroom.

Then I do it again.

## **How I Feel While I Am Doing It**

1.  Clear-headed from about 5:30, not because I am a morning person but because nothing has reached me yet.

2.  Relieved after the Anti-Worry Hour, every single time, and always more than the task deserved.

3.  Frustrated during hour two and fine with being frustrated, because the hour still counts and the timer does not care.

4.  Completely absorbed in hour three, which is the closest thing to enjoyment any of this produces.

5.  Flat when I put the X on the calendar. I thought hitting this life would feel like something. It mostly feels like Wednesday, which I have decided is the point.

# **My Average Wednesday**

I am up at 4:40 and in the gym by five. I am 45 and I lift numbers I could not have touched at 30, which is not a brag. It is just what happens when you do the same thing for fifteen years and never take the six months off.

Home by 6:15. The numbers go into the sheet before anything else. Weight, yesterday's calls booked, close rate, cash position. Four minutes, and then I know what kind of day it is before anybody else gets to tell me.

Breakfast with the kids is loud and stupid and my phone is in the other room. That rule was hard to keep for about two years and it has not been hard since.

Drop off. In the office at eight.

Wednesday means the first hour is the Anti-Worry Hour. Most of the list is nothing. A lease renewal, an insurance form, a text I have not answered in three weeks. But one thing on there has been sitting since May, because it is a conversation with a guy who has been with me since we had four trucks and is no longer good enough for the job he is in. I make that call at 8:20 in the morning. It goes badly and it is done, and I get back an hour of my own head that I have not had since May.

Hours two, three and four are the pay plan. We do \$100 million a year and I am rewriting the technician comp plan for the fourth time in nine years, because in this business the pay plan is the company. Everything else is downstream of what you pay a man to do. Nobody believes that until they watch one change behavior across 300 techs inside a month.

Hour two is bad. I build three versions and all three of them quietly punish the best techs, which is what every bad pay plan in this industry does. I spend most of the hour producing nothing I can use, and I stopped panicking about that a long time ago.

Hour three is not bad. It cracks about twenty minutes in and I look up and the timer is gone.

Four notes on the wall by noon and an X through Wednesday. Everything after this point is extra. That is the part that took me a decade to understand. Not that I get more done, but that by lunchtime nothing else can wreck the day.

After lunch I read the regional reports. My GM in San Antonio let a dispatcher go that I would have kept. He is probably right, and I am definitely not calling him about it. Sitting on my hands is most of the job now and it is the part I am worst at.

The 2.0 Hour is at one. Today it is the Self Mastery List, because I caught myself opening the dispatch board twice during the four hours last week, and that is exactly how this starts coming apart. I write down what is actually happening in that moment and what I am going to do instead. I will probably write it again next year.

Out at three. I sit in the car line for eleven minutes and read, and those eleven minutes are the thing the entire company was built around, which sounds ridiculous written down and is true anyway.

My mom texts about something small. Whatever it is, it is handled, and she has not had to think about money in nine years.

A transfer went out on the first to a place I picked, and nobody at the office knows about it. I have not thought about it once today, which I think is right.

Evening is not work. Dinner, one kid's practice, twenty pages before bed.

Desk cleared, tomorrow written down, phone left in the kitchen.

My dad did 31 years for somebody else and got a clock. I do not think about that every day anymore. I think about it on the days I want to take the easier option, which is often enough.

Then I do it again.

6.  Awake in a way I was not for most of my thirties, and the only thing that changed is that I stopped negotiating with the alarm.

7.  Relieved after the Anti-Worry Hour, every single time, and always more than the thing deserved.
