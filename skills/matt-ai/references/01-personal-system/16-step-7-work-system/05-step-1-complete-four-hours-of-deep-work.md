### Prerequisites

- [Step #2: Block The First Four Hours](https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/c15cebb8?md=0e4c1da5f9d44f46bb4428172bd18b60) (Time Block Course)

### Example

**Wake up: 6:00 a.m.**

- 6:00 to 6:30 a.m.: Morning Routine (30 minutes) (You'll learn more about this in the Daily Operating System Course)
- 6:30 to 6:45 a.m.: 15-Minute Daily Setup (15 minutes) (You'll learn more about this in the Daily Operating System Course)
- 6:45 to 7:45 a.m.: Hour One (60 minutes)
- 7:45 to 8:00 a.m.: Break (15 minutes)
- 8:00 to 9:00 a.m.: Hour Two (60 minutes)
- 9:00 to 9:15 a.m.: Break (15 minutes)
- 9:15 to 10:15 a.m.: Hour Three (60 minutes)
- 10:15 to 10:30 a.m.: Break (15 minutes)
- 10:30 to 11:30 a.m.: Hour Four (60 minutes)

**Finished at 11:30 a.m.**, 5 hours, 30 minutes after waking up, with all four hours on the same project.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Keep all four hours focused on the same project.** Splitting them across four different things turns four deep work hours into four shallow ones.
2. **Start as early as you can and get through them as quickly as possible.** The blocks are already in your calendar from the Time Block course. Getting them done early is what stops the rest of the day interfering with them.
3. **Complete four separate 60-minute work periods every day.** Four separate hours, each one timed, not four hours of sitting at the desk and calling it work.
4. **Take a 10 to 15 minute break between each hour.** The break is part of the system, not a reward for being tired. It sits between the hours so you can actually hold focus through all four.

### Completion Audit

- Did you complete four separate 60-minute work periods?
- Did you start as early as you could and get through them as quickly as possible?
- Did you take a 10 to 15 minute break between each hour?
- Were all four hours focused on the same project?
