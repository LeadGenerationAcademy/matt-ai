### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Complete the four hours as early and as close together as possible.** These are four actual 60-minute hours, not four hours of work spread across an entire day.
2. **Record when you woke up and when you completed each hour.** This goes into your [Time Tracking Spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/14t3J3f-tSw_N91AczMsPPPvkLnuwWwAevCEuPEB1esk/edit?usp=sharing), which works out your efficiency from those times. [in this skill: assets/templates/tracking-spreadsheet/]
3. **Keep doing it even when it is difficult at first.** You may waste a lot of time before Hour One, your breaks may run long, and sitting there for the entire 60 minutes may be hard.
4. **Treat locking in as a skill.** After enough repetitions, starting work and completing the four hours becomes normal, the same way driving or tying your shoes became normal.
5. **Try to beat your efficiency.** If it takes you 11 hours after waking up to complete four hours of work, that is not very good, and you should know that.

### Completion Audit

- Did you complete the four hours as early and as close together as possible?
- Did you record when you woke up and when you completed each hour?
- Are you keeping at it even when it is difficult?
- Are you treating locking in as a skill?
- Did you try to beat your efficiency?
