### Examples

Here are some examples of destructive thoughts:

- Running imaginary arguments in your head

- Resenting people
- Assuming things will fail
- Telling yourself you are not built for this
- Envying people who are doing better than you
- Staying angry about things you cannot change
- Doubting whether any of it will work
- Worrying about things you cannot control
- Fearing what happens if it does not work out
- Telling yourself the day is already wasted

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Treat noticing yourself repeatedly thinking something destructive as the cue.** Imaginary arguments, resenting people, assuming things will fail, or telling yourself you are incapable.
2. **Ask yourself whether you actually want more of that in your life.** Your thoughts are seeds. Pay attention to what you keep planting.
3. **Stop continuing the conversation.** You cannot stop every unwanted thought from appearing, but you can stop feeding it once you have noticed it.
4. **Choose something more useful to think about.** Your Thought Habit List already has what you decided to think in the situations you face regularly.
5. **Get back to what you are doing.** The whole thing should take a few seconds.

### Completion Audit

- Did you treat noticing yourself repeatedly thinking something destructive as the cue?
- Did you ask yourself whether you actually want more of that in your life?
- Did you stop continuing the conversation?
- Did you choose something more useful to think about?
- Did you get back to what you are doing?
