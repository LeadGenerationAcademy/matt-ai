The three most important things in your life are your time, your health, and your finances. We need to track all three because knowing what is actually happening helps us make better decisions.

You can have an idea of how well you are doing that is completely different from reality.

You think you eat 2,500 calories per day, but it is closer to 4,000. You tell people your net margin is 30%, but it is actually 15%. You say you worked all day, then discover you completed 90 minutes of actual work.

The same thing happens when someone says they “close everyone” and “never churn.” Once they measure it, their close rate is 20% and churn is 15%.

Until you know those numbers, you are making decisions based on something that is wrong.

##### Awareness Is Protection

Two things usually happen when you start measuring something. You discover that you are doing worse than you thought, and seeing what is happening changes how you behave.

It becomes harder to keep telling yourself the same story when the numbers are in front of you.

If you thought you were following your food plan, seeing how much you actually ate gives you a reason to reconsider what you are doing. If you thought you were working hard, seeing how few focused hours you completed gives you something specific to address.

That awareness gives you an opportunity to change your behaviour. Continuing to measure then helps you see whether the change improved anything.

This is what we mean by What Gets Measured Gets Managed. We are using the information to make decisions about how we live.

##### Your Time

You need to know how much focused work you actually complete and how long it takes you to complete it.

Those are different things. You might finish four hours of Deep Work but drag them across the entire day because you started late or kept extending your breaks.

The Time Tracking Spreadsheet records when you wake up and when you finish each work hour. That allows you to see how quickly you begin, how efficiently you move through the hours, and how consistently you complete all four.

You can then identify what needs attention. Perhaps getting started is the problem. Perhaps you complete the first two hours quickly and lose most of the afternoon before finishing the others.

Knowing where the time goes gives you a better idea of what to change. You can work on the part of the day that is causing the problem and see whether your completion times improve.

##### Your Health

Your Health Tracking Spreadsheet gives you a record of your habits and what happens over time.

You can compare planned calories with actual calories, see your sleep and wake-up times, and look at how consistently you exercise. Your weight and the weekly and monthly summaries give you additional information to consider alongside those behaviours.

This matters because your memory of how you have been living can be very generous.

You might remember the days you followed your food plan and give very little thought to the days you went well over it. You might feel like you have been training consistently, but the record shows how often you actually trained.

Having that information helps you understand what you have been doing before deciding what needs to change.

It also gives you a longer view. One morning’s weight or one poor night of sleep is part of a record you can review over time. You have more to work with than whatever happened most recently.

##### Your Finances

You need to know what you expect to spend, what you actually spend, and how much money you currently have.

The Financial Tracking Spreadsheet brings those things together through Planned Expenses, Monthly Transactions, and Bank Balances.

Planned Expenses gives you something to compare your actual spending against. The transactions show where money came from and where it went, with personal and business expenses separated. Bank Balances shows the money currently held across your accounts.

That gives you a much clearer basis for decisions. If spending is higher than planned, you can see where the difference came from. If you are keeping less of your income than you expected, you can look at the expenses contributing to that result.

The comparison between months also helps you see what is changing. You can check whether a decision to reduce an expense actually reduced it and whether your overall financial position is improving.

The numbers need to represent what happened. Missing expenses, duplicate transactions, or counting a transfer as income will give you a misleading picture. The decisions are only as useful as the information you base them on.

##### The 15-Minute Daily Setup

We will bring this information into the beginning of your day through a 15-minute Daily Setup.

You will update the financial, health, and time records, then review the three to five assignments on your War Map that need your attention today. After that, you begin working.

Doing this regularly keeps you aware of what happened yesterday while you are deciding how to approach today.
