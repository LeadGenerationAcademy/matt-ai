### Example

1. My parents are still alive, and I can pick up the phone and speak to them.
2. I have customers who trust me enough to pay for something I created.
3. My wife stayed beside me during the year the business was struggling.
4. My body allows me to walk, lift weights, and do something about my health.
5. I have control over my calendar, even if I have not been using it properly.
6. My children still want to spend time with me.
7. An employee caught a mistake last week before it reached the customer.
8. I can afford food, a safe place to live, and the basic tools I need to work.
9. I have a friend who tells me the truth when I need to hear it.
10. I have learned skills that I can use to earn money even if this particular business does not work out.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Write down the things you genuinely feel grateful for.** We forget how good we have it, especially when we spend the whole day looking at what we have not achieved yet.
2. **Put at least ten things on it.** You can have as many as you want beyond that.
3. **Make sure you actually mean them.** Three real answers are more useful than 50 pieces of generic bullshit you do not actually feel.
4. **Create the first version during your 2.0 You Hour.** That hour is for developing your vision, working on your lists, and keeping your organized planning current.
5. **Use it as needed.** When you catch yourself becoming negative, resentful, discouraged, or stuck in self-doubt, open the list and use something from it.
6. **Add things when they come to mind.** You do not need to update this one on a schedule.

### Completion Audit

- Did you write down the things you genuinely feel grateful for?
- Did you put at least ten things on it?
- Do you actually mean them?
- Did you create the first version during your 2.0 You Hour?
- Did you use it as needed?
- Did you add things when they came to mind?
