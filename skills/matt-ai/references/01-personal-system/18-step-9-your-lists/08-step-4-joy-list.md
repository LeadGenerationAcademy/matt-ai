### Example

1. Taking my dog to his favorite park and letting him run around.
2. Playing the song I always turn up when it comes on in the car.
3. Getting a coffee and walking outside after my four hours.
4. My daughter running to the door when I get home.
5. Watching the old video that always makes my brother and me laugh.
6. Cooking breakfast with my wife when neither of us is rushing.
7. Calling the friend who makes me laugh about things that have nothing to do with business.
8. Sitting by the water for ten minutes without looking at a screen.
9. Playing basketball with my friends.
10. Looking through photos from the family vacation I actually enjoyed.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Write down the things that bring you genuine joy.** Anything that reliably makes you happy, changes your state, or brings you back to yourself.
2. **Make sure each one actually makes you smile or immediately lifts your mood.** That is the test for whether something belongs on the list.
3. **Do not put something on here because you think it should make you happy.** Do not turn this into an assignment and start inventing answers. Put down what actually does.
4. **Write most of the first version in one sitting.** You already know what most of these are, so this is not one you have to work out slowly.
5. **Use it when you need it.** When you catch yourself becoming negative, resentful, discouraged, or stuck in self-doubt, open the list and use something from it.
6. **Add things as you discover them.** This one does not need a weekly update, unlike your Self Mastery, Meaning and Thought Habits Lists.

### Completion Audit

- Did you write down the things that bring you genuine joy?
- Does each one actually make you smile or immediately lift your mood?
- Did you avoid putting something on there because you think it should make you happy?
- Did you write most of the first version in one sitting?
- Did you use it when you needed it?
- Did you add things as you discovered them?
