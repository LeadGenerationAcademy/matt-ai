Your Life Vision gives you something to work toward. These lists help you understand the ordinary decisions and situations that affect whether you actually get there.

You will notice things about yourself as you use the System. There will be moments when you lose control, thoughts you keep repeating, problems you have been avoiding, and things that could work better. You will also notice what makes you happy and what you already appreciate about your life.

The lists give you somewhere to keep that information and a way to use it. Each one serves a different purpose.

##### Your Self Mastery List

Self-mastery happens in specific moments. Your alarm goes off and you want to stay in bed. The break timer ends and you want another five minutes. Someone questions your decision and you want to defend yourself before hearing them out.

These are situations where you get to practice controlling your behaviour.

Your Self Mastery List makes the response you expect from yourself clear. Getting out of bed when the alarm goes off is a specific standard you can follow. So is returning to your desk when the break ends.

Repeatedly following through gives you practice behaving according to the standards you chose. When you keep struggling with the same situation, you have something specific to work on during your 2.0 You Hour.

The list develops as you understand where you lose control and what you need to become better at.

##### Your Anti-Worry List

Small unresolved problems can keep bothering you long after you first notice them. You still need to send something to the accountant, book an appointment, or deal with a subscription that keeps charging you.

Your Anti-Worry List connects each of those concerns to something you can actually do about it.

That matters because remembering a problem repeatedly does very little to resolve it. Having a clear action gives you a way to finish dealing with it.

The weekly Anti-Worry Hour provides time to handle those actions. This keeps the 2.0 You Hour available for the thinking and personal development it is meant for.

The list keeps changing because life keeps producing things that need attention. As problems get handled, they stop being outstanding items you have to keep remembering.

##### Your Joy List

Your Joy List contains things that reliably make you happy or change how you feel.

It might be a particular song, spending time with your dog, or an old video that always makes you laugh. What belongs on the list depends on your actual experience.

This gives you somewhere to turn when you want to lift your mood or enjoy yourself. You already know these things do something for you.

The list does not need constant development. Something that made you happy last month may still make you happy now. New entries come from discovering more of those things as you live your life.

##### Your Gratitude List

When you spend a lot of time thinking about your goals, you can become very focused on everything you have not achieved yet.

Your Gratitude List brings your attention back to things you already have and genuinely appreciate. Your parents may still be alive. You may have customers who trust you, people who supported you through a difficult period, or abilities you rarely stop to think about.

The details matter because you need to feel something when you read them. Three things you actually appreciate are more useful than 50 pieces of generic bullshit.

There can be overlap with your Joy List. Talking to your dad might make you happy, while remembering that you can still pick up the phone and call him gives you something to feel grateful for. You are looking at a different part of the same experience.

##### Your Forgiveness List

Your Forgiveness List contains things you are still holding against other people or yourself.

Some of these may have happened years ago and still affect how you feel about your life. You might keep returning to an opportunity you wasted, a decision that failed, or a period when you know you did not give your full effort.

I have shared this with maybe two dozen people. For roughly 90–95%, the bigger issue was forgiving themselves. That may be the case for you too.

The list makes it clear what you are still carrying so you can work through it. Something you regret may need attention during your 2.0 You Hour, especially if it keeps affecting how you think about yourself now.

Most of the list may be written once. Working through what is on it can take longer, which is why it remains available whenever you need to return to something.

##### Your Improvement List

Your Improvement List holds possible improvements to yourself, your life, and your business.

A lot of these will be small. An employee brief could include a completed example. A document everyone uses could be easier to find. Your break timer could be somewhere you can actually hear it.

These are things you often notice while doing something else. Keeping them gives you a chance to consider them properly later.

An idea going on the list does not automatically make it an assignment. Your 2.0 You Hour gives you time to decide which improvements deserve action and which can wait.

We can aim for a huge 2.0 transformation while making small improvements along the way. Both are useful. There is no reason to ignore an obvious improvement because it is only a small one.

##### Your Meaning List

Your Meaning List connects ordinary actions to things you personally care about.

Some of those actions will also appear on your Self Mastery List. The difference is what you are considering. Self-mastery concerns following through on the behaviour you chose. Meaning concerns why following through matters to you.

Finishing your four hours without dragging out the breaks might mean being able to close your laptop when your kids get home. Going to bed at the time you chose might mean giving your family a less exhausted and irritated version of you the following morning.

Those connections give the actions more significance. You can understand how a decision that seems small contributes to something you care about much more.

This is one of the ways your Life Vision becomes relevant during an ordinary day. As that vision gets clearer, you may find stronger and more personal reasons to follow through.

##### Your Thought Habit List

Your Thought Habit List concerns how you interpret situations you regularly encounter.

A prospect rejects your proposal. An employee makes a mistake. The work becomes difficult. Each situation can produce a familiar thought, and that thought affects what you do next.

You can deliberately choose a more useful way to think about it.

When an employee makes a mistake, considering whether you explained what good work looked like gives you something to investigate. When an assignment becomes difficult, seeing that moment as an opportunity to practice focus gives you a reason to stay with it.

The thought is useful because of how it helps you respond.

Returning to these thoughts makes them more familiar. The intention is for the thought you deliberately chose to become easier to remember when the situation actually happens. As you notice more of your recurring thoughts, you have more material to work with.

##### Your Foundational Causes List

Some ordinary decisions have effects that carry into many other parts of your life.

The decisions you make around bedtime affect how rested you feel the next morning. That can affect your concentration, how much work you complete, and how patient you are with the people around you.

Your Foundational Causes List identifies decisions and repeated behaviours with those larger consequences.

This matters because several things you want to improve may depend partly on the same cause. Understanding that connection helps you recognise how much a particular habit deserves your attention.

Your Self Mastery List might contain the standard of closing the computer and leaving your phone outside the bedroom. Your Foundational Causes List explains why that recurring decision affects so much of what happens afterward.

These are the causes you need to keep creating if you want the life you have described.

##### How The Loop Works

Your daily life gives you the material for these lists.

You encounter a situation and notice how you respond. You might follow through, lose control, repeat an unhelpful thought, or discover something that needs attention. That experience gives you something specific to consider during your reviews and your 2.0 You Hour.

You then work out what needs to change. It may be the behaviour you expect from yourself, the meaning you attach to it, or the thought you want to use when the situation appears again. Sometimes there is a practical problem to resolve or an improvement to put into one of your plans.

That thinking affects how you approach the next occurrence. What happens next gives you more information.

Your Self Mastery List, Meaning List, Foundational Causes List, and Thought Habit List work together within that process. They can all concern the same moment while helping you understand different parts of it.

Take the decision to stop working and go to bed. Your Foundational Causes List helps you recognise the consequences for tomorrow. Your Meaning List connects that decision to the work and relationships you care about. Your Thought Habit List gives you a way to remember those consequences in the moment, and your Self Mastery List makes the behaviour you expect from yourself clear.

Returning to those four lists several times per day gives you repeated opportunities to remember what you have already decided. Your experience then shows you where those decisions need more thought or practice.

The other lists are available when you need them. Joy and Gratitude can help you change what you are paying attention to. Forgiveness gives you somewhere to work through what you are still holding against yourself or someone else. Improvements can become decisions or planned actions, while practical worries get handled during the Anti-Worry Hour.

You do not need to work through every list every day. What matters is that something you notice in your life can lead to a considered change, and that the change gets used when you return to your day. If the same problem keeps happening, you bring that experience back and work on it again.
