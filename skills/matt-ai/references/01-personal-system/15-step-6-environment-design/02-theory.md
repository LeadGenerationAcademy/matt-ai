# Theory

Course: Step #6: Environment Design
System: Personal System
Skool link: https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/49a1b368?md=504a098ddbc349008651d7def83b1c51

---
Your environment is very important.

This is fairly obvious.

If your phone is sitting beside you, checking it takes almost no effort.

If your phone is in another room, you have to stop working, stand up, leave the room, find it, and bring it back.

You can obviously still do that.

The difference is that you are much less likely to do it without thinking.

The same thing applies to everything else in your environment, both good and bad.

If YouTube is already open, it is easier to watch YouTube.

If people are talking around you, it is harder to concentrate.

If you have three monitors with random things open on all of them, there are more things available to distract you.

If the only interesting thing in front of you is the work, it becomes easier to keep working.

None of these things force you to behave a certain way.

They just change how easy different behaviors are.

That matters a lot.

You are not going to make one decision each morning to focus and then remain perfectly focused for the rest of the day.

You are going to have hundreds of small opportunities to get distracted.

You will think about checking your phone.

You will want to open another tab.

You will get bored.

You will hear something outside the room.

You will randomly remember something you want to look up.

A bad environment makes it incredibly easy to act on all of these impulses.

A good environment puts a little bit of friction between the impulse and the action.

That friction is often enough.

This is why I do not think Environment Design replaces discipline or Self-Mastery.

You still need to become a person who can focus for long periods of time.

You still need to work when you do not feel like working.

You still need to be able to resist distractions when they appear.

But there is no reason to make this harder than it needs to be.

There is no prize for putting your phone beside you and successfully resisting it 40 times.

Just put the phone somewhere else.

There is no prize for working in a loud room and forcing yourself to concentrate through it.

Make the room quiet.

Environment Design is simply another tool in our arsenal.

We have motivation.

We have discipline.

We have Personal Transformation.

We have systems.

We have Environment Design.

We should use all of them.

Our ability to do Deep Work is largely going to determine our success or failure in the business world, so we should make doing Deep Work as easy as we reasonably can.

That is what we are going to do here.

We are basically going to engineer our Work Room so that doing the work becomes the most obvious thing available.

We will face our desk toward a wall.

We will remove unnecessary distractions.

We will keep our phone outside the room.

We will keep the Workspace simple.

And over time, we are going to build the wall in front of us into part of the System.

That wall will contain reminders of our goals and reminders of the work we have already completed.

The previous work matters because it gives us evidence.

Evidence that we work hard.

Evidence that we can focus.

Evidence that we follow through.

Evidence that we are becoming the type of person who does a lot of difficult work.

The goals matter because they remind us what all of this work is for.

So every time we sit down, we are surrounded by reminders of two things:

What we have already done.

And what we are trying to create.

That makes working more obvious and more rewarding.

There is also another effect that starts to happen over time.

If you repeatedly do the same thing in the same place, you start to associate that place with the behavior.

You probably already experience this in other parts of your life.

You get into bed and your brain knows that this is where you sleep.

You walk into a gym and you know that you are there to train.

You sit down at a desk that you have used for hundreds of hours of work and eventually that desk starts to mean something as well.

You work here.

That is the association we are trying to build.

You enter the Work Room.

You sit down.

You see the same Workspace.

You see your goals.

You see the evidence of your previous work.

Your phone is somewhere else.

There is very little around you that is more interesting than the assignment in front of you.

And then you work.

Do that enough times and beginning becomes easier.

This is why I do not want you doing Deep Work from your bed one day, your couch the next day, a coffee shop the day after that, and your actual Workspace whenever you happen to feel like it.

We want to create a place that means one thing.

Work.

You will see what I mean as you incorporate each part of the Setup, but this is a very powerful thing for us to be doing early in the course.

We are trying to make Deep Work as obvious, low-friction, and rewarding as possible while making distraction slightly more difficult.

You still have to do the work.

But we are going to make sure your environment is helping you instead of fighting you.
