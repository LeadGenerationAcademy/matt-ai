### Framework & Criteria

1. **Pre-Call Research:** Five to fifteen minutes reviewing their business and anything they have already told you.
2. **Sales Call Plan:** Introduction, questions, demonstration, objections and close.
3. **Sales Call Presentation:** The offer, proof, price, timing and what happens next.
4. **Follow-Up Flow:** For people who do not buy. Your personal follow-up should address whatever you discussed on the call.
5. **Contract, One-Pager + Payment:** Ready to check, customize and send as soon as someone agrees to buy.
6. **Call Recordings:** Set up so you can review the calls and update your learnings document.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Take every one of these calls yourself.** You need to learn how to sell this offer to cold traffic before handing them to someone else. Even if you have sales reps from other lead sources, this is not necessarily the same thing. Eventually you will have closers for your Facebook ads, but without this knowledge and experience, you will not be able to build a sales process you can plug people into.
2. **Spend five to fifteen minutes on pre-call research before each call.** Look through their website, their relevant profiles, their application and anything they have already told you. It looks SO unprofessional when someone has explained their situation in an application or over text, and you clearly have not read it.
3. **Know what they sell, who they serve and why they booked.** This is so you can ask for clarification on the call without making them start over. The bar is very low here. Even a few minutes of preparation can make it obvious that you care enough to understand their business before trying to sell them something.
4. **Write down two or three observations to investigate on the call.** Otherwise, you are going to waste a lot of time on your sales call asking them.
5. **Pull up relevant proof before the call.** A case study from someone in a similar situation will be much more useful than whatever screenshot you happen to find during the call. For example, if a financial advisor books a call, bring case studies from other financial advisors.
6. **Open your sales call plan.** It should already cover your introduction, questions, demonstration, objections and close. It helps you understand the person's situation, explain the offer in a sensible order and finish with either a sale or a clear next step.
7. **Have your sales call presentation ready.** It should cover the offer, proof, price, timing and what happens next. These are the things someone needs to evaluate before buying.
8. **Prepare your contract, one-pager and payment link before the call.** They need to be ready to check, customize and send as soon as someone agrees to buy. Send all three together in one clearly labeled message through email and text.
9. **Record every call so you can update [Your One-Page Learning Document](https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/988cd10a?md=9ffba3c4a6304105b3c98f740e61ff49).** After each sales call, review the recording and update what you are learning.
10. **Book another meeting while you are still on the call if you need one.** Some people will need more time or have something they still need answered before making a decision.
11. **Confirm your follow-up flow is running for people who do not buy.** It keeps giving them case studies, answers to common questions and industry insights over the following weeks. Some people will need more time before making a decision.
12. **Send your personal follow-up after the call.** It should address whatever you discussed on the call. The flow keeps useful material in front of them over the following weeks, while your personal follow-up addresses whatever you specifically discussed on the call.
