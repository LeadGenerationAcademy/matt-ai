---
This course is about getting your first 10 clients from paid ads.

10 is very important. One or two clients could be a fluke. But if you can get 10, you are really on to something.

There are four stages of scaling Facebook ads:

- Your first 10 clients (beginner)
- Early stage scaling (intermediate)
- Transitory scaling (advanced)
- Mature scaling (advanced)

This course is all about stage #1. I would guess about 99.9% of businesses are in this stage right now.

### Facebook Ads Are One Part Of The Process

This is the most important thing you need to understand, and almost nobody ever tells you.

In lead generation, the actual ads are only one piece of the process. There are four phases if you want to get a client:

1. **Clicks:** Usually through paid ads and content.
2. **Calls:** They go to your sales page with your VSL and book a call.
3. **Shows:** Your pre-call process gets them to actually show up to the appointment they booked.
4. **Closes:** Your sales call, your close and your onboarding.

The Facebook ad takes care of the click. It gets someone into the process. They still need to book, show up and buy.

If any phase falls apart, you do not get the client.

These are all "Facebook ads." When someone says "my Facebook ads don't work," they could mean several completely different things. Is the creative bad? Is the landing page bad? Is the application form too strict? Are people booking but not showing? Are people showing but not buying? Do you have enough calendar availability? Are you botching the sales call?

You need to specify.

People get so scared of uploading ads and clicking the right buttons that they put this off for years. Meanwhile, the thing determining their results might be somewhere else entirely. Look at the whole process. Find the phase where it falls apart.

### Cold Traffic Is Much Different Than Referrals

When we use Facebook ads to reach strangers, we are dealing with cold traffic. They do not know, like or trust us. That is significantly harder to close than referrals, your network or leads from content.

Someone who has watched your YouTube videos for 10 hours may already know, like and trust you. Sometimes they barely need an offer. They just want to buy from you.

Whatever you currently sell through referrals and relationships might not be the best thing to put in an ad. We want to advertise the thing most likely to turn a stranger into a customer.

We will set ourselves up as well as possible. But do not expect cold traffic to be anywhere near as easy.

### You Need The Right People To Click

A lot of people obsess over their cost per click. A $1 cost per click of the wrong people is worse than a $15 cost per click of the right person.

More than almost anything you can change inside the ad account, your messaging will determine who you attract.

An agency owner trying to get their first client might care about cold emails getting no replies or having no proof. An established agency owner might want to get out of sales, add a second acquisition channel or scale ads without their acquisition cost exploding.

Same industry. Different level. Different messaging.

If you talk about beginner problems, you are going to attract beginners. Then people complain that Facebook keeps sending them shitty leads.

### Sell Something People MUST Buy

This makes everything a lot easier.

Selling something that needs an in-depth explanation, or something new they have never heard of, is always going to be much harder in ads, even if it would really solve their problem.

When someone already understands what you sell and knows they need it, you can spend much more of your time showing them why they should buy it from you.

There are three versions:

- **Legally must buy:** They are required to have it, like auto insurance or tax help.
- **Functionally must buy:** They need it to function or operate, like mortgage insurance, because they will not get a mortgage without it.
- **Competitively must buy:** They need it to compete at the level they want, like lead generation services.

The closer your offer gets to one of these, the less education and convincing you have to do.

They still need to believe YOU are the person to deliver it. Someone can desperately want the result and have absolutely no confidence that you can get it for them.

### Your Ad, Above The Fold And VSL Are One Unit

We need to treat our ad, above the fold and the first 15 seconds of our VSL as one unit. A lot of people have them completely disconnected.

- **The ad:** Makes the ICP and offer clear and gives them a reason to click.
- **Above the fold:** Immediately confirms the offer they clicked for and makes the next step obvious.
- **The first 15 seconds of the VSL:** Shows proof and explains the offer.

If we have done our job right, the ad and above the fold have already shown them the ICP and offer. We can get straight into the proof in our VSL.

For example, an ad for a lead generation agency says it will generate exclusive and qualified annuities leads and sync them directly to your CRM. The page says the same thing. Then the VSL opens with: "All these financial advisors you see on screen are in different states, have different teams, and have different amounts of AUM. But they all have one thing in common: they bought leads from us."

A lot of people will have an ad that promises annuities leads, and then the headline on the page says "Scale your business." Incongruency just feels weird. It is an icky feeling, usually subconscious, and it makes people less likely to continue. On a practical level, they clicked on your ad because it was interesting to them. So continue that same messaging on the page and in the VSL.

### You Need To Do All Of This Yourself

Expecting someone else to figure this out for you is unrealistic. This is founder-level shit. Maybe CMO-level shit. But most people do not have a CMO.

You can do the media buying yourself. You do not need to hire someone to upload your ads and click the buttons.

YOU are also the one who needs to take these calls. Even if you have sales reps from other lead sources, this is not necessarily the same thing. You are the owner, and you need to know how this works.

Eventually, you can have closers. But without this knowledge and experience, you will not be able to build a sales process you can plug people into.

### How Facebook Decides Who Sees Your Ads

You need to understand this before you start looking at your results and deciding what to change.

**Facebook does know what it's doing.** Meta is worth over $1.5 trillion, and advertising is a huge part of that business. You are not equal to Facebook. That does not mean it is never wrong. But for most people, assuming Facebook knows more than you is a much better starting point.

**It is interruption marketing.** Someone is scrolling through Facebook or Instagram, and your ad interrupts them. They were not looking for you, unlike Google Ads where someone is actively searching. They can scroll past your entire pitch with one movement of their thumb. So the ad needs to make sense immediately. Who is it for? What can they get? Why should they care?

**It is a prediction machine.** Facebook's job is to predict who is most likely to see your ad and take the action you want. You indicate that action through your conversion event: Purchase, Submit Application, Schedule and so on.

**It is an auction.** You are bidding against other advertisers trying to reach the same people. But the highest bidder does not automatically win. Facebook considers your bid, how likely it thinks the person is to take the action you want, and the quality of your ad.

**It learns from your conversions.** As conversions come in, Facebook gets more information about who is responding. But a booked-call event tells Facebook someone booked. It does not automatically tell Facebook whether that person can buy. Imagine you spend $1,000 and book ten calls. Six are unqualified. Four are qualified. You might think, "That is still $250 per qualified call." We want qualified people producing the conversions we are optimizing for.

**Results are a power law.** You will often find that a small number of your ads receive almost all the budget, while most receive very little. Do not assume something is broken. This is why we create multiple versions. We are giving ourselves more opportunities to find something that works.

**Your creative does the targeting.** This is a triple-whammy:

- Facebook uses what your ad is about to help determine who to show it to.
- The people who relate to it are the ones who click, apply and book.
- That conversion data feeds back into the system and helps it find more people likely to take the same action.

If your messaging appeals to the wrong customer, that loop keeps bringing you more of them. I generally recommend broad targeting. Give Facebook room to find people, and put your effort into making the creative relevant to the customer you actually want.

**Performance gets worse as you scale.** Think of an ad working at $100 per day. Increasing that to $1,000 means finding substantially more opportunities to spend. The additional people you reach may need more explanation, stronger proof or a better offer before they buy.

**Ad fatigue is usually not the problem.** When someone's ads stop working, this is often the first thing they blame. A lot of the time, people confuse fatigue with their ads simply not being good enough. When something stops working, first look at whether it is good enough.

### Why The Ad Itself Is Only A Small Part Of It

If I had to put a number on it, I would say your Core Seven make up 80% of your performance, and your ads and funnel make up 20%. Those are your industry, ICP, offer, unit economics, Big Idea, messaging and proof.

There are four steps: clicks, calls, shows and closes. If we divide that remaining 20% equally between them:

20% × 25% = 5%

These are illustrative numbers. But you could make a real argument that the actual ad represents a very small part of your total success.

People are dumbfounded that their ads do not work while putting almost all their attention on the advertisement itself. Meanwhile, they are selling the wrong thing to the wrong person, have no proof, or lose everyone between the click and the sale.

At this point, we can leave most of the Core Seven alone. We need to get our ads to work first and foremost. Getting our first 10 clients will smash our previously held self-limiting beliefs. Once our ads work and we want to go further, this stuff becomes extremely important.

### Get Facebook And Your Profiles Ready First

Before we run ads, we need Facebook to know when someone books a call. We also need our profiles to look professional.

When someone books a call, they are redirected to your thank you page, and the pixel fires. That is how Facebook knows a conversion happened. Use Schedule as the conversion event, on the thank you page URL.

Your ads are going to deliver from Instagram and Facebook, so those profiles need to look professional, hopefully with content on them.

A lot of people will see your ad, back out, open Google and search your name or company. This happens way more than you think. What they find can affect whether they book, show up or buy. Bad results are obviously a problem, but nothing showing up is not great either. Make sure all of your profiles are professional, and make sure they can continue the funnel through each.

Clone your Calendly event and give it a separate link, used only in this funnel, so you can identify the calls booked through it. It is not perfect. Someone could see your ad, Google you and book through another link. But this gives us a simple way to track bookings without some complicated attribution system at this phase.

You also need a Slack notification and an SMS to your phone whenever someone books a call. You need to know immediately so you can reach out, ideally within five minutes.

### Keep The Ads Simple

At this phase, we will probably have a lot of self-limiting beliefs and lack the skills to make really high-quality video ads. That is fine, because we are going to keep it simple. We will focus on direct-offer static image ads. These will be more than enough to get us our first 10 clients.

Perception = reality. You know everything that makes your offer good. Someone seeing your ad or landing page for the first time does not. They cannot read your mind.

If you get results quickly, do all the work for them, include something valuable or have great proof, SAY IT. None of that helps you sell if people don't know it exists. Do not assume something is obvious just because it is obvious to you.

We write five versions of the copy. It is essentially the same offer, said five different ways. For example, "Get Annuity Leads That Actually Answer The Phone" and "Stop Competing With Other Advisors For The Same Annuity Leads" are the exact same offer to the same ICP. Some might appeal more than others. We do not know which one is going to resonate, because we have limited or no history of what works. So we throw some stuff out there and see what sticks.

Then we make at least three designs. Each design should:

- Make the ICP and offer instantly clear.
- Use high contrast and readable text, so it stands out when they are scrolling Instagram.
- Address roughly three important objections.
- Include a clear CTA.

Five headlines × three designs = 15 ads. Within each design, keep everything else the same and only change the headline.

Then look at the finished ads alongside your above the fold and VSL. Make sure the page delivers the same offer the ad promises.

### Media Buying Is About Avoiding Mistakes

For some reason, people get really bent out of shape about media buying. In the past few years, it has mattered less and less. It is much more about avoiding mistakes than doing anything impressive, especially when we are trying to get our first 10 clients.

Do not give $3,000 or $5,000 a month away to an agency. They are not doing anything more than what this course shows you. Figuring out your ads is much more about figuring out your offer, your Core Seven, and building a good funnel with good enough ads alongside it.

We will use broad targeting. The only things we may change are the location, age and gender, if relevant.

Your conversion event in the ad set has to match the one you set up on your thank you page. If you set up Schedule on the page and choose Submit Application in the ad set, it will not track.

### Remove The Application For Now

Normally, we use the application to stop unqualified people from booking. This phase is the one exception.

When you have never done this before, getting experience quickly is extremely valuable. Even unqualified calls can teach you what people think you sell, what they want and what questions they have. You will adjust to selling to cold traffic and learn what needs to change in your offer or sales process.

A lot of the time, we will remove the application for even the first 10 calls, let alone the first 10 clients. You do not have to do this, but it is recommended.

We can always add the application back after our first 10 clients. For now, we want as little friction as possible.

### Track What Happens After You Spend Money

We need to track what happens after we spend money. Otherwise, when something does not work, we will have no idea where the problem is.

Track your ad spend, outbound clicks, calls booked, calls attended, qualified calls attended, clients acquired and cash collected. From those, we can calculate cost per click, cost per booked call, show rate, close rate and cost per client.

Keep qualified calls attended separate from total calls attended. Ten calls with people who can buy is very different from ten calls with people who cannot.

These are the numbers you will use to make your decisions.

### Keep One Page Of Everything You Learn

You should have one document that stays on one page. After each sales call, review the recording and update what you are learning:

- Who is booking and who is buying?
- What do they actually want?
- What do they understand or misunderstand about the offer?
- What questions and objections keep coming up?
- Why did they buy or not buy?
- What needs to change in the ads, funnel or sales process?

Use their actual language.

If people keep misunderstanding something, change how you explain it. If the same objection keeps coming up, answer it in your VSL, pre-call material or sales presentation.

Keep updating the same page. The point is to learn as fast as possible.

### Your Funnel Matters More Than Your Ads

The unfortunate part of a beginner Facebook ads course is how little Facebook ads, in terms of creative and media buying, have to do with our success.

Our Core Seven are more important, and our funnel is still more important than our ads. Facebook ads are just the first phase. We still have calls, shows and closes. Without a minimum viable funnel, your ads cannot hope to succeed.

Before launching, go through the entire thing on your phone and make a test booking.

**Your above the fold is your first impression for your entire business.** It is the section they can see on mobile when the page loads, without scrolling. They have never heard of you before. They clicked on your ad, and this is the first thing they ever see. If you look at heat maps, upwards of 70% of people do not scroll down at all. If your information is tucked away, they will never see it.

**Your VSL gives them a reason to believe you can help.** It should quickly show proof, explain what you do, and then answer the questions most likely to affect their decision. Someone coming from a paid ad may have never heard of you before.

**The rest of your sales page has to work without the video.** Someone should be able to understand your offer and why it is valuable without watching the VSL for one second. Some people want to watch the video, some want to read in depth, some want to skim. If all the information is only in the VSL, the people who don't watch VSLs are not going to know how it works, and they are not going to book a call.

**Your calendar can lose people too.** Once someone has decided to book, we need to make it easy for them to find a suitable time while they are still interested. That means as many available slots as possible, appointments as soon as possible, and a booking window of no more than 72 hours. If it is 8:11 p.m., can someone book a call at 8:15 p.m.? That is what I mean by as soon as possible. A calendar with very little availability creates another place for interested people to drop out.

### Getting Them To Show Up

Booking does not mean they are going to show up. If it did, the metric of show rate would not exist.

**Your thank you page tells them what to do before the meeting.** It confirms the booking and tells them to accept the calendar invitation, reply to your text and watch the material that answers their biggest questions. It helps them confirm the appointment and gives them something useful to do while their interest is still high.

**Your reminders make sure they can find the call.** Calendly's built-in email and SMS reminders go out 24 hours, four hours and one hour before the call. Someone can be interested in your offer and still miss the call because they forgot or could not find the link. There is very little to figure out here. Just turn them on.

**Your pre-call flow keeps them learning while they wait.** Each email answers an important question and gives them more reason to attend. Put the most important information first, because someone who books a call for later today will see fewer emails than someone who books three days ahead.

**Your selfie video puts a face to the name.** It is a short video, 60 seconds or less, from the person they are going to speak with. This matters when they have come through a cold ad and barely know who you are. They get to see and hear the actual person behind the meeting, which makes the call feel more familiar before it happens.

**Your appointment-setting message gives them an easy way to reply.** Say your name, your company and the call they booked. Put 100% of the information in the text, with no multi-step and no hiding your intentions. Sometimes someone is ready to talk now, and there is no reason to leave them waiting for an appointment several days away.

**Your Loom and one-pager explain the offer before the call.** Send both with your personal reach-out, so they arrive at the call understanding what you offer and why they might want your help.

### You Take These Calls

YOU should be taking these calls. You need to learn how to sell this offer to cold traffic before handing them to someone else.

**Spend five to fifteen minutes on pre-call research.** Look through their website, relevant profiles and anything they have already told you. You should know what they sell, who they serve and what they have said about why they booked.

It looks SO unprofessional when someone has explained their situation in an application or over text, and you clearly have not read it. You are going to waste a lot of time on your sales call asking them.

Write down two or three observations to investigate and have relevant proof ready. If a financial advisor books a call, bring financial advisor case studies. A case study from someone in a similar situation will be much more useful than whatever screenshot you happen to find during the call.

The bar is very low here. Even a few minutes of preparation can make it obvious that you care enough to understand their business before trying to sell them something.

**Follow a sales call plan.** It is the structure you follow from the introduction and questions through the demonstration, objections and close. It is not necessarily a strict script, but it is a repeatable process. You may not think you need this as the founder, because you have all the authority and know everything. Develop it anyway. Eventually you will need it, because you will get nowhere if you let your closers do whatever they want.

**Have a sales call presentation.** It explains what you are trying to accomplish for the prospect and how you propose to do it: why your approach makes sense, what it costs, how long it takes and what the risks are.

**Have a follow-up flow.** Some people will need more time or have something they still need answered before making a decision. The flow keeps useful material in front of them over the following weeks, while your personal follow-up addresses whatever you specifically discussed on the call. If you need another meeting, book it while you are still on the call.

**Have your one-pager, contract and payment link ready before the call.** Then you can customize, check and send them immediately after someone agrees to buy.

### Going Live

Once your ads are built and your funnel is ready, upload the 15 static image ads and schedule the campaign.

Check that every ad goes to the correct sales page. Check the audience, locations and placements, with age capped at 59 and Instagram and Facebook only. Make sure Schedule is selected as the conversion event. Check the start date, time and ad account time zone. Make sure the ads are approved and actually start running.

Start at $100 per day, or $200.

### Make Sure You Did Not Mess Up The Setup

Right away, 24 to 72 hours after launching, make sure your ads are not accidentally sending to the wrong stuff.

Check gender, age, platform and location, if relevant. You should never see 59+ for age. You should only see Facebook and Instagram for platform. If you see Messenger or Audience Network, that is no good.

### Every Call Makes The Next One Better

Review the recording after each call. This is an ongoing process. Every call you take, you want the next one to be better.

Pay attention to what the person wanted, what they understood or misunderstood about the offer, the questions they asked, the objections they raised, and the reasons they gave for buying or not buying. Use their actual language.

Then update the relevant part of your funnel or sales process. If the same misunderstanding keeps coming up, improve how you explain the offer. If people repeatedly raise the same objection, address it in your VSL, pre-call material or sales presentation.

Use the recordings to improve what the next person sees and hears.

### When To Spend More

Once you have traction, I recommend increasing your spend to at least a few hundred dollars per day.

You can do this after your first close. As soon as you get your first close, this is working at least to some degree.

Or, if you feel confident, after your first five qualified shown calls, even if they have not closed yet. Maybe there is a sales cycle and you want to speed things up. Those five calls need to be qualified people who actually attended. Five bookings do not mean the same thing.

I do not think it is necessary to increase the budget before this. If it is not going to work at $100 or $200 per day, it is not going to work at $500 or $1,000 per day. It only gets worse.

### The Only Number That Really Matters

The golden rule: If CPA is acceptable, nothing else matters. Every other metric could be absolute shit.

However, if your CPA is not acceptable, we use these metrics to troubleshoot. This golden rule remains true throughout every phase of scaling.

These benchmarks are the barebones ones for this phase. We do not need to overcomplicate it.

**Clicks.** Look in your ad account. You want a $25 to $70 CPM. In an exceptionally crowded niche, something like $125 might be acceptable. But if it is $250 or $300, something is probably wrong. Just like if it was a $4 CPM. Your outbound CTR should be 1 to 2%+. Anything under 1% a lot of the time means your ads are not resonating enough. Anything over 2% is usually a function of your CPM being very high.

**Calls.** Compare these to your outbound clicks. You want a 6 to 10% application rate, once you put the application back in, and a 3 to 5%+ booked call rate. Your booked call rate is really a function of your application rate. A lot of the time, it will be 50 to 70% of it. You also want an 80 to 90% qualified rate. It will never be perfect, but anything less at this phase is probably not good.

**Shows.** Compare shown calls to calls booked. 50 to 70%+ is what you want. 100% would be phenomenal, but 70% is very realistic. A lot of B2C and mass market offers will skew lower.

**Closes.** Compare closed calls to calls booked. You want 15 to 30%. When you have a sales team, you usually base close rate on live calls to judge their sales ability. But since you are the one taking all these calls, and you want your show rate up anyway, we compare to calls booked.

### If This, Then That

- **CPM over $100:** Completely redo or rethink your ads. Unless you are in a very, very competitive niche, your ads probably have a terrible quality ranking.
- **Outbound CTR under 1%:** Completely redo or rethink your ads.
- **Cost per outbound click over $10:** Not a dealbreaker if you are getting calls booked, but unlikely to work. If it is $20 and you are not getting any calls booked, it really is unlikely to work.
- **Application rate under 6%:** Rework the above the fold and VSL. Make the offer clearer, show relevant proof and make sure the page matches the ad.
- **Application rate over 10%:** If you are still using the application, tighten it and add friction.
- **Close rate under 15%:** Check show rate first, then review pre-call framing, the sales calls and follow-up. Use the recordings to identify what needs work.
- **Close rate over 30%:** Increase your price. You never really want your close rate to be more than 30%.
- **Unqualified calls over 50%:** Realistically, even over 20%. Make the ICP and offer more specific in your ads and on the page. Tighten the application if needed.
- **Show rate under 50%:** Improve pre-call flow, reminders and speed to lead.

If every single metric is poor and you think you did a good job, it is probably your offer. If you have spent a few thousand dollars, generated clicks and calls, and REALLY tried to make this work, it may be worth revisiting the offer.

We are talking about something that remains a complete battle after you have worked on the execution, beyond the normal learning curve and growing pains.

This is subjective. Most people give up too soon and blame the offer when the problem is their execution. But the reality is that some offers just are not good, and we may need to try another one.

### Keep Going Until You Have 10 Clients

You will probably be bad at this at first. You were terrible at everything you are now good at at some point. You learned. This is no different.

At $200 per day, 30 days is $6,000 in ad spend. You need to be comfortable with the possibility of spending that without getting a client.

Stay in this phase until you have 10 clients from paid ads.

I have no idea how long that will take you. It could be one week on the fast end, one month or two months. It depends on you, your skill and your budget.

Keep taking the calls, learning from the recordings, making more ads and improving your funnel and sales process. That is really all it takes to get your first 10 clients from Meta ads.
