### Framework & Criteria

1. **Specific ICP:** The specific ICP the offer is for.
2. **Result & Deliverables:** The result and deliverables they get.
3. **Scope:** What is included and what is outside the scope.
4. **Delivery Timeframe:** How long it takes to deliver.
5. **Customer Requirements:** What the customer needs to provide or do.
6. **Price & Payment Terms:** The price and how they pay it.
7. **Acquisition Cost For Planning:** Never assume less than $2,000 to acquire a customer.
8. **Required Upfront Collection:** Acquisition cost plus fulfillment cost, multiplied by 2, collected upfront after fees.

### Example

**Offer Pricing**

- $2,000 acquisition + $1,200 fulfillment = $3,200
- $3,200 × 2 = $6,400 required upfront collection
- Aim for $6,500 or more collected upfront, after fees

**Collecting Three Months Upfront**

- Recurring service at $2,500 per month, with the first three months sold together
- $2,500 × 3 months = $7,500 collected upfront
- 80% gross margin target, so fulfillment can cost 20% of that revenue: $7,500 × 20% = $1,500 to fulfill all three months
- $1,500 fulfillment + $2,000 acquisition = $3,500 total cost
- $7,500 collected ÷ $3,500 total cost = 2.14x before fees

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Write down the specific ICP your offer is for.** We need enough similarity between customers that the work, processes and learning from one customer are useful for the next. If the team has to start from scratch every time, getting better results and reducing fulfillment costs becomes much harder.
2. **Write down the result and deliverables, and what is outside the scope.** We already covered what makes an offer attractive to the customer. Now we need to write down the actual offer we are going to sell repeatedly. We cannot have every new customer creating a completely different project for the team to figure out.
3. **Write down the delivery timeframe.** A standardized offer lets us keep improving the same workflow through technology, training, AI, automation, assets, templates and SOPs, and those improvements become useful across more customers.
4. **Write down what the customer needs to provide or do.** We will build the fulfillment system later. Right now, we need to settle on an offer we can actually build it around.
5. **Set your price and payment terms to collect at least 2x your combined acquisition and fulfillment costs upfront, after fees.** Add up your acquisition and fulfillment costs, then multiply that total by 2. For planning, never assume less than $2,000 to acquire a customer. With $2,000 acquisition and $1,200 fulfillment, you need $6,400, so aim for $6,500 or more collected upfront. We will refine this using your actual costs in Step #9: Unit Economics Model.
6. **Collect several months upfront if a single upfront price doesn't make sense for your offer.** Not everyone has an offer that makes sense to charge $6,500 upfront for. Selling the first three months of a $2,500 per month service together collects $7,500 upfront and gets you above 2x, while keeping the monthly price the same.
7. **Make sure sales and fulfillment are working from the same offer, and train your sales team to sell it.** If we keep changing what is included to get each person to buy, we haven't actually standardized anything. Our offer and funnel should do as much of the selling as possible, and all our sales training and practice can go toward getting good at selling one thing. We can add another trained salesperson without having to rebuild everything around them.

### Completion Audit

- Did you write down the specific ICP your offer is for?
- Did you write down the result and deliverables, and what is outside the scope?
- Did you write down the delivery timeframe?
- Did you write down what the customer needs to provide or do?
- Did you set your price and payment terms to collect at least 2x your combined acquisition and fulfillment costs upfront, after fees?
- If a single upfront price didn't make sense for your offer, did you collect several months upfront?
- Are sales and fulfillment working from the same offer, and are you training your sales team to sell it?
