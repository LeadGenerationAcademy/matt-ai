### Framework & Criteria

**Workflow Breakdown**

1. **What Needs To Happen:** The action itself.
2. **Information Needed:** What information is needed to do it.
3. **Who Does It:** The person responsible for the step.
4. **Time:** How long it takes.
5. **Completed Work:** What the completed work looks like.

**Training System**

1. **Document:** You create the checklist.
2. **Delegate:** You get on a call with them and do the task in front of them, using only the checklist.
3. **Duplicate:** They do the task in front of you, using only the checklist. Once they can complete it properly that way, they are trained on that task.

**Targets**

1. **Time To First Value:** Within 24 hours of the customer paying us.
2. **Micro Wins:** 1% improvements over and over, working toward cutting fulfillment costs in half.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Document how you deliver the offer, step by step.** Break the work down far enough that someone can follow the process. We need to document how we fulfill the offer now, even if parts of it are still manual or depend on you. Once booking calls becomes as easy as tying our shoes, fulfillment and our ability to maintain results will become huge constraints. We can hire more people, but they need to know exactly what to do. If everyone has to figure things out from scratch, hiring more people becomes expensive very quickly.
2. **List every single micro step from payment to the customer getting the result.** There should probably be 50–100+ steps. Do not combine steps here. If something involves five separate actions, write out all five. Include collecting information, chasing approvals, fixing mistakes and all the little things people forget they spend time doing.
3. **Write down the five workflow details for each step.** This is what needs to happen, what information is needed, who does it, how long it takes and what the completed work looks like. We need this level of detail because we are going to improve the individual steps. If everything is grouped together, we cannot properly see where the work is going.
4. **Look for individual tasks where AI, automation or a reusable template can save time.** This is why the offer needs to be standardized, so you can put your training, technology and processes behind delivering the same thing repeatedly. Go through every individual step and look at how software, AI or automation could make it quicker, cheaper or remove the need to do it manually. We are looking for 1% micro wins over and over here, working toward cutting our fulfillment costs in half. Make sure the improvement actually saves work. If a tool saves twenty minutes but creates thirty minutes of checking and corrections, we have made it worse.
5. **Templatize as many individual steps as possible.** If we keep creating the same document, build a template. If we keep explaining the same thing, create an asset we can reuse. If we keep making the same decision, give the team a framework for making it. Make it clear which parts they need to change for the client and which parts should stay the same. The mindset here is to get as close to "selling air" as possible, with high revenue and very low fulfillment costs. If we manually recreate everything for every client, we have to keep paying for the same work over and over. If we build something once and reuse it, that work can benefit all of our clients.
6. **Keep your templates, assets and frameworks together.** Everyone should be working from the same versions. We do not want people recreating things or copying some old document because they cannot find the right one.
7. **Turn each task into an SOP checklist.** This lets someone get the task done without needing any outside information. Every step needs to be there, along with the instructions, links, templates and examples required to complete it. If they need to ask you something halfway through, that information needs to go into the checklist. We should be able to hand this to someone who has been trained, have them follow each step and get the work completed properly. We will test whether the checklist is actually good enough during training.
8. **Do the task in front of the person you are training, using only the checklist.** Training and SOPs are two different things. The SOP gives them the steps. The training is where we make sure they can follow those steps and get the result. People will hire someone, hand them some instructions and barely spend a minute showing them how to do anything. We need to show them how to do it, then watch them do it and correct the mistakes.
9. **Have them do the task in front of you, using only the checklist.** Once they can complete it properly that way, they are trained on that task.
10. **Add anything missing from the checklist and go through it again.** If either of you needs information that is not on the checklist, it is not good enough yet. This gives us a training process we can repeat with each person we hire, and we can see whether the problem is that the instructions are incomplete or the person needs more practice.
11. **Decide what the customer's first value is.** We need time to first value to be within 24 hours. The customer should get something meaningful within the first day of paying us.
12. **Find where the work sits waiting.** Look at how long the work takes and how long it sits waiting. A task might take thirty minutes but sit untouched for three days because someone is waiting for information or approval. Collect what we need earlier, remove unnecessary handoffs and move the work that creates that first value earlier in the process.
13. **List everything you need from the customer.** Go through the workflow and identify the information, meetings, approvals, access and anything their team has to do.
14. **Cut how much work the customer has to do.** Look at what we can do for them, what we can collect once and what we can eliminate. Some involvement will be necessary. Make it easy by giving them clear instructions and asking for things together where possible. We want them to get the result with as little work and disruption to their business as possible.
15. **Update the SOPs as you learn better ways to do it.** Over time you should get better, faster and less expensive at fulfilling it. When we find a better way to do something, we need to update the process so everyone starts doing it that way. We want to be able to clone what we are doing, change the client details and get the same outcome for each client. Our fulfillment team should be getting really good at doing the same thing over and over, without coming up with an entirely new strategy every time we sign someone. When we find a winning ad, landing page, VSL, email or sales tactic, we want to be able to apply that same thing across our clients. Then, every improvement we make becomes useful across the business.
