### Framework & Criteria

Winning ad → New variations → New ad sets → New winners → Repeat

### Example

"Works" means acceptable acquisition cost with enough data to judge. "Fails" means acquisition cost is too high after enough data to judge. These results are hypothetical.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Keep the message and offer that worked.** When you find a winning ad, you use it as the starting point for the next batch of creatives. Before rewriting it, identify what you want to carry over. Otherwise, you can change the ad so much that you remove the thing you were trying to build on.
2. **Turn the winner into different concepts from [Option A #4: Different Ad Concepts](https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/031026d9?md=cce18101ad4a48389f870820f3dd0b24).** Take the message that worked and explain it in different ways: negative, positive, how-to, case study and step by step. Keep the underlying message and offer consistent.
3. **Turn the winner into different ad types using the [Format Copywriting Bot Template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1nqmZGoj9L2_EvhT8U54mRO6i6sVTtxvR4dnuqUfatAM/edit?tab=t.ob9ny0mk92xm) you made in [Step #12G: Copywriting Bot For Each Format](ADD LINK).** Put the same message into the ad types we already built production systems for. A winning talking-head ad could become a static image, looping B-roll, a Loom screenshare or another format that makes sense. Give the bot the actual ad to work from. We can combine these methods. The case-study version could be delivered as a talking-head video, a screenshare or slides. [in this skill: assets/templates/format-copywriting-bot-template/]
4. **Test those variations using [Step #13E: The Next Batch Goes Into New Ad Sets](https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/031026d9?md=39910121a2f14b9096dabd4b749cf255).** The original ad worked. Now we need to find out which variations also work. Judge them against our target CPA, with enough results to make a decision. Every variation does not need to succeed. We are looking for repeated success that gives us more confidence in the angle.
5. **Be careful about what you conclude from each variation.** If you change the avatar, framing and format together, you cannot tell which change caused the difference.
6. **Look at what keeps working across the variations.** You might notice the same problem, wording, proof or presenter showing up across multiple winners. Grouping your tests by a variable makes these patterns easier to see. Part of getting good at this is knowing the difference between a pattern and something that happened once. Eventually, you will get better at spotting potential winners before you run them, but we still need to test them.
7. **When another winner emerges, use it to produce the next batch.** Over time you build repeatable production loops around your strongest messages. We want angles that work across multiple formats and a message we can keep making successful ads around. If that message also applies to a large portion of our ICP, it can become one of our workhorse angles and form the basis of our campaigns. Niche angles are still useful and can get us plenty of clients. They just speak to fewer people, so they may have less room to scale.
8. **Continue testing new messaging angles alongside these loops.** This is how you keep finding new starting points. At the start, you will probably try tons of different concepts, angles and formats. More and more of your testing will focus on improving and expanding the winners rather than finding something new from scratch. Realistically, we want at least four workhorse angles and fifteen or more niche angles that work across lots of variations. In the next phase, we will combine vertical and horizontal scaling no matter which one we started with, so keep that in mind while testing now.

### Completion Audit

- Did you keep the message and offer that worked?
- Did you turn the winner into different concepts?
- Did you turn the winner into different ad types using the Format Copywriting Bot Template?
- Did you test those variations?
- Were you careful about what you concluded from each variation?
- Did you look at what keeps working across the variations?
- When another winner emerged, did you use it to produce the next batch?
- Are you continuing to test new messaging angles alongside these loops?
