### Framework & Criteria

**Targets**

1. **Unit Economics Multiple:** Net upfront cash collected ÷ combined acquisition and fulfillment costs = 2X or more.
2. **Gross Margin:** 80%+. Fulfillment costs 20% or less of the revenue from the offer.
3. **Effective Hourly Rate:** $250+ per hour of total team delivery time.

**Fulfillment Costs**

1. **Labor:** Including the time spent checking work and fixing mistakes.
2. **Fulfillment Software:** Allocate a share of shared software to each client.
3. **Managers & Supervision:** Allocate a share to each client, and make sure management labor is not also counted in the labor number.

**Acquisition Costs**

1. **Ad Spend:** What Facebook says you spent to acquire the customer.
2. **Appointment Setter Costs:** What you pay setters per customer.
3. **Closer Costs:** What you pay closers per customer.
4. **Acquisition Software:** Your share of the software used to acquire customers.
5. **Ad Creative Production & Labor:** Whoever makes the ads.
6. **Media Buyer Labor:** Only if applicable.

**Price Increases**

1. **Trigger:** Close rate above 25%, judged across a run of sales calls.
2. **Increase:** 20% every five clients, when demand supports it.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Make a copy of the [Unit Economics Model Spreadsheet Template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1623PcXQn_0-KuF9JmcbMkM4zCulrxcpCoVxrCuyceC0/edit?usp=sharing).** This is where you put your actual offer into the model and work out what you collect upfront, what it costs to acquire each customer and what it costs to fulfill everything you sold them. We already covered the theory. Now we need to build this with the numbers we have now. Some costs may be estimates initially. [in this skill: assets/templates/unit-economics-model-spreadsheet-template/]
2. **Enter the cash you collect upfront and your payment processing fees.** Use the price and payment terms from [Step #3: Offer Standardization](https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/031026d9?md=c0c1679875a04e2eaf22d7a01a477b11). The spreadsheet subtracts the fees to give you your net cash collected, which is what we measure against the 2X target.
3. **Enter the months of delivery covered upfront.** Count the entire delivery commitment. If we collect three months upfront, we need to include three months of fulfillment costs.
4. **Enter your funnel numbers from your [Scaling Metrics Tracking Template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1AS4hNqUVefgaCe1szr12kwuVLJVB9sUwlrg5EUCAkLk/edit?usp=sharing).** This is your cost per click, application rate, call booked rate, show rate, qualified call rate and close rate against live calls. Take cost per click and application rate straight from Ads Manager. The spreadsheet turns these into your ad spend per customer. [in this skill: assets/templates/scaling-metrics-tracking-template/]
5. **Enter your monthly ad spend.** The spreadsheet uses it to work out how many customers you get each month, so shared costs can be divided across them.
6. **Enter your closer and appointment setter costs per customer.** If Facebook says we spent $2,000 to acquire someone, but we also paid a setter, closer and someone to make the ads, our acquisition cost is higher than $2,000. All of that needs to be in the model.
7. **List every salary and tag its department.** Tag fulfillment labor as Fulfillment and ad creative production or media buyer labor as Acquisition. Tag managers and supervision as Management, so management labor is not also counted in the labor number.
8. **List every software cost and tag its department.** For shared software, the spreadsheet allocates a share of the cost to each client.
9. **Enter any other acquisition and fulfillment costs.** Include every cost that does not fit into the lists above. We need the total cost of getting and serving a customer.
10. **List every step in your fulfillment workflow with the time it takes.** Use the workflow breakdown you wrote in [Step #8: Fulfillment Framework](https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/031026d9?md=a8bce73726914145bb25fab87f592275). Include the time spent checking work and fixing mistakes, since that is still fulfillment. Count everyone's time. If three people each spend five hours, that is fifteen hours.
11. **Check your unit economics multiple is 2X or more.** This is chad scaling territory. We collect enough money to acquire and service this client, and then we have enough left to acquire and service the next one.
12. **Check your gross margin is 80% or more.** It gets much harder to achieve our 2X target when fulfillment eats up a large part of what we collect. The fulfillment cost needs to cover the complete offer, including labor, software and management.
13. **Check your effective hourly rate is $250 or more.** As a rule of thumb, I would not price the work below $250 per hour of total team delivery time. This is revenue per delivery hour. We still need to check what those hours and the other fulfillment costs actually cost us. If any of these three checks miss the target, we can see in the model what needs to change for the offer to work.
14. **Raise your price once your close rate goes above 25%.** You only increase when you have demand. Judge that across a run of sales calls, rather than one or two wins. A lot of the time, you can and should be charging much, much more.
15. **Increase the price by 20% every five clients.** Keep checking the close rate and unit economics as you go. Getting half the clients at twice the price gives us the same revenue. If acquisition and fulfillment costs per client stay the same, the total of those costs is cut in half. That can mean substantially more profit with fewer clients to service, and the model will show how much.
16. **Replace your estimates with actual numbers as you get them.** This will change and become more sophisticated over time. As we get more customers and improve fulfillment, we need to keep the model updated.
