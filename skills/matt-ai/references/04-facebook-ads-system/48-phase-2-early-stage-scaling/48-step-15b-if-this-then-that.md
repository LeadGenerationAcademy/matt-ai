### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Open the [If This, Then That Template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1nqmZGoj9L2_EvhT8U54mRO6i6sVTtxvR4dnuqUfatAM/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.698h4rv70kgg).** This is a tab in the same document as your Unit Economics Model Template. These benchmarks help us troubleshoot when acquisition is too expensive. [in this skill: assets/templates/format-copywriting-bot-template/]
2. **Start with your CPA.** Golden Rule: If your CPA is acceptable, the other metrics can look like shit and the campaign can still work.
3. **Find where people are dropping off, then work on that part.** Follow the same progression: Clicks → Applications → Bookings → Shows → Closes. If people click but do not apply, look at the ad, page and application. If qualified people show up but do not buy, look at the sales process.
4. **If your CPM is over $100 and your CPA is unacceptable, rethink the ads.** Is it instantly clear who the ad is for? Is it instantly clear what you are offering? Are you using messaging your ICP actually cares about? Are you giving them a reason to believe you? Rework the creative around those things. The high CPM is a reason to investigate. Whether we can acquire customers at an acceptable cost is still what determines whether the campaign works.
5. **If your outbound CTR is below 1% and acquisition is too expensive, start with the ad.** Work on the hook, the offer, the proof and how quickly someone can understand what they are looking at. For a static image, look at the headline, contrast and visual hierarchy. For a video, look at the opening. They should know who it is for and what you are offering immediately, with proof within the first five seconds. Do not make them sit through a long introduction before you get to the thing they care about.
6. **If your application rate is below 6%, figure out which of three problems is causing it.** The ad appeals to the wrong people and is getting clicks from people who do not fit the ICP or want the offer. The landing page is not good enough, meaning the above the fold, VSL or proof does not give them enough reason to apply, so check that the page matches what the ad promised. Or the form is too strict and makes it unnecessarily difficult for qualified people to get through. Figure out which one it is before changing everything.
7. **If your application rate is above 10%, look at who is applying.** A higher application rate is not automatically better. Are these actually the people you want to sell to? We have to look at application volume and qualification together.
8. **If fewer than 90% of live calls qualify, make the ICP and offer more specific in the ad and on the page.** Then tighten the application where needed.
9. **If your show rate is below 50%, review what happens between booking and the call.** Start with how soon someone can book. Make more calendar spots available sooner and shorten the maximum booking window. Then check speed to lead, the thank you page and selfie video, the pre-call emails and sales assets, email and SMS reminders, and whether the calendar invite and emails are actually arriving. Someone booking a call does not mean they will remember why they booked it three days later. The pre-call process needs to keep doing its job.
10. **If fewer than 15% of live calls close, check qualification first.** If the people are qualified, listen to the recordings. Look at the introduction and questions, the presentation, objection handling and how the closer asks for the sale, and find where the conversation is going wrong. Also check what happens afterward. Is there a clear proposal, contract, payment link and next step? Is follow-up actually happening? If another meeting is needed, book it while you are on the current meeting. We need a specific problem to fix. "The close rate is bad" does not tell the closer what to do differently.
11. **If sales or fulfillment cannot handle more volume, hold or reduce spend while you fix that.** Increasing spend also requires cash and capacity.
