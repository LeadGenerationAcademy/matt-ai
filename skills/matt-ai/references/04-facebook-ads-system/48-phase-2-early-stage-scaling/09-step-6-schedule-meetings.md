### Framework & Criteria

**Fulfillment Meeting**

1. **Target:** 80%+ gross margin while maintaining results.
2. **Fulfillment Cost:** 20% or less of the revenue from that service. If we collect three months upfront, count all three months of fulfillment.
3. **Workflow Questions:** How can we get results faster? How can we make the customer do less? What can we automate or eliminate? How can we make results more consistent?

**Acquisition Meeting**

1. **Targets:** Clicks, booked calls, shows and closes. These depend on your offer and economics.
2. **Maximum CAC:** Net upfront cash collected ÷ 2 − full fulfillment cost.

**Unit Economics Review**

1. **Average Net Upfront Cash Collected:** For customers acquired over the previous seven days.
2. **Average Acquisition Cost:** Broken down into advertising and sales costs.
3. **Full Fulfillment Cost:** For everything they purchased, broken down into labor, software and other delivery costs.

**Metrics Review**

- Gross revenue
- Gross cash collected
- Net cash collected
- Fulfillment costs
- Fulfillment cost per client
- Acquisition costs
- Acquisition cost per client
- Gross margin
- Net margin
- Median client payment
- Monthly churn rate, if applicable
- Close rate
- Cost per booked call

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Schedule a weekly fulfillment meeting, acquisition meeting, unit economics review and metrics review.** The fulfillment meeting is with your head of fulfillment, or the people responsible for doing the work. The acquisition meeting should include your marketing team, which is probably mostly you, and your sales reps. Do the unit economics review with your business partner or executive team if you have one. Otherwise, block time to do it yourself.
2. **In the fulfillment meeting, give the team a target of 80%+ gross margin and go through the workflow.** Fulfillment should cost 20% or less of the revenue from that service while maintaining results. If we collect three months upfront, count all three months of fulfillment. Make this an explicit goal with the team. Every meeting, ask how we can get results faster, how we can make the customer do less, what we can automate or eliminate, and how we can make results more consistent.
3. **In the acquisition meeting, set targets for clicks, booked calls, shows and closes, and calculate your maximum CAC.** These targets depend on your offer and economics. Random benchmarks would be a disservice. Our acquisition cost needs to support the 2x target. Maximum CAC = net upfront cash collected ÷ 2 − full fulfillment cost.
4. **In the acquisition meeting, review where the funnel is falling short.** Decide what needs to improve. Keep having this meeting even when acquisition costs are acceptable. As spending increases, acquisition can get more expensive, and we need to improve to maintain our current costs at higher spend.
5. **In the unit economics review, compare the customers acquired over the previous seven days.** Look at their average net upfront cash collected, their average acquisition cost and the full fulfillment cost for everything they purchased.
6. **In the unit economics review, break down your costs and check whether you are hitting the 2x target.** Break fulfillment down into labor, software and other delivery costs. Break acquisition down into advertising and sales costs. Look at which costs are changing from week to week.
7. **In the unit economics review, match costs to the customers they produced.** Account for your sales cycle, since customers paying this week may have come from earlier spending. Track when they entered the funnel and when they paid, and allow recent leads time to close.
8. **In the metrics review, go through the Dashboard tab of the [Scaling Metrics Tracking Template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1AS4hNqUVefgaCe1szr12kwuVLJVB9sUwlrg5EUCAkLk/edit?usp=sharing) you made a copy of in [Step #5: More Sophisticated Metrics Tracking Setup](https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/031026d9?md=6297cfd3115b49608e1d85f725187dd7).** Review every metric on the Metrics Review list. Use the appropriate period for each metric. Monthly churn needs a month of data. If you are not tracking this stuff, it will not end well when you try to scale. [in this skill: assets/templates/scaling-metrics-tracking-template/]
9. **Assign a named owner for each part of the business, and keep handling the ads yourself.** You are still in charge, but someone needs to update the metrics daily in your Scaling Metrics Tracking Template. Someone needs to manage fulfillment. Someone needs to own sales and follow-up. At this point, you should usually still handle the ads, with help uploading them and doing repetitive work.
10. **Assign the improvements agreed on in each meeting.** Otherwise, you will have the same conversation next week and nothing will change.
