### Framework & Criteria

1. **Scaling Campaign:** Already successful ads.
2. **Testing Campaign:** Trying new creatives.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Create a scaling campaign for your already successful ads.** We need to allocate some of our budget to running proven ads and some to testing new ones.
2. **Create a testing campaign for your new creatives.** This is where every new batch from your ad creative production system gets tested.
3. **Decide how much of your budget goes to testing using the [Unit Economics Model Spreadsheet Template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1623PcXQn_0-KuF9JmcbMkM4zCulrxcpCoVxrCuyceC0/edit?usp=sharing) you made in [Step #9: Unit Economics Model](https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/031026d9?md=d8ac5ae3859646b7a8cb60d848653418).** How much we allocate to testing depends on how profitable our ads are. The more profitable, the more we can afford to test while still hitting our unit economics target. [in this skill: assets/templates/unit-economics-model-spreadsheet-template/]
4. **Include everything you spent when you review the numbers.** Sadly, the testing budget still counts. As a rule of thumb, most tests will fail, so testing will usually bring our overall acquisition cost up. We cannot just look at the winning campaign and pretend the unsuccessful tests didn't cost us anything.

### Completion Audit

- Did you create a scaling campaign for your already successful ads?
- Did you create a testing campaign for your new creatives?
- Did you decide how much of your budget goes to testing using your Unit Economics Model Spreadsheet Template?
- Are you including everything you spent when you review the numbers?
