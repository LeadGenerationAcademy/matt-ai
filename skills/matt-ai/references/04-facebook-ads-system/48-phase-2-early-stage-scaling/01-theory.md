---
This course is for people who have already gotten at least 10 clients from Facebook Ads with their current offer or a substantially similar offer.

You already know how to run ads and get clients. Now you are trying to scale.

There are four stages of scaling:

- Your first 10 clients (Beginner)
- Early-stage scaling (Intermediate)
- Transitory scaling (Advanced)
- Mature scaling (Advanced)

This course covers the second stage. It takes you from your first 10 clients until your current business model is approaching its structural limit.

That point is somewhat subjective, but it becomes obvious when you reach it. Continuing to increase ad spend stops creating more growth and starts exposing deeper problems throughout the business. Sales, fulfillment, marketing, finance, or several of them at the same time.

I do not mean you need a slightly better SOP, another closer or an hour of additional training. I mean scaling further will require you to rebuild a meaningful part of the business.

In a typical white-collar skilled service business, this often happens somewhere between $2.5 million and $5 million per year.

### The #1 Thing You Must Understand

The actual Facebook Ads are one of the least important parts of scaling Facebook Ads.

Your primary measurable constraint will normally be your unit economics. If you do not consciously design them, they will eventually catch up to you.

Most people are dumbfounded when they begin scaling and it does not work. They waste all of their time trying to find a new ad, change their targeting or run another A/B test. Meanwhile, the business underneath the ads is fundamentally incapable of supporting more scale.

You can view this course as showing you how to scale Facebook Ads until you reach the limit of your current unit economics and operating model.

The goal is not to wait until everything collapses. The goal is to recognize the approaching constraint, pause before it becomes destructive and rebuild what needs to be rebuilt.

### This Is Early-Stage Scaling But We Cannot Change Easily Later

In the first phase, we didn't really care about much other than the offer. We just needed to sell something to someone with direct offer ads.

In this phase, we begin to care DEEPLY about the industry, ICP, offer, unit economics and messaging, and we accumulate proof as customers get results.

We have to hold the foundations to a high standard now. Once we scale, changing industries, ICPs or core offers could mean starting over and proving demand, acquisition and fulfillment again.

We can change our processes, technology and team structure as we grow. That is part of scaling. Changing the foundations is going back to phase one.

### Facebook Ads Are One Part Of The Process

In lead generation, the ads are only one piece. There are four phases:

1. **Clicks**
2. **Calls**
3. **Shows**
4. **Closes**

The ad gets someone into the process. They still need to book, show up and buy. If any phase falls apart, you do not get the client.

That is why the first thing in this course is making sure every phase is actually built. Your sales page, VSL, application, welcome flow, reminders, pre-call flow, sales call plan, follow-up and sales assets all need to exist before you pour more money into the top.

### Why Performance Gets Worse As You Scale

People often say their ads die when they increase the budget.

They assume the ads are fatiguing. A lot of the time, the ads are simply not good enough to keep performing as they reach people who are less likely to buy.

At $100 per day, you reach the people most likely to buy. At $1,000 per day, another $900 is reaching the next-best group. Say that group is 20% less likely to buy. Your total spending goes up, but the additional spending is reaching people who are harder to convert.

The setup that works at $100 per day may not be good enough at $1,000, $10,000 or $100,000 per day.

A useful mindset is that every time you want to double spending, you need to make your ads and funnel 50% better to keep the same performance. It will never be literally that clean. But it keeps you focused on improving the system as the budget goes up.

### One-Time (Yet Continuous) Funnel Optimization

Since you have already gotten clients, you probably have some of these pieces in place. Keep the parts that are working, improve what needs work and build anything you are missing.

Then keep improving them. If people repeatedly ask something you thought the VSL explained, improve that explanation. When you get better proof, use it.

Your Top 30 FAQs sit underneath all of it. These are the most important questions and objections people have before buying, taken from real sales conversations in their own language, with the ones that most affect the decision first.

**Perception = reality.** You know everything that makes your offer good. Someone seeing your ad or page for the first time does not. If you get results quickly, do all the work for them or have great proof, SAY IT. None of that helps you sell if people don't know it exists.

People will also get "stuck" at every stage of your funnel. They are interested, just not interested or confident enough to move to the next step. Some get stuck before applying. Some apply and never get on a call. Others get on the call and do not buy.

Your sales assets are how we move these people along. The lead magnet, 6+ hour video course, 3+ hour audit/rebuild and 3+ hour case study give us something useful to put in front of them while they figure it out. If someone wonders whether we can help a business like theirs, they should see a relevant case study. If they do not understand the process, they should see us explain or demonstrate it.

### Application

In the first phase, you may have removed your application to get experience as quickly as possible. Now we want more control over who reaches the calendar.

Your application checks whether someone has the budget, authority, need and timing for your offer before they get access to your calendar. A booked calendar is not particularly useful if the people on it cannot buy. You want to spend your time speaking with people you can actually help and sell to.

A higher application rate is not automatically better. We want at least 90% of the people attending calls to fit our qualification criteria. If they do not, make the ICP and offer more specific in the ad and on the page, then tighten the application where needed.

We have to look at application volume and qualification together.

### Offer Standardization

You already have an offer people have bought. Now we need to make sure we can keep selling it and maintain the results and economics behind it.

Booking calls might still feel difficult right now. But we are building toward the point where it feels as routine as tying our shoes. When that happens, we need to be ready to fulfill more sales. We cannot have every new customer creating a completely different project for the team to figure out.

**We want copy and paste results so fulfillment is not a constraint.** We need enough similarity between customers that the work, processes and learning from one customer are useful for the next. With a standardized offer, we can improve the same workflow through technology, training, AI, automation, assets, templates and SOPs.

**We want our offer and funnel to be so good our sales reps are interchangeable.** We don't want every salesperson having to learn ten different services, prices and delivery requirements. A standardized offer gives us a repeatable sales process we can train people to follow.

Write down the actual offer you are going to sell repeatedly:

- The specific ICP it is for.
- The result and deliverables.
- What is included and what is outside the scope.
- The delivery timeframe.
- What the customer needs to provide or do.
- The price and payment terms.

If we keep changing what is included to get each person to buy, we haven't actually standardized anything.

### Offer Pricing

The target is to collect at least 2X our combined acquisition and fulfillment costs upfront.

For planning, I would never assume less than $2,000 to acquire a customer. If we can fulfill the complete offer for $1,200, that is $3,200 in combined costs. Double it and we need $6,400 collected upfront. I would aim for $6,500 or more, after fees.

Not everyone has an offer that makes sense to charge $6,500 upfront for.

If you sell a recurring service at $2,500 per month, one option is to sell the first three months together and collect payment upfront. That is $7,500. At an 80% gross margin, fulfillment can cost $1,500 for all three months. Add the $2,000 acquisition cost and the total cost is $3,500.

$7,500 ÷ $3,500 = 2.14X before fees.

We get above the 2X target by collecting for a longer service commitment upfront, while keeping the monthly price the same.

### Expand On Your Ads That Are Currently Working

The rest of our strategy will take time to set up. It will not happen overnight. This gives us something to work on immediately, using ads that have already worked, while we build the rest.

Our first opportunity is to turn the winning static image ads into equivalent talking-head videos. Keep the offer and the message that worked. Use the video to explain the offer, show proof and address the questions someone might have.

Even inside your winning ad angle, there are quite a few different ways to say the same thing. Write 10 versions of that message. Each one will literally be about one sentence. Even though they say something very similar, the ads can feel quite different, and one version might appeal to a part of your ICP that another does not.

Then write three versions of the body, around 100 words each, where you explain the offer, show proof and answer their questions. That gives us 30 complete scripts.

Film those 30 scripts in five different setups.

**10 angles × 3 bodies × 5 setups = 150 ads.** All based on messages that have already worked. The idea is to get the next leg up in your ad account without having to come up with 150 new ideas.

**You > Actors > AI.** My preference is that you film these. It is your business, and you need to learn how to speak on camera and make compelling ads. Actors are the second option. AI is the final option, although by the time you fuck around with it, you may have spent longer than it would have taken to film the ad yourself. Most of the reasons not to do it yourself are complete cope. There are no tricks or hacks to getting comfortable on camera. You need to practice.

### More Sophisticated Metrics Tracking Setup

Let's say we want $10 million in total revenue from the customers we acquire, and each customer is worth $20,000 over their lifetime. That means we need 500 customers.

Work backward with some hypothetical funnel numbers and $5 per outbound click:

- A 20% close rate means 2,500 shows.
- A 62.5% show rate means 4,000 calls booked.
- A 50% call-booked rate means 8,000 applications.
- An 8% application rate means 100,000 outbound clicks.
- 100,000 clicks at $5 means $500,000 in ad spend, or about $1,370 per day over a year.

That is $125 in ads per booked call and $1,000 in ads per customer. Your own model needs to use your actual funnel performance.

We also need to track the money coming in, what it costs to acquire and fulfill each customer, and how the funnel performs.

Clone your landing page and calendar event and give them a separate link. A separate event used only in this funnel gives us a simple way to identify the calls it booked. It is not perfect, but at this phase, it tracks funnel bookings without needing a complicated attribution system.

### Schedule Meetings

**Fulfillment:** Give your head of fulfillment, or whoever does the work, a clear target. 80%+ gross margin while maintaining results.

**Acquisition:** This includes your marketing team, which is probably mostly you, and your sales reps. Our acquisition cost needs to support the 2X target, so the maximum CAC is net upfront cash collected ÷ 2, minus the full fulfillment cost. Keep having this meeting even when acquisition costs are acceptable. As spending increases, acquisition can get more expensive.

**Unit economics:** For customers acquired over the previous seven days, compare average net upfront cash collected, average acquisition cost and the full fulfillment cost. Account for your sales cycle. Customers paying this week may have come from earlier spending.

**Metrics:** Keep the numbers updated daily and review them weekly. If you are not tracking this stuff, it will not end well when you try to scale.

You are still in charge, but each part needs a named owner. Someone updates the metrics. Someone manages fulfillment. Someone owns sales and follow-up. At this point, you should usually still handle the ads yourself.

Assign the improvements agreed on in each meeting. Otherwise, you will have the same conversation next week and nothing will change.

### Sales Framework

Once we can consistently book qualified calls, we need enough people who can actually close them.

If you are the only person who can sell the offer, your calendar becomes the constraint. We need to be able to hire someone, train them and get them producing without you personally handling every opportunity.

We want the offer, funnel and sales process to be so good that our closers are as close to cashiers as possible. They still need to understand the customer and handle the conversation. But they should not have to invent the pitch or figure out how to sell the offer from scratch.

The framework puts the pieces of the sales process together so someone else can follow them: pre-call preparation, the call plan, the offer details, the follow-up and the tracking. Include examples of good calls so a new closer can hear what this actually looks like, then review their calls against the process.

Start by documenting what is working now, then improve it as you take more calls.

### Fulfillment Framework

Fulfillment will become a huge bottleneck once we perfect our ads. We can hire more people, but they need to know exactly what to do. If everyone has to figure things out from scratch, hiring more people becomes expensive very quickly.

**We need copy and paste results.** We want to be able to clone what we are doing, change the client details and get the same outcome for each client.

**As close to "selling air" as possible.** The best businesses have high revenue and very low marginal fulfillment costs. Software and digital advertising are great examples. We will probably never get our offer to that level, but the goal is the same. Increase what we collect while reducing the cost and effort required to fulfill.

Start by listing every single micro step from payment to the customer getting the result. There should probably be 50 to 100+ steps. Do not for one second combine steps here. Include chasing approvals, fixing mistakes and all the little things people forget they spend time doing.

Then go through every step looking for software, AI or automation that makes it quicker or cheaper. We are looking for 1% micro wins over and over. If we keep doing this across the entire workflow, we can work toward cutting our fulfillment costs in half. Templatize everything you keep recreating.

**SOPs and training are different things.** The SOP is a checklist that lets someone get the task done without needing any outside information. Training is where we make sure they can follow it. People will hand someone instructions and barely spend a minute showing them how to do anything. It is pretty insane.

Training follows document, delegate, duplicate. You create the checklist. You do the task in front of them using only the checklist. They do the task in front of you using only the checklist. If either of you needs information that is not on it, the checklist is not good enough yet.

Finally, look at the customer's side. Time to first value needs to be within 24 hours. A task might take thirty minutes but sit untouched for three days because someone is waiting for information or approval. And we want them to get the result with as little work and disruption to their business as possible.

### Unit Economics Model

You can be profitable and still run out of cash. It is a rite of passage as a business owner the first time you discover this.

If it costs us $5,000 to acquire and fulfill a customer and they pay us $1,000 per month for ten months, we made money. But after making the sale, we are still $4,000 in the hole. Now go get 100 customers.

**The promised land is collecting enough upfront to cover the acquisition and fulfillment costs of this customer AND the next customer.** That means collecting at least 2X our combined costs. This is chad scaling territory. We are using the cash from each sale to fund more acquisition.

If Facebook says we spent $2,000 to acquire someone, but we also paid a setter, closer and someone to make the ads, our acquisition cost is higher than $2,000.

There are three targets:

1. **2X+ unit economics multiple:** Net upfront cash collected ÷ combined acquisition and fulfillment costs.
2. **80%+ gross margin:** Fulfillment costs 20% or less of the revenue from the offer. It gets much harder to hit 2X when fulfillment eats up a large part of what we collect.
3. **$250+ effective hourly rate:** As a rule of thumb, I would not price the work below $250 per hour of total team delivery time.

You only increase price when you have demand. My rule of thumb is to raise it when the close rate goes above 25% across a run of sales calls, then by 20% every five clients. Starting at $5,000, four increases takes you to $10,368.

Getting half the clients at twice the price gives us the same revenue with half the total acquisition and fulfillment costs. A lot of the time, you can and should be charging much, much more.

### Choose Scaling Model

There are two approaches we will use.

**Horizontal scaling** takes a proven message and changes its presentation, format or audience language. For a broad, mass-market offer that requires relatively little trust, I would usually begin here.

**Vertical scaling** develops new messaging around different problems, circumstances and desired outcomes. For a more specific, high-ticket offer that requires more explanation and trust, I would usually begin here.

You will eventually use both in Mature Scaling. But you will often get through this Early-Stage Scaling phase with just one of the two.

Either way, the end goal is a repeatable way to produce more winning ads at an acquisition cost that supports our unit economics.

### Horizontal Scaling

We start with one proven ad and create more versions of it. The underlying problem, offer and reason to buy stay consistent while we change how we present them.

There are seven ways to do this:

1. **Different avatars:** Have people of different ages, genders and races recreate the same ad. I am a 30-year-old white guy, and many of the people who respond to my ads are similar to me. Other presenters show whether the same message resonates with other people in the market.
2. **Different formats:** A talking-head video, podcast conversation and whiteboard explanation can all communicate the same message.
3. **Different designs:** Keep the concept and change the layout, typography, colors, images or caption style.
4. **Different concepts:** Frame the same message as a negative, positive, case study, story, how-to or example.
5. **Different keywords:** Keep almost the entire script and swap a few words, like "patients" for a medical provider or "cases" for a law firm.
6. **Different ways to say the same thing:** Change the phrasing while keeping the meaning.
7. **Different backgrounds, locations and settings:** Film the same ad at a desk, in an office, in front of a whiteboard, walking outside or where the work happens.

Take one proven angle with 2 genders, 3 races, 3 age ranges, 5 formats, 3 designs, 6 framings, 3 keyword variations, 3 phrasings and 3 settings. That is 43,740 theoretical combinations from one angle.

You would never need to turn one winner into 43,000+ ads. But it demonstrates there is effectively no upper limit, and any reason why you cannot scale your ads is just cope.

### Vertical Scaling

Here we keep the same customer and offer, but find different problems, circumstances and desired outcomes we can speak to.

People inside the same ICP can care about different things. One person may recognize a problem immediately. Another may have already solved it or have a completely different priority. If we only talk about one problem, we are giving everyone else very little reason to pay attention.

Build a list of at least 50 problems, circumstances and desired outcomes for the exact customer you want. Every one still needs to fit them. An established agency owner may want to add a second acquisition channel or get out of sales. Messaging about getting a first client speaks to someone at a completely different stage.

Do not guess what they care about. Research it.

For years, almost all of my auto insurance ads were some variation of "Save money on auto insurance." Then I copied reviews from the big insurance companies into a spreadsheet and manually ranked what people complained about.

Price was not even in the top 15. I was absolutely flabbergasted and embarrassed.

The complaints were things like "They were nice during the sale, then ghosted me," "They lowballed my claim" and "The claims process took forever." I started making ads around those problems, using the exact language from the reviews. I literally 10X'd the business in four months.

Start with your sales call recordings, then reviews, Reddit, forums and relevant communities. Keep the original wording. If five people describe the same problem differently, keep those versions, but count them as one angle.

**Workhorse angles vs. niche angles.** A workhorse angle could apply to a large portion of our ICP and has the potential to support substantial spend. A niche angle speaks to a more specific situation. Fewer people identify with it, but it can resonate strongly with the people who do.

We want at least 10 potential workhorse angles, aiming to end up with at least four that actually work. And at least 40 potential niche angles, aiming to build a couple dozen useful ones over time.

### Hiring & Team

You will still be responsible for all of this, but the team needs to take stuff off your plate. You need to have at least one person with a brain on your team here. Someone who can follow the process, figure things out and get work finished without asking you about every little thing.

- **Assistant:** A jack of all trades whose primary job is to get you back time.
- **Editor:** Turns your footage into finished video ads.
- **Designer:** Handles static image ads and branding-related work.
- **Setter:** Hire once you consistently get 8+ applications submitted per day.
- **Closer:** Hire once you are getting 25+ calls from paid ads per week and are confident you can scale up.
- **Head of fulfillment:** It is best if you already have one. Eventually, you will not be able to oversee fulfillment and marketing.
- **Head of sales:** Get one once you pass five closers.

### Ad Creative Production System

The top advertisers are producing substantially more creative than almost everyone else. They are making more attempts while improving the quality of those attempts.

As a rough planning assumption, one to three ads out of every hundred may become the winners that get almost all of the spend. That is why we need to be pumping creative. At this stage, I would prioritize quantity and use the repetitions and results to improve the quality.

The target is one creative per week for every $10 per day in budget. $100 per day is 10 creatives per week. A lot of these will come from applying the horizontal scaling methods to something that already works.

The most valuable thing you can get here is the experience and systems-level thinking required to mass produce creative. To me, learning how to solve that is even more valuable than the first version of the system.

Choose five to seven ad formats you have the will to go all in on. Five formats is plenty.

**Editing focuses on proof, not making brainrot.** Identify the ICP within 0.1 seconds, put captions underneath the chin, remove unnecessary pauses and add proof throughout the edit.

**The ad must work with the above the fold and VSL.** The ad creates an expectation, and the page and VSL need to continue explaining that same thing. If we have multiple winning ad angles, we should clone the landing pages and match the headlines.

**Image ads** need instant clarity, high contrast, answers to roughly three objections and a clear CTA. We should not be trying to sell the entire service inside the ad. That is what the page, VSL and sales process are for.

**Video ads** need the ICP and offer within one second, proof within five seconds and everything explained within fifteen seconds. After that, more proof and answers to objections.

**Write the original ads manually every time.** AI copywriting is not where I want it to be for that. Once an ad works, use a copywriting bot to repurpose that winner into other formats. That is where it saves a lot of time, because we have already supplied the thinking and the message we want to preserve.

Lots of people will also see your ads more than once. A static image might make the offer clear, while a longer presentation answers the questions that kept them from booking. Different formats nurture people over time.

### The Five Formats

1. **Static image:** A simple text-based graphic, an Apple Notes-style ad or another layout. The ICP callout, high contrast and clarity matter most.
2. **Looping B-roll:** Basically a static image ad over seven or eight seconds of looping footage.
3. **UGC:** The casual, phone-filmed style. It is meant to look organic.
4. **Talking-head VSL:** The classic style where someone speaks directly to the camera and explains the offer.
5. **Loom + screenshare:** A demonstration of the actual deliverable, account or workflow.

To expand within these five, use different presenters and different framing. Three people in three framings gives you nine versions within a single format.

### Ad Testing System

We usually have two main campaigns. A scaling campaign runs already successful ads. A testing campaign tries new creatives.

How much goes to testing depends on how profitable our ads are. Sadly, the testing budget still counts. Most tests will fail, so testing will usually bring our overall acquisition cost up. We cannot just look at the winning campaign and pretend the unsuccessful tests didn't cost us anything.

We use ABO for testing, with one ad set per group of a particular variable, like format, concept or avatar. If we only have $500 per day for testing, one ad per ad set means each one gets almost nothing. Grouping lets Facebook put the budget toward the ads it chooses to deliver.

We usually judge an ad once it has around 100 outbound clicks. That gives us an initial sample of people going through the funnel.

**Identifying winners requires judgment.** The primary thing we look at is cost per booked call, provided a normal proportion of those people end up closing. There is no clean chart that tells you exactly what to do at 100 clicks.

When we find a winner, we do not immediately add it to the scaling campaign. Turn off the other ads in that ad set, relabel it as a winner and let it keep running. Add it to the scaling campaign the next time you are going to change it anyway. The next batch of creatives goes into new ad sets.

### Winning Ad Loop System

As you make and test more ads, you will train yourself to recognize what keeps working.

We want angles that work across multiple formats. Take a winner, keep the message and offer, and turn it into different concepts and different ad types. Then test the variations.

Say you make twenty variations of one winning ad and fifteen work. That is an angle we can keep producing successful ads around. Do the same with another winner and only two work. We can still run those two, but that angle has not given us the repeated success we are looking for.

Be careful about what you conclude. If you change the avatar, framing and format together, you cannot tell which change caused the difference.

Over time, more and more of your testing will focus on improving and expanding the winners rather than finding something new from scratch. Realistically, we want at least four workhorse angles and fifteen or more niche angles that work across lots of variations.

### Scaling & Decisions

**Golden rule: If your CPA is acceptable, the other metrics can look like shit and the campaign can still work.**

The benchmarks help us troubleshoot when acquisition is too expensive. Follow the progression from clicks to applications, bookings, shows and closes. Find where people are dropping off, then work on that part.

Review spend weekly using the unit economics model:

- **Below 0.75X:** Decrease spend.
- **0.75X to below 1X:** Keep spend the same.
- **1X to below 2X:** Increase spend by 20%.
- **2X or more:** Increase spend by 50%.

Give your sales cycle time to play out.

Start hiring your next closer when the existing sales team reaches roughly 75% capacity. Lead score your calls so your best leads go to your trained closers, and ramp new closers up with spillage from the existing team.

Do the same with fulfillment. Start hiring at roughly 75% capacity, and keep watching what happens as client volume increases. If things are beginning to break, stop increasing ad spend until fulfillment can handle the volume.

### The End Of This Phase

This phase ends when something in the business is seriously broken, and **if you keep scaling, the whole thing will collapse.**

We are talking about something far beyond needing a better SOP or another closer. Adding more people is no longer enough to maintain results, control costs or keep delivery from falling apart.

At that point, stop increasing ad spend. Hold it where it is, or reduce it to a level you can actually handle while you fix the underlying problem.

This is where we move into Transitory Scaling.
