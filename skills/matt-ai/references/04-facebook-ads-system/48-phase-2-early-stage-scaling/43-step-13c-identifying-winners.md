### Framework & Criteria

1. **Workhorse Angles:** These speak to a large portion of your ICP. You are looking for angles that can support substantial spend while continuing to acquire clients at an acceptable cost.
2. **Niche Angles:** These speak to a smaller group with a specific problem or circumstance. They may produce fewer clients and support less spend while still being worth running.

### Example

Target: $200 per booked call

1. Ad A: $1,500 spend, 10 booked calls, $150 per call
2. Ad B: $1,500 spend, 3 booked calls, $500 per call
3. Ad C: $50 spend, 1 booked call, $50 per call

I would be more interested in making variations of Ad A. It has produced repeated results below our target. Ad C looks promising, but we have barely spent anything on it.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Wait until an ad has received around 100 outbound clicks.** We are looking at outbound clicks here, not whether the ad has spent some fixed dollar amount. The same amount of money can buy very different amounts of traffic, and spend alone does not make an ad a winner. One hundred is a round number. There is nothing special about the hundredth click. It gives us an initial sample of people going through the funnel so we can start judging the results. Some ads will barely spend and never get there. That is different from an ad getting 100 outbound clicks and producing poor results.
2. **Check what that ad is producing.** Follow the results through applications, bookings and attended calls. Check the actual people coming through in your CRM, and your UTM codes should pass through.
3. **Compare the cost per booked call with what you can afford using the [Unit Economics Model Spreadsheet Template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1623PcXQn_0-KuF9JmcbMkM4zCulrxcpCoVxrCuyceC0/edit?usp=sharing) you made in [Step #9: Unit Economics Model](https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/031026d9?md=d8ac5ae3859646b7a8cb60d848653418).** At around 100 outbound clicks, cost per booked call is the primary thing we look at. We need to know what we can afford from our unit economics model. Otherwise, we have no basis for deciding whether those calls are too expensive. [in this skill: assets/templates/unit-economics-model-spreadsheet-template/]
4. **Check whether a normal proportion of those calls become clients.** Cheap calls are only useful if they turn into customers. If an ad gets cheap booked calls but those people don't buy at anything close to our normal rate, we cannot judge it the same way.
5. **Give the sales process time and check how much evidence you have before judging the calls.** Yesterday's booked calls cannot be judged as failed sales if the calls have not happened yet. Look at the cost per booked call, how many calls we actually have and what happens to those people afterward. One customer at an acceptable acquisition cost is promising. Ten customers at that same average cost tell us a lot more.
6. **Judge the angle by how much of your ICP it applies to and how it performs across its creative variations.** A niche angle can be successful at a volume that would be disappointing for a workhorse angle. Judge its cost and client quality alongside its potential reach. One ad receiving little spend gives you limited evidence about the potential of the entire angle.
7. **Use your judgment to decide which ads are winners.** There is no clean chart that tells you exactly what to do at 100 clicks. Reaching 100 clicks gives us a point to review the ad. It does not automatically make the result conclusive. With practice, you will get better at recognizing which ads deserve more variations, which need more time and which are falling short.
