### Framework & Criteria

For each format, we need:

1. **Copywriting Framework & Training:** The framework explains what belongs in the ad and how it should be presented. The training shows someone how to apply it.
2. **Editing Or Design Framework:** Explains how to turn that copy into the finished asset.
3. **Copywriting Bot:** Adapts the original ad to that format while following the rules.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Build a copywriting framework and training for each format.** Use finished examples and explain the decisions behind them: why the opening is there, which objection a line answers, what the proof supports and where the CTA leads.
2. **Build an editing or design framework for each format using [Step #12B: Editing Framework](https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/031026d9?md=0a382512e8c74c4f9122efb415327707).** This explains how to turn the copy into the finished asset.
3. **Write the original ads manually every time.** I would not let the bot write the original ads from scratch. AI copywriting is not where I want it to be for that. You decide the angle, wording, offer explanation, proof and objections. Once an ad works, use the bot to repurpose that winner into the formats and script styles you want. That is where it can save us a lot of time, because we have already supplied the thinking and the message we want to preserve.
4. **Give the bot the full context.** When we create the copywriting bot for each format in Step #12G: Copywriting Bot For Each Format, feed in the original ad, the above-the-fold section and the VSL. Include the visuals and transcripts where available. The bot needs to understand what happens after someone clicks. Otherwise, it can produce an ad that sounds reasonable by itself but creates an expectation the page does not support. Also give it the relevant proof, the rules for the format and examples of finished ads we would approve.
5. **Specify what changes and what stays the same.** A static image needs short copy and a layout. A presenter-led video needs a spoken script. A slide presentation needs an explanation spread across slides. The bot can adapt how the information is presented. It should preserve the ICP, offer, claims, proof and next step. It should also follow the format's rules about clarity, timing and what needs to appear on screen.
6. **Review the adaptation against the original before recording or designing it.** Cutting a sentence should not accidentally remove an important condition or change what we are promising.
7. **Save each version you like with the instructions and assets used to produce it.** The next person, and the bot, should be able to see what we mean rather than interpret a vague instruction like "make it engaging."
8. **Update the instructions and examples when you repeatedly make the same correction.** That is how the workflow becomes quicker and more consistent over time.
9. **Use different formats to nurture people over time.** Lots of people will see your ads more than once. Different ads can resonate with them for different reasons while communicating the same underlying offer. A static image might make the offer clear. A demonstration might help them understand what they would receive. A longer presentation might answer the questions that have kept them from booking a call. We do not control the order in which every person sees them, but each ad can contribute something useful. As we improve the mix of ads, we give people more ways to understand the offer and more reasons to believe it.
