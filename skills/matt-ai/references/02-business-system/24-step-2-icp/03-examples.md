### Example #1: Agency Program

After analyzing the previous 100 customers, the company found that approximately 80% fit this profile:

- **Gender:** Male
- **Age:** 25–34
- **Business:** Agency owner
- **Annual Company Revenue:** $500,000–$5,000,000
- **Location:** United States

Characteristics such as age, gender, and location can be used directly when targeting Facebook ads.

The remaining information becomes extremely useful for messaging.

You can research the top 25 problems, circumstances, and desired outcomes experienced by agency owners at this specific level, then use their language to make your ads and funnel resonate with them.

### Example #2: Auto Insurance

Many people would assume the ICP for auto insurance is anyone who owns a vehicle.

However, after analyzing the customers, a much more specific ICP appears:

- **Age:** 25–60
- **Marital Status:** Married
- **Homeowner Status:** Homeowner
- **Vehicles:** Two or more

Married homeowners are more likely to bundle home and auto insurance.

Being married also means the household is more likely to own multiple vehicles and have the ability to pay for additional coverage.

People under 25 are less likely to own a vehicle or may still be covered under their parents’ insurance.

People over 60 may begin driving less and become less valuable as long-term customers.

The ICP is not anyone with a car.

It is married homeowners between 25 and 60 with multiple vehicles and the potential to purchase bundled coverage.

### Example #3: HVAC Repair & Installation

Most people would assume the ICP for HVAC repair is any homeowner whose system breaks down. A much more specific ICP would actually look something like this:

- Age: 35–64
- Homeowner Status: Homeowner
- Annual Household Income: $75,000–$150,000
- Employment: Employed or Business Owner

Renters almost never pay for HVAC repairs themselves, that cost falls on the landlord, so they're not a real customer no matter how broken their AC is.

Homeowners under 35 are more likely to be in newer homes or condos where the system is still under warranty or hasn't failed yet, so there's rarely a job to sell them.

Homeowners over 65 are often on a fixed income and more price-sensitive, they'll delay the repair, shop around for the cheapest quote, or patch the system instead of replacing it.

The $75,000–$150,000 income bracket can afford a full system replacement without stalling for weeks comparing quotes, which is where the real margin is.

The ICP is not anyone with a broken AC unit. It is homeowners aged 35 to 64, earning $75,000 to $150,000 a year, who can move on a repair or replacement decision fast.
