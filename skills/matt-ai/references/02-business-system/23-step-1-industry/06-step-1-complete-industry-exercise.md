The framework below determines whether your industry can support a large and profitable business. There are six criteria worth five points each, for a maximum score of 30.

### Framework & Criteria

#### Criterion #1: High Customer LTV

How much gross profit is one average customer worth to a business inside the industry over the entire customer relationship?

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Excellent**: Huge customer value. Obvious ROI.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Good**: High customer value. Easy to justify your price.

⭐⭐⭐ **Neutral**: Enough customer value to make the economics work.

⭐⭐ **Poor**: Low customer value. You need volume.

⭐ **Bad**: Very low customer value and limited volume. Your price has a hard ceiling.

Calculate one customer’s LTV first, then look at the combined value of those customers.

A lower customer LTV can still support good economics when you can generate enough customers profitably.

Customer LTV Score: ___ / 5

#### Criterion #2: Easy to Reach

How easily can you identify qualified prospects and get your message in front of the person who can buy?

Consider paid ads, content, outreach and partnerships. Also consider how many gatekeepers stand between you and the decision-maker.

You are looking for several practical ways to reach the customer.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Excellent**: Easy to find through multiple channels, with few gatekeepers.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Good**: Easy to find, but you still need to filter them.

⭐⭐⭐ **Neutral**: Reachable, but not directly listable.

⭐⭐ **Poor**: Weak targeting. You are relying on signals.

⭐ **Bad**: No reliable way to identify them

Easy to Reach Score: ___ / 5

#### Criterion #3: Large Market

How many qualified customers exist within the industry, location and company size you are targeting?

Compare that with how many customers you would need to build the business you want.

Customer value matters here too. A smaller market containing valuable customers can still support a large business.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Excellent**: Massive market. You only need a tiny percentage.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Good**: Large market. Plenty of room to grow.

⭐⭐⭐ **Neutral**: Smaller market, but still enough for a high-value offer.

⭐⭐ **Poor**: Limited market. You need too much of it.

⭐ **Bad**: Tiny market. You will quickly run out of prospects.

Large Market Score: ___ / 5

#### Criterion #4: Significant Pain

How painful and urgent is the problem you intend to solve?

Think about what happens if the customer does nothing. Do they lose money, face serious consequences or struggle to operate? How comfortably can they put the problem off?

Score the problem your offer actually addresses for the ICP you want to serve.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Excellent**: Mandatory problem. It cannot be ignored.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Good**: Serious problem. It directly costs them revenue.

⭐⭐⭐ **Neutral**: Real problem, but they can wait.

⭐⭐ **Poor**: Mild problem. Fixing it would be nice.

⭐ **Bad**: No serious problem.

Significant Pain Score: ___ / 5

#### Criterion #5: Spending Power

Can the average qualified customer comfortably afford your offer?

Customer LTV tells you what a customer could be worth to them. Spending power tells you whether they have money available to pay you.

Judge this against what you intend to charge. Someone can badly need your service and still be unable to afford it.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Excellent**: They can comfortably afford an expensive service.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Good**: Strong spending power.

⭐⭐⭐ **Neutral**: They can pay, but they still need a clear return.

⭐⭐ **Poor**: Limited spending power.

⭐ **Bad**: They cannot afford a serious service.

Spending Power Score: ___ / 5

#### Criterion #6: Growing Market

Is the industry growing, remaining stable or declining?

Use market data from the previous three years where possible. Look at what is happening to demand, customer spending and the number of people buying.

The examples show the types of signals you are looking for. Check whether those signals apply to the specific market you are evaluating.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Excellent**: Fast-growing market. You get the tailwinds.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Good**: Clearly growing market.

⭐⭐⭐ **Neutral**: Stable market. Completely fine.

⭐⭐ **Poor**: Shrinking market. Growth gets harder.

⭐ **Bad**: Dying market. You are fighting the current.

Growing Market Score: ___ / 5

#### Final Score

Add your six scores together.

Total Score: ___ / 30

Use the following as a general guide:

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Excellent**: 27–30

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Good**: 24–26

⭐⭐⭐ **Neutral**: 20–23

⭐⭐ **Poor**: 15–19

⭐ **Bad**: 6–14

Five-star scores should be rare.

Look at the individual scores as well as the total. A large market does not fix customers who cannot afford you. Easy access does not fix a problem nobody cares about.

A score of one or two for Customer LTV, Easy to Reach, Significant Pain or Spending Power can make the industry extremely difficult regardless of how well it scores elsewhere.

### Example

**Wealth Management**

Managing money for people who already have a lot of it.

- High Customer LTV: **Excellent** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐. One client can stay for years and keep paying you. A good relationship can be worth a huge amount.
- Easy to Reach: **Neutral** ⭐⭐⭐. You can get in front of wealthy people, but finding someone who wants to change wealth managers is harder.
- Large Market: **Good** ⭐⭐⭐⭐. There are plenty of potential clients, and you do not need thousands of them to build a very good business.
- Significant Pain: **Neutral** ⭐⭐⭐. Managing their money matters, but they can usually put off hiring someone or stay with whoever they already use.
- Spending Power: **Excellent** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐. Your customers have money. That is literally who you are choosing to work with.
- Growing Market: **Good** ⭐⭐⭐⭐. More people retiring and needing their money to last creates more demand for this.

**Total: 24/30 — Good**

### Residential Roofing

Repairing and replacing roofs in a city and the surrounding area.

- High Customer LTV: **Good** ⭐⭐⭐⭐. One roof replacement can produce a good amount of profit. The downside is that the same person probably will not need another one for years.
- Easy to Reach: **Neutral** ⭐⭐⭐. Finding homeowners is easy. Finding the ones who need a roof and are ready to pay for it takes more work.
- Large Market: **Good** ⭐⭐⭐⭐. There are plenty of houses. You only need a small percentage of the jobs in your area to keep your crews busy.
- Significant Pain: **Excellent** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐. If water is coming through the roof, putting it off means damaging the rest of the house.
- Spending Power: **Neutral** ⭐⭐⭐. Owning a house does not mean someone has the cash for a new roof. Some will need financing or insurance to cover it.
- Growing Market: **Neutral** ⭐⭐⭐. Assuming a stable area, roofs keep wearing out and needing replacement. We are not relying on a construction boom.

**Total: 22/30 — Neutral**

### Independent Restaurant

A normal restaurant serving people in a large city.

- High Customer LTV: **Poor** ⭐⭐. You do not make much from one visit. Regular customers help, but you still need a lot of people coming through.
- Easy to Reach: **Good** ⭐⭐⭐⭐. People can find you through Google, social media, recommendations, or literally walking past the restaurant.
- Large Market: **Good** ⭐⭐⭐⭐. There are plenty of people who eat out. Having lots of competitors does not mean the market is small.
- Significant Pain: **Poor** ⭐⭐. They need to eat, but they do not need your restaurant. They can cook or go somewhere else.
- Spending Power: **Neutral** ⭐⭐⭐. People can afford a meal, but there is a limit to how much they will spend and how often.
- Growing Market: **Neutral** ⭐⭐⭐. People still eat out. This is not some exploding market where growth does half the work for you.

**Total: 18/30 — Poor**

**Local Newspaper**

Selling printed newspaper subscriptions to people in your area.

- High Customer LTV: **Poor** ⭐⭐. Subscribers pay repeatedly, but you also keep paying to print and deliver their papers. You need a lot of subscribers.
- Easy to Reach: **Neutral** ⭐⭐⭐. You can reach local people. Finding the ones who still want to pay for a physical newspaper is harder.
- Large Market: **Poor** ⭐⭐. It is only people in your area who want the printed version. Everyone who reads news is not your customer.
- Significant Pain: **Poor** ⭐⭐. Local news can matter, but most people do not need it delivered on paper.
- Spending Power: **Neutral** ⭐⭐⭐. Most people can afford a subscription. That does not mean they will pay whatever you want.
- Growing Market: **Bad** ⭐. Print readership has been shrinking for years. You are trying to grow while the number of potential customers goes down.

**Total: 13/30 — Bad**

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Confirm you are in a real industry and not a non-industry.** Roofing, HVAC, insurance, healthcare and law are real industries. Ecommerce, SaaS, agencies, coaches, consultants and local businesses are not. If your business feels harder than it should be and you cannot put your finger on why, this is usually it.
2. **Use real data wherever possible.** If exact data is unavailable, compare what you know with the descriptions and make your best estimate.
3. **Score High Customer LTV out of 5.** Calculate one customer's LTV first, then look at the combined value of those customers. Score the average qualified customer, not the largest or most successful business you can find.
4. **Score Easy to Reach out of 5.** Consider paid ads, content, outreach and partnerships, and how many gatekeepers stand between you and the decision-maker. You are looking for several practical ways to reach the customer.
5. **Score Large Market out of 5.** Filter by location, company size and category first, then count. Compare that number against the size of business you actually want, and treat around 10,000 qualified people in your ICP as enough.
6. **Score Significant Pain out of 5.** Think about what happens if the customer does nothing. Do they lose money, face serious consequences or struggle to operate, and how comfortably can they put the problem off?
7. **Score Spending Power out of 5.** Customer LTV tells you what a customer could be worth to them. Spending power tells you whether they have money available to pay you.
8. **Score Growing Market out of 5.** Use market data from the previous three years where possible, and look at what is happening to demand, customer spending and the number of people buying.
9. **Add your six scores together for a total out of 30.** Six criteria worth five points each.
10. **Compare your total against the scoring guide.** Scores run from 6-14 Bad up through 27-30 Excellent, and five-star scores should be rare.
11. **Look at your individual scores as well as the total.** A large market does not fix customers who cannot afford you. Easy access does not fix a problem nobody cares about. A one or two for Customer LTV, Easy to Reach, Significant Pain or Spending Power can make the industry extremely difficult regardless of how well it scores elsewhere.
12. **Decide what you are doing based on the score.** Anything less than neutral and I would definitely consider changing industries. If it comes out bad, I would plan to quit everything I am doing right now and start brainstorming my next opportunity.
