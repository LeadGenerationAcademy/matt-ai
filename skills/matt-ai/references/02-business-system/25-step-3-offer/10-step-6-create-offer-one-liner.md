Create one sentence explaining what you do.

This is what you say when someone asks "What do you do?" It must take 7 seconds or less to say out loud.

### Framework & Criteria

**Your One-Liner should make two things immediately clear:**

1. Who you help
2. What result or deliverable you provide

Mention the mechanism, speed, or reduced effort only if it is important and still fits within 7 seconds.

**Use This Template**
"We help [specific ICP] [achieve a clear result or receive a clear deliverable]."

### Example

**Example #1:**

"We fill roofing sales teams' calendars with qualified homeowner appointments."

**Example #2:**

"We keep multi-location healthcare practices out of six-figure patient data fines."

**Example #3:**

"We hand home service companies sales reps who've already been tested.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Make a copy of the Offer One-Liner Template.** This is the same "Offer Templates" doc from earlier steps, just a different tab.
2. **Write three versions of your One-Liner using the template above.** Testing multiple versions side by side makes it easier to see which one actually lands.
3. **Fill in [specific ICP] with who you help in each version.** Use the same ICP you already built, which is the 2 to 5 characteristics most of your customers share. Name them specifically, like "roofing sales teams," so whoever hears it can qualify or unqualify themselves.
4. **Fill in [achieve a clear result or receive a clear deliverable] with what you provide in each version.** Ideally, state a result. If you do not control the final result, state the clear deliverable that is directly connected to it. Whoever hears it should know exactly what they would be buying and receiving.
5. **Add the mechanism, speed, or reduced effort only if it is important and still fits within 7 seconds.** Who you help and what you provide are the two things your One-Liner has to make immediately clear. Anything else is extra.
6. **Open the Stopwatch on your phone and time yourself saying each version out loud.** This gives you an objective read on your timing instead of guessing. Start the Stopwatch before you start talking, or your timing won't be accurate. Say it the way you actually would to a stranger, not a rehearsed reading. The time on the Stopwatch when you finish is your real time to compare against the 7-second limit.
7. **Remove words until each version takes 7 seconds or less.** Cut anything that isn't who you help or what result you provide. Time and trim all three versions the same way before picking a winner.
8. **Say it to someone unfamiliar with your business, ask them what they think you do, and simplify it again if they cannot explain it back to you.** A stranger's reaction tells you more than your own judgment does. Their answer, in their own words, is the real test of whether the One-Liner worked. Keep cutting until anyone can repeat back what you do after hearing it once.
9. **Write your finished One-Liner into the "Write Your Offer One-Liner" section of the document.** You should be able to say it immediately without thinking, hesitating, or using any "umms" or "uhhs," memorize it so well you sound like a robot when someone asks, answering within 0.1 seconds.
