Create the presentation you or your sales team will use during sales calls. The purpose of the presentation is to explain the Offer clearly, answer the major objections, and ask the customer to buy.

### Framework & Criteria

1. **Offer and Primary Result:** Your Offer stated clearly, along with the result it gets them. If you do not control the final result, state the clear deliverable directly connected to it.
2. **Who the Offer Is For:** The person you sell to inside your industry. Name the specific characteristics of your ICP, usually 2 to 5, so people can qualify or unqualify themselves.
3. **Their Main Problem:** Something going wrong that your ICP wants fixed, and that your Offer solves. Describe it in the exact way they describe it.
4. **Their Current Circumstances:** The situation they currently find themselves in, usually a negative one. Include the specific thoughts they're having and the experiences they've been through.
5. **Their Desired Outcome:** Something they want to achieve, and the reason they want their problem solved. It has to be what your level of ICP actually wants.
6. **Why Previous Solutions Failed:** Why common solutions or what they've tried previously has failed.
7. **How Your Offer Works:** Your mechanism, the believable explanation for why your Offer should produce the result. Show why it's different from what they've tried before and why it's the best vehicle to get them to their dream outcome. It does not need to be fancy.
8. **What They Receive:** All the deliverables of your Offer related to getting them their dream outcome, so they know exactly what they're buying and receiving.
9. **Delivery Timeline:** The timeline and steps they'll go through to achieve their dream outcome with your Offer, including when they get their first meaningful result.
10. **Proof and Case Studies:** Evidence that you can solve the problem they're coming to you to solve. Use images, case studies, and testimonials from customers at the same level of ICP.
11. **Price and Payment Options:** The price of your Offer and the payment options you have.
12. **Risk Reversal:** How much downside they face if the Offer doesn't work out. Their fee isn't their only risk, since they can also lose time, money, opportunity and reputation. A guarantee can help, but never guarantee a result you do not fully control.
13. **Next Step:** The exact next step they should take after your sales call.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Make a copy of the [Offer Presentation Template](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1oaKb3FPLReXbO9UIST2MhJjG01HNbRcsaE-5rkP7BwU/edit?usp=sharing).** This becomes the working file where you'll build out all 13 content slides. Slide 1 links to an example presentation built using this exact template. Use it as a reference if you get stuck on any individual slide. [in this skill: assets/templates/offer-presentation-template/]
2. **Fill in Slide 2: Offer and primary result.** State your Offer clearly, along with the main result it gets them. Keep this consistent with the same ICP, problem, dream outcome, deliverables, timeline, price, mechanism, proof, and risk reversal your Offer already has.
3. **Fill in Slide 3: Who the Offer is for.** Do not accidentally create a different Offer inside the presentation, this needs to match the ICP you already defined. Name the specific characteristics of your ICP, so people can qualify or unqualify themselves.
4. **Fill in Slide 4: Their main problem.** This should stay consistent with the problem you've already identified for your Offer. Describe it as the main problem your ICP is facing that your Offer solves, and use their language and experiences.
5. **Fill in Slide 5: Their current circumstances.** This is how they see their problems right now, including the specific thoughts they're currently having and the experiences they've been through. This leads naturally into the next slide, their desired outcome.
6. **Fill in Slide 6: Their desired outcome.** This should match the same Dream Outcome your Offer is already built around. This is the reason they want to solve their problem in the first place.
7. **Fill in Slide 7: Why previous solutions failed.** Cover the common solutions and what they've tried before, and why those failed. This sets up the next slide, how your Offer's mechanism is different.
8. **Fill in Slide 8: How your Offer works.** This is where your mechanism goes, keep it consistent with the Offer you've already built. Explain why your Offer is different from what they've tried before, and why it's the best vehicle to get them to their dream outcome. Your mechanism is simply the believable explanation for why your Offer should produce the result. It does not need to be fancy.
9. **Fill in Slide 9: What they receive.** This should list the same deliverables your Offer already promises. Include every deliverable related to getting them their dream outcome.
10. **Fill in Slide 10: Delivery timeline.** Keep this the same timeline you've already committed to elsewhere in your Offer. Lay out the timeline and steps they'll go through to achieve their dream outcome with your Offer.
11. **Fill in Slide 11: Proof and case studies.** Use images, case studies, and testimonials. Use proof from customers matching the same level of ICP you are targeting, if you're targeting high-level people, do not use beginner-level proof.
12. **Fill in Slide 12: Price and payment options.** This should match the same price you've already set for your Offer. Include the payment options you have.
13. **Fill in Slide 13: Risk reversal.** Use the same guarantee or risk-reducing element your Offer already includes. This is your guarantee, if you have one, or anything related to your Offer that reduces the prospect's risk.
14. **Fill in Slide 14: Next step.** End by clearly asking them to buy and showing them exactly what to do next after your sales call. Don't leave the ask implicit, state it directly.
15. **Use one clear idea per slide.** Don't cram multiple ideas into a single slide, it makes the presentation harder to follow.
16. **Answer the major objections throughout your presentation.** There are usually about 6 to 12 objections that come up repeatedly on sales calls, make sure your presentation addresses them.
