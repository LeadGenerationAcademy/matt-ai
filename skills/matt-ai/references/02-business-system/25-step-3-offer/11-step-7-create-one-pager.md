### Framework & Criteria

1. **Offer Name**
2. **Who It's For:** Your ICP.
3. **The Problem It Solves:** Your main problem.
4. **What You Get:** The result or deliverable.
5. **How It Works:** Your mechanism.
6. **What's Included:** What they receive.
7. **Timeline:** Your delivery timeline.
8. **Proof:** Relevant proof, images work here.
9. **Price:** Price or starting price, if applicable.
10. **Next Step:** The next step.

**Example 1**

1. Exclusive HVAC Leads
2. **Who it's for:** Residential HVAC companies doing $1M+ with 5 or more technicians.
3. **The problem it solves:** Your schedule runs on referrals. Some weeks are packed, some are empty.
4. **What you get:** 20 exclusive homeowner leads in 30 days, or we work free until you hit it.
5. **How it works:** We only do residential HVAC, running campaigns already tested on our other HVAC clients.
6. **What's included:** Google LSA and Meta campaigns, HVAC landing page, call tracking into your CRM, account manager, weekly reporting.
7. **Timeline:** Days 1-14 build. Day 15 live. Day 30 review and plan month two.
8. **Proof:** 44 qualified leads per client on average in the first 30 days.
9. **Price:** $3,000/month, month to month. Ad spend separate. No contract.
10. **Next step:** Book a free strategy call and see your campaign plan before spending a dollar.

**Example 2**

1. Patient Data Compliance
2. **Who it's for:** Healthcare practices with more than one location.
3. **The problem it solves:** Patient data sits across locations with nobody owning it. One breach averages a $250,000 fine.
4. **What you get:** Your patient data brought into compliance and kept there.
5. **How it works:** We fix the gaps ourselves instead of handing you a report to implement.
6. **What's included:** Gap audit at every location, remediation by our team, staff training, all documentation, quarterly re-audits.
7. **Timeline:** Day 3 first gap report. Day 30 gaps closed. Quarterly re-audits after.
8. **Proof:** 200+ multi-location practices. Zero reportable breaches.
9. **Price:** $2,500 per location, per year.
10. **Next step:** Book a 20-minute call and we'll tell you which gaps you have before you pay.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Make a copy of the [One-Pager Template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/10trg6w97DUTYKPTB1YtRkgCMXHD8cHTDglJ4sZmKP5o/edit?tab=t.h0qnoq4r53hs), then scroll to the "Write Your Offer One-Pager" section of the document.** If you've already made a copy of the Offer Templates document for an earlier step, just use that same copy instead of making a new one. The "Write Your Offer One-Pager" section is the blank fill-in version, separate from the "Use This Template" instructions and worked example above it. [in this skill: assets/templates/offer-audit-template/]
2. **Fill in your Offer Name.** This is the first thing someone sees, so it should be immediately clear. Skip the stupid-sounding names like "Six-Step Email Revenue Vortex" that try to pass as a unique mechanism. A plain name that says what it is works better.
3. **Fill in Who It's For.** Use the same ICP you already built for the rest of your Offer. Name the specific characteristics of your ICP, so people can qualify or unqualify themselves the second they read it.
4. **Fill in The Problem It Solves.** Use the same problem you've already identified elsewhere in your Offer. Write it as the main problem your ICP is facing, using their language and experiences.
5. **Fill in What You Get.** Use the same Dream Outcome your Offer is already built around. Ideally, sell a result. If you do not control the final result, write the clear deliverable that is directly connected to it.
6. **Fill in How It Works.** Keep this consistent with the mechanism you've already established. Your mechanism is simply the believable explanation for why your Offer should produce the result. It does not need to be fancy. Someone should read it and think "That makes sense."
7. **Fill in What's Included.** This should list the same deliverables your Offer already promises. List what the customer actually receives. If you only describe the activities you do, they still won't know what they're getting.
8. **Fill in Timeline.** Keep this the same delivery timeline you've already committed to elsewhere in your Offer. Show when they receive each part, including their first meaningful result, measured from when they buy.
9. **Fill in Proof, Price and Next Step.** Images work well for Proof, since the whole point is for someone to understand your Offer within seconds. For Price, use your actual price or starting price if it varies, and don't leave this section vague. Make Next Step a single, specific action, not a vague suggestion.
10. **Use short headlines, bullet points, and images of proof, and remove anything that does not help someone understand the Offer or take the next step.** Long paragraphs work against the one-page limit. If a sentence doesn't earn its place, cut it.
11. **Open the finished One-Pager on your iPhone, take one screenshot, and shorten it if anything is cut off.** This is your actual test for whether it's really one page. If the entire thing doesn't fit inside one screenshot, it's not a One-Pager. Keep cutting content until everything fits inside that single screenshot.
12. **Save the One-Pager as a PDF, an image, and a link anyone can open, then keep all three versions somewhere you and your team can access immediately.** The image makes it easy to send directly in a text or DM without opening a link. The link is useful when you want someone to be able to view it without downloading anything. The next time someone asks for more information, you should be able to send it within seconds.
