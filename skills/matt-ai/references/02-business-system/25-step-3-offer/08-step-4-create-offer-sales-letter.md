Create the written version of your Offer. Your Sales Letter should explain the Offer clearly, overcome the major objections, and ask the customer to buy. It should be written in your exact voice: a good litmus test is whether you could hear yourself saying it out loud. If not, change your writing.

### Framework & Criteria

1. **ICP:** Who this is for.
2. **Headline:** The primary result they get.
3. **Their Main Problem:** The real block stopping them.
4. **Their Current Circumstances:** How they see their situation right now.
5. **Their Desired Outcome:** What they actually want.
6. **Why Previous Solutions Failed:** What they tried and why it did not work.
7. **Your Mechanism:** The thing that makes your approach different.
8. **How It Works:** The steps your Offer goes through.
9. **What's Included:** Everything they receive.
10. **Timeline:** When they see results and when it is fully delivered.
11. **Proof:** Case studies from clients at the same level of ICP.
12. **Objections:** The major objections and your answers to them.
13. **Price:** Price and payment options.
14. **Next Step:** Exactly what to do to start.

### Examples

**Example #1**

**1. ICP**

This is for residential HVAC companies doing $1M+ a year in the United States.

You need at least 5 technicians on staff, authority over the marketing budget, and you want a repeatable system instead of relying on referrals alone. If you're a two-truck operation, this isn't for you yet.

**2. Headline**

Get 20 qualified homeowner leads booked on your calendar in the next 30 days.

**3. Their main problem**

Your business is dependent on referrals.

Some weeks your guys have more jobs than they can handle. Other weeks the schedule is wide open and nobody knows why. You're living off referrals and repeat customers, so your calendar depends on stuff you don't control.

**4. Their current circumstances**

Right now you're either running ads yourself with no real system behind them, or you already tried an agency that took your budget, sent a handful of unqualified leads, and never delivered what was promised.

You've started to believe paid advertising just doesn't work for a business like yours. You're doing the math on whether it's worth trying again.

**5. Their desired outcome**

You want a calendar booked out weeks in advance.

You want ready-to-buy homeowners filling your technicians' schedules, not more phone calls to sort through. You want recurring revenue predictable enough to plan hiring around, and you want to spend your time running the business instead of chasing your own leads.

**6. Why previous solutions failed**

Referrals don't scale. You can't grow a business waiting for the phone to ring.

SEO takes months to produce anything, which is more patience than most HVAC owners can afford.

Generic agencies build the same funnel for every industry, so nothing is built around how homeowners actually search for and buy HVAC service.

Hiring an in-house marketer means paying a full salary before you know whether they can deliver.

**7. Your mechanism**

We only do residential HVAC.

Every ad, landing page, and offer is built around how homeowners search for and buy HVAC service. We run the same campaign system across every HVAC client, so it's already tested before it ever runs for you.

Your dedicated account manager builds, launches, and optimizes your campaigns on that system. All you have to do is pick up the phone and close the leads we send you.

**8. How it works**

We start by getting access to your ad platforms and CRM. We build your ad creative and landing page and send it to you for approval. We set up call tracking and test the CRM connection end to end. Then campaigns go live across Google Local Service Ads and Meta, and your account manager optimizes them daily from the first click.

**9. What's included**

Fully built and managed Google Local Service Ads and Meta campaigns

A custom landing page built specifically for residential HVAC offers

Call tracking and lead notifications connected to your CRM or inbox

A dedicated account manager for your account

Weekly reporting on ROAS, cost per lead, and campaign performance

**10. Timeline**

Days 1-4: Onboarding call, account setup, and access to your ad platforms and CRM.

Days 5-10: Ad creative and landing page built and sent to you for approval.

Days 11-14: Call tracking and CRM integration tested and confirmed working.

Day 15: Campaigns go live across Google Local Service Ads and Meta, and your account manager monitors performance from the first click.

Days 15-30: Leads come in while your account manager optimizes daily, then we review your numbers with you on day 30 to plan month two.

**11. Proof**

We've worked with hundreds of residential HVAC companies. Our average is 44 qualified leads per client in the first 30 days.

**12. Objections**

"I hired an agency before and got garbage leads." Most agencies take the same funnel they built for a dentist and point it at you. Everything we run was built for residential HVAC and tested on other HVAC companies first. You also see your cost per lead every week, so you'll know exactly what you're getting.

"Are you selling these same leads to my competitors?" No. Every lead we generate is yours exclusively.

"I don't have time to manage another vendor." You get one account manager and a weekly report. You don't touch the ad account.

"I don't want to be locked into anything." You aren't. Month to month, no setup fee, cancel whenever.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Make a copy of the [Offer Sales Letter Template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/10trg6w97DUTYKPTB1YtRkgCMXHD8cHTDglJ4sZmKP5o/edit?tab=t.0).** If you've already made a copy of the Offer Templates document for an earlier step, just use that same copy instead of making a new one. [in this skill: assets/templates/offer-audit-template/]
2. **Fill in the Keep The Offer Consistent section.** Copy all eight fields straight across from your Sales Presentation, don't rewrite them here. This is what you write the letter against, and if any of them come out different, you've built two different Offers.
3. **Write your ICP.** Use the same ICP you already defined for your Sales Presentation, don't build a different one here. Name the specific characteristics of who this is for, so the reader can qualify or unqualify themselves.
4. **Write your headline featuring the primary result.** This is the main result they get from your Offer. It should be written in your exact voice, if you couldn't hear yourself saying it out loud, rewrite it.
5. **Describe their main problem.** This is the real block stopping them, the main problem your ICP is facing that your Offer solves. Use their language, not a generic description. You collect these properly in the Messaging course later on, so come back and sharpen this once you have it.
6. **Describe their current circumstances.** This is how they see their situation right now, including the specific thoughts they're having and the experiences they've been through. Use their language again, and come back to this after the Messaging course the same way.
7. **Describe their desired outcome.** Use the same Dream Outcome your Offer is already built around. This is what they actually want, and the reason they want to solve their problem in the first place.
8. **Explain why previous solutions failed.** This should use the same reasoning already established in your Sales Presentation. Cover the common solutions and what they've tried before, and why each one didn't work.
9. **Introduce your mechanism.** Keep this consistent with the mechanism from your Sales Presentation, a different mechanism here means you've built two different Offers. Your mechanism is the thing that makes your approach different, and the believable explanation for why your Offer should produce the result. It does not need to be fancy. The reader should think "That makes sense."
10. **Explain how the Offer works.** Keep the steps consistent with what you already presented on your sales calls. Walk through the steps your Offer goes through, in the order they happen.
11. **List everything they receive.** This should list the same deliverables your Offer already promises. List what they actually receive. If you only describe the activities you do, they still won't know what they're getting.
12. **Explain the delivery timeline.** Keep this the same timeline you've already committed to elsewhere in your Offer. Show when they see results and when the Offer is fully delivered.
13. **Show relevant proof and case studies.** Use proof from clients at the same level of ICP you're targeting, don't use beginner-level proof for high-level customers.
14. **Answer the major objections.** Write down the ones you already hear on calls. There are usually about 6 to 12 objections that come up again and again. The Messaging course gives you the full list in their exact words, so you'll be able to make this sharper afterward.
15. **Present the price and payment options.** This should match the same price you've already set for your Offer. Include every payment option you have, so the reader doesn't have to ask.
16. **Tell them exactly what to do next and end with a clear call to action.** End with a clear, specific next step, not a vague suggestion. Don't leave the ask implicit, state it directly.
17. **Use one clear idea per headline, keep paragraphs short, and remove unnecessary words, vague claims, and corporate language.** Don't cram multiple ideas into a single headline. Long, dense paragraphs make the letter harder to get through. Write it the way you'd actually speak it.
18. **Make sure the customer understands the Offer even if they only read the headlines.** Someone skimming should still walk away knowing what you're offering.
