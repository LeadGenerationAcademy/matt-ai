### Framework & Criteria

#### The Simple Version

There are four ways to make an Offer more valuable:

1. Give the customer a better result
2. Make them believe they are more likely to achieve it
3. Give them value faster
4. Require less work from them

5 stars always means good. 1 star always means bad.

#### The Value Formula

Alex Hormozi's Value Formula is:

**Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice)**

You do not need to calculate the actual formula. Score the four parts below and improve your lowest score.

#### Score #1: Dream Outcome

How valuable is the result to your specific ICP?

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** Solves an urgent problem the customer feels they must address now.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** Solves an important problem the customer clearly wants to address.

⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** Solves a real and valuable problem, but the customer is comfortable waiting.

⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** Solves a minor problem or provides a useful nice-to-have.

⭐ **1 Star:** Solves a problem the customer barely cares about.

Dream Outcome Score: ___ / 5

#### Score #2: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement

How strongly does the customer believe your Offer will work for someone like them?

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** Has overwhelming proof from customers in similar situations and makes success feel extremely likely.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** Has strong relevant proof, a clear mechanism and answers to the major objections.

⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** Has a believable explanation and some proof.

⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** The promise sounds possible but is mostly unsupported.

⭐ **1 Star:** The customer has no reason to believe it will work.

Perceived Likelihood Score: ___ / 5

#### Score #3: Time to First Value

How quickly does the customer receive their first meaningful result or benefit?

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** Within 24 hours.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** More than 24 hours, but within seven days.

⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** More than seven days, but within 30 days.

⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** More than 30 days, but within 90 days.

⭐ **1 Star:** More than 90 days.

Time to First Value Score: ___ / 5

#### Score #4: Customer Effort and Sacrifice

How much work must the customer complete to receive the value?

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** Almost everything is done for them, with minimal onboarding and almost no ongoing work.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** The customer completes some onboarding and a small amount of ongoing work.

⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** The customer must participate regularly, but the workload is manageable.

⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** The customer must complete substantial work throughout the service.

⭐ **1 Star:** The customer must do almost everything.

Effort and Sacrifice Score: ___ / 5

### Scoring Scale

- 4-7: Terrible
- 8-11: Weak
- 12-15: Acceptable
- 16-18: Strong
- 19-20: Exceptional

### Examples

**Dream Outcome**

Say you sell to agency owners doing $500k to $5m per year.

"Get your first client" is a bad desired result. It sounds desirable for an agency owner, but not this one. They already have clients.

Better versions:

- Build a second acquisition channel
- Get out of sales
- Scale paid ads without their cost per acquisition exploding
- Build a marketing team that generates customers without them
- Create consistent lead flow so revenue stops being feast or famine

**Perceived Likelihood of Achievement**

Weak: "We help businesses generate more leads."

Strong: "We exclusively work with residential HVAC companies with three or more crews. Here are 27 examples of companies like yours, what they paid per lead and how many of those leads turned into jobs."

The result barely changed. The second one just feels far more likely to work, because they can see people like them getting it.

**Time to First Value**

Weight loss, ranked by how fast someone gets a result and what each one can charge:

- Calorie and fitness apps where you do everything yourself: $9.99 per month
- A personal trainer and a meal plan: more than the apps
- Liposuction: thousands to tens of thousands
- A method where you swipe your card and instantly have a six-pack: you could charge $100,000 or more

I sell leads in auto insurance. Clients get their first lead within 48 hours, but I hand them five quick wins the moment they sign, including a sales script, an appointment setting message, and branding changes for the page the ads run to. They get value before my actual service has even been delivered.

**Customer Effort and Sacrifice**

Two companies promise the exact same result, at the same price, in the same amount of time.

Offer A: you attend three calls per week, complete homework between calls, learn the process, build everything yourself, then spend another five hours a week implementing it.

Offer B: you do one 30-minute onboarding call, give them access, and they do the rest.

Which one would you rather buy? Offer B, every time.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Make a copy of your [Offer Amplification Template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/10trg6w97DUTYKPTB1YtRkgCMXHD8cHTDglJ4sZmKP5o/edit?tab=t.b208gf6wqxc3).** If you've already made a copy of the Offer Templates document for an earlier step, just use that same copy instead of making a new one, it's the same document, just a different tab. [in this skill: assets/templates/offer-audit-template/]
2. **Score Dream Outcome using the template.** This is how valuable the result is to your specific ICP. To improve this score, offer a result that makes them more money, saves them more time, reduces more risk, or solves a more important problem.
3. **Score Perceived Likelihood of Achievement using the template.** This is how strongly the customer believes your Offer will work for someone like them. To improve this score, add better proof, relevant case studies, clearer deliverables, a believable mechanism, and answers to every major objection.
4. **Score Time to First Value using the template.** This is how quickly the customer receives their first meaningful result or benefit. To improve this score, simplify onboarding, standardize fulfillment, and give the customer useful Quick Wins early.
5. **Score Customer Effort and Sacrifice using the template.** This is how much work the customer must complete to receive the value. To improve this score, reduce meetings, decisions, information requests, onboarding time, and required implementation.
6. **Add up all 4 scores for a total out of 20 and compare it to the scoring scale to see how your Offer ranks.** Scores run from 4-7 Terrible up through 19-20 Exceptional.
7. **Find the category with your lowest score and improve that category first.** This is the highest-leverage place to focus your improvements. Use the specific improvement tip for that category before touching anything else.
8. **Score your Offer again and repeat until every category scores at least 4 stars.** Re-score all four categories after making your changes, not just the one you improved. Keep cycling through this process until nothing is dragging your Offer down.
