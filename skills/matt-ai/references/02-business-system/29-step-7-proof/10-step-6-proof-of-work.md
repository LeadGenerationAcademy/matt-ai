Even more so than all the logos, testimonials, screenshots, and case studies, the number one form of proof is proof of work. If you have a portfolio and a visual service, your ad creatives, your email flows, and the websites you made are all examples of proof of work.

There are certain services where this isn't possible, but proof of work with a named client, a named business, and a named type, especially a before-versus-after if that's possible, is the most powerful form of proof there is.

Get a portfolio, put it on your website, put it in a document, and have it ready to send at a moment's notice.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Identify the visual or tangible work you've made for real clients.** This is your ad creatives, email flows, websites, or anything else people can actually look at, and it's stronger proof than anyone talking about the work. Even more so than logos, testimonials, screenshots and case studies, this is the number one form of proof. There are certain services where this isn't possible, so only use what your service actually produces.
2. **Create a master [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com/drive/home) folder called "Proof Of Work," with a subfolder inside it for each category of visual work your service provides.** This becomes the single place everything lives instead of scattered across your computer or someone's inbox. Splitting it up by category makes it usable instead of one giant dump of files nobody can navigate.
3. **Place each piece of work into its matching subfolder.** Everything should have an obvious home the moment you finish a new piece of work.
4. **Attach a named client, a named business and a named type of work to each piece.** An anonymous piece of work loses most of its power, and naming who it's for makes it real. The business gives the work context and makes it verifiable. The type of work means someone immediately understands what they're looking at without guessing.
5. **Create a before-versus-after version wherever possible.** This is the single strongest format proof of work can take, so use it every time the work allows for it.
6. **Put the portfolio into a standalone document and keep it ready to send at a moment's notice.** This gives you something you can send directly instead of pointing someone back to your Drive folder. If it takes you time to pull this together when someone asks, you've already lost momentum you didn't need to lose.
7. **Place the portfolio on your website.** This is where most people will look for it first.

### Completion Audit

- Did you identify the visual or tangible work you've made for real clients?
- Did you create a master Google Drive folder called "Proof Of Work," with a subfolder inside it for each category of visual work your service provides?
- Is each piece of work placed in its matching subfolder?
- Does each piece have a named client, a named business and a named type of work attached?
- Did you create a before-versus-after version wherever possible?
- Is the portfolio available as a standalone document, ready to send at a moment's notice?
- Is the portfolio placed on your website?
