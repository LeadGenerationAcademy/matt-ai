It can be really good, especially in certain markets, to pay for PR, or to do PR, which stands for public relations. It's all of those things, like when you see people featured in Forbes or [Entrepreneur.com](http://Entrepreneur.com), like Entrepreneur of the Year.

The dirty little secret is that probably 75% to 80% of the time, people actually pay for that. You pay to get on Forbes 30 Under 30, for example. You don't get paid, and you don't get randomly selected. A lot of the time, you have to apply.

This is worth it, especially as you scale to a more normie audience. They've been very impressed by this stuff, and it's very good to stack your Google search results with these. If you have extra money to spend, take a small percentage of your ad budget and put it towards PR. A lot of the time it doesn't cost more than $2,500, $5,000, or $10,000.

Start stuffing your search results with people talking about you. These are usually high-domain-authority websites. They'll drown out any negative stuff you have, if you have any, and it just looks really strong.

You can also use these logos on your website and say "As Featured In," then link to the actual article. That's very powerful. A lot of people say "As Featured In" and it's just dead links you can't find anything behind, which is sketchy.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Identify PR outlets relevant to your industry, such as Forbes or [Entrepreneur.com](http://Entrepreneur.com).** Not every outlet carries the same weight with your audience, so start with the ones that would actually impress the people you're trying to reach. This matters even more as you scale to a more normie audience, since they're very impressed by this stuff.
2. **Research whether each outlet requires an application, a payment, or both.** Most of these placements are paid or applied for, not earned by being randomly discovered, so know what you're actually getting into before you commit.
3. **Set aside a small percentage of your ad budget for PR.** Most placements run somewhere between $2,500 and $10,000, with $10,000 already being a lot, so this doesn't need to eat a large chunk of what you're already spending.
4. **Apply or pay for the placement and get featured in the outlet.** Follow whatever process that specific outlet requires. The actual piece of coverage is what everything else on this page depends on.
5. **Add the outlet's logo to an "As Featured In" section on your website, link it directly to the live article, and confirm the link works.** This is what turns a single article into an ongoing piece of credibility for anyone visiting your site. Anyone who says "As Featured In" without a real link behind it looks sketchy, and a dead link defeats the entire purpose of putting this proof on your site.
6. **Repeat for enough outlets to stack your Google search results.** These are usually high-domain-authority websites. The more of these you accumulate, the more they drown out anything negative and the stronger your overall search presence looks.
