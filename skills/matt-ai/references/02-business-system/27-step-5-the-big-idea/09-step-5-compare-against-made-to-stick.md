Made to Stick by Chip Heath and Dan Heath explains the six qualities that make an idea memorable: Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, and Stories.

We will score each of our three remaining Big Ideas against all six qualities.

Every quality receives an individual score out of five. The six scores are then added together for a Total Score out of 30.

However, the Total Score does not override a fatal weakness. A score of one or two in any individual category kills the Big Idea.

### Framework & Criteria

Each Big Idea receives an individual score for all six criteria:

1. Simple
2. Unexpected
3. Concrete
4. Credible
5. Emotional
6. Stories

Each criterion is worth five points.

**Maximum Total Score: 30/30**

A score of 1/5 or 2/5 in any individual criterion is fatal, regardless of the Total Score.

#### Criterion #1: Simple

Can the Big Idea be immediately understood and repeated in one sentence?

It should communicate one core belief rather than several competing messages.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** Immediately understood and accurately repeated after one exposure.
⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** Clear and repeatable with minor simplification.
⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** Understandable, but contains multiple messages.
⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** Requires substantial explanation.
⭐ **1 Star:** There is no identifiable core idea.

**Simple Score:** ___ / 5

#### Criterion #2: Unexpected

Does the Big Idea challenge an existing belief or introduce a surprising opportunity?

It should be different enough to get attention without becoming unbelievable.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** Completely changes how the ICP understands the problem while remaining believable.
⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** Presents a strong and distinct perspective.
⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** Uses a familiar idea with a slightly different angle.
⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** Generic and predictable.
⭐ **1 Star:** Indistinguishable from everyone else's marketing.

**Unexpected Score:** ___ / 5

#### Criterion #3: Concrete

Can the ICP clearly picture what the Big Idea means?

Use specific people, problems, outcomes, mechanisms, numbers, and examples instead of vague language.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** Extremely specific and easy to visualize.
⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** Mostly specific with very little abstraction.
⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** Understandable, but still contains vague language.
⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** Mostly abstract.
⭐ **1 Star:** Built entirely from meaningless claims or buzzwords.

**Concrete Score:** ___ / 5

#### Criterion #4: Credible

Does the ICP have a reason to believe the Big Idea?

Use the proof, demonstrations, case studies, specific examples, firsthand experience, and believable mechanisms you can think of right now.

We will build the complete Proof System later in the Proof Section. For now, score the Big Idea using the proof you already know exists.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** Supported by overwhelming relevant proof and is easy to believe or verify.
⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** Has strong proof and a believable explanation.
⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** Sounds plausible and has some support.
⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** Possible, but mostly unsupported.
⭐ **1 Star:** Unbelievable or contradicted by the available evidence.

**Credible Score:** ___ / 5

#### Criterion #5: Emotional

Does the Big Idea make the ICP care?

It should connect to something personally important, including money, time, freedom, status, security, identity, fear, or ambition.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** Creates an immediate and powerful emotional response.
⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** Connects strongly to something the ICP cares about.
⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** Valuable, but primarily logical.
⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** Creates very little emotional response.
⭐ **1 Star:** Gives the ICP no reason to care.

**Emotional Score:** ___ / 5

#### Criterion #6: Stories

Can the Big Idea be demonstrated through real stories?

Use the founder stories, customer transformations, and specific before-and-after examples you can think of right now.

We will organize the complete collection later in the Proof Section. For now, score the Big Idea using the stories you already know exist.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** Reinforced through several compelling and specific stories.
⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** Supported by multiple relevant stories.
⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** Supported by at least one clear story.
⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** Only supported by vague anecdotes.
⭐ **1 Star:** Never demonstrated through a story.

**Stories Score:** ___ / 5

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Go to Tab 5 of your Big Idea Template.** Three audit blocks are already built, one for each of your final three.
2. **Copy the three Big Ideas that survived Step #4 into the Big Idea field of each audit block.** Audit all three separately. Do not combine them, compare them vaguely, or give the group one shared score.
3. **Fill in the Simple Score field for each Big Idea.** Determine whether the ICP could immediately understand and repeat the Big Idea after seeing it once. If it requires substantial explanation or contains multiple competing messages, score it accordingly.
4. **Fill in the Unexpected Score field for each Big Idea.** Determine whether it changes how the ICP understands the problem or presents a genuinely distinct perspective. Being clear is not enough if everyone else says the same thing.
5. **Fill in the Concrete Score field for each Big Idea.** Determine whether the ICP can clearly picture what the idea means. Specific people, problems, outcomes, mechanisms, numbers, and examples are concrete. Vague claims and corporate buzzwords are not.
6. **Fill in the Credible Score field for each Big Idea using the proof you can think of right now.** Think through the proof, demonstrations, case studies, examples, experience, and mechanisms you already have. Do not invent proof. We will build the full Proof System later, but the Big Idea still needs a legitimate reason to be believed now.
7. **Fill in the Emotional Score field for each Big Idea.** Determine whether it connects to something the ICP genuinely cares about, including money, time, freedom, status, security, identity, fear, or ambition. An idea can be logical and still be too emotionally flat to matter.
8. **Fill in the Stories Score field for each Big Idea using the stories you can think of right now.** Think through the founder stories, customer transformations, and before-and-after examples already available. Do not invent stories. We will organize all of them properly in the Proof Section later.
9. **Add the six individual scores and fill in the Total Score field.** Every individual score stays on the tab right next to the total. A Total Score by itself can hide a fatal weakness, which is why the individual ratings matter just as much as the total.
10. **Mark Yes or No for Does Any Criterion Score 1/5 or 2/5.** This is a mechanical check. Run down the six scores and answer honestly.
11. **Mark Yes or No for Fatal Weakness.** Any idea carrying a one or a two in a single criterion has a fatal weakness, no matter what the Total Score says.
12. **Fill in the Notes field for each Big Idea.** Write down what made the idea strong or weak while the scoring is still fresh. Step #7 asks you to compare these ideas against each other, and by then you will not remember your reasoning.
13. **Remove every Big Idea that received a score of one or two in any individual criterion.** A high Total Score does not save it. If the idea scores well in five categories but fails one completely, it is still dead.
14. **Write every surviving Big Idea into the Ideas Moving To Tab 6 list.** If none survive, return to the earlier steps and create stronger ideas. Do not lower the scoring standard because your original three failed. The point is to find a Big Idea strong enough to build the entire business around.
