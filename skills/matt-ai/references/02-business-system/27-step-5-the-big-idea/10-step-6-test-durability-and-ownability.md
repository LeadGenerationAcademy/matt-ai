# Step #6: Test Durability & Ownability

Course: Step #5: The Big Idea
System: Business System
Skool link: https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/278a13f9?md=5299a5590c7948728e8f904a8e1156ac

---
A Big Idea is not useful if it works today but becomes irrelevant next year.

It also does not matter how good the idea is if another company already owns it in the mind of your ICP or if you are unwilling to repeat it long enough to become associated with it.

Owning a Big Idea takes years of repetition. You need to test whether the idea will survive, whether you have a realistic opportunity to own it, and whether you are actually willing to commit to it.

Willingness to Commit deserves extra attention because that part is based entirely on you. Durability and Ownability can be researched. Only you know whether you are genuinely willing to build your business around an idea and repeat it for years.

### Framework & Criteria

Score every surviving Big Idea across three criteria:

1. Durability
2. Ownability
3. Willingness to Commit

Each criterion is worth five points.

**Maximum Total Score: 15/15**

A score of 1/5 or 2/5 in any individual criterion is fatal, regardless of the Total Score.

#### Criterion #1: Durability

Will this Big Idea still be relevant in five years?

It should be broad enough to support years of marketing without depending on a temporary trend, platform, technology, or market condition.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** The underlying belief will remain relevant for at least five years and can support years of marketing.
⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** The belief should remain relevant for at least five years with only minor changes.
⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** The underlying problem will remain, but the idea may require meaningful changes over time.
⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** The idea depends heavily on a temporary trend, platform, technology, or market condition.
⭐ **1 Star:** The idea is short-lived and will probably become irrelevant before the business can own it.

**Durability Score:** ___ / 5

#### Criterion #2: Ownability

How much competition exists for this Big Idea?

The idea should be distinct enough that your business can become associated with it in the mind of your ICP.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** No serious competitor owns the idea, and there is a clear opportunity for your business to become strongly associated with it.
⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** Some competitors use related ideas, but yours remains distinct and realistically ownable.
⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** Several competitors use similar ideas, but no business clearly owns the position.
⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** The idea is crowded, generic, and easily interchangeable with competitors.
⭐ **1 Star:** The idea is already strongly owned by another business or could be claimed by almost anyone.

**Ownability Score:** ___ / 5

#### Criterion #3: Willingness to Commit

Are you willing to repeat this Big Idea for years?

This criterion deserves extra attention because it is based entirely on you.

Durability and Ownability can be researched. Your Willingness to Commit cannot.

A Big Idea can be durable, distinct, and completely available to own, but none of that matters if you become bored, stop repeating it, or replace it six months from now.

Do not give yourself a high score because the idea looks good on paper. Give yourself a high score only if you are genuinely willing to build the business around it and repeat it through your Offer, messaging, ads, content, funnel, proof, and sales process for years.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **5 Stars:** You are completely willing to build the business around this idea and repeat it for at least five years.
⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4 Stars:** You are strongly committed to the idea with only minor reservations.
⭐⭐⭐ **3 Stars:** You are willing to use it, but you are not completely certain you want the business associated with it long term.
⭐⭐ **2 Stars:** You are already reluctant and will probably replace or abandon it before the market associates it with you.
⭐ **1 Star:** You are unwilling to build the business around the idea or repeat it consistently.

**Willingness to Commit Score:** ___ / 5

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Go to Tab 6 of your Big Idea Template.** Three audit blocks are already built, the same shape as Tab 5 with three criteria instead of six.
2. **Copy every Big Idea that survived Step #5 into the Big Idea field of each audit block.** Only test the ideas that passed every Made to Stick criterion without receiving a fatal score.
3. **Fill in the Durability Score field for each Big Idea.** Ask whether the underlying belief will still matter five years from now. Remove ideas that depend on temporary trends, platforms, technologies, or market conditions that may disappear before you have time to own them.
4. **Research what your competitors repeatedly communicate through their Offers, websites, ads, content, and funnels.** You cannot score Ownability from memory. Go and look at what the market is already hearing from everybody else before you decide whether a position is still open.
5. **Fill in the Ownability Score field for each Big Idea.** Determine whether another business already owns the idea, whether the market is crowded with similar claims, or whether a clear position remains available.
6. **Fill in the Willingness to Commit Score field for each Big Idea.** This score is completely dependent on you, so it requires more honesty than the others. Do not give an idea five stars because it sounds intelligent or looks good on paper. Give it five stars only if you are willing to repeat it for years without becoming bored, changing direction, or replacing it with the next idea that catches your attention.
7. **Add the three individual scores and fill in the Total Score field.** All three individual scores stay on the tab right next to the total. A strong Total Score cannot hide a fatal weakness in Durability, Ownability, or Willingness to Commit.
8. **Mark Yes or No for Does Any Criterion Score 1/5 or 2/5.** Run down the three scores and answer honestly.
9. **Mark Yes or No for Fatal Weakness.** A one or a two in a single criterion is fatal here the same way it was on Tab 5.
10. **Fill in the Notes field for each Big Idea.** Step #7 asks you for the Strengths and Weaknesses of every remaining idea. This is where that material comes from.
11. **Remove every Big Idea that received a score of one or two in any individual criterion.** Do not keep a temporary idea because it is currently exciting. Do not keep an idea another company already owns. Do not keep an idea you already know you will abandon.
12. **Write every surviving Big Idea into the Ideas Moving To Tab 7 list.** Do not choose the winner yet. Step #7 is where the remaining candidates will be compared and the final Big Idea will be selected.
