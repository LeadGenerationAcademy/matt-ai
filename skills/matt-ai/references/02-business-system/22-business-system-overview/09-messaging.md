Messaging is where all of this theoretical work becomes something people actually see.

Your industry, ICP, offer, and big idea can all be correct, but if you communicate them badly, your marketing can still fail.

Messaging determines who pays attention, who clicks, who applies, and who becomes a client. If your messaging speaks to beginners, you are going to attract beginners, even if you intended to sell to larger businesses.

We need to describe our ICP’s problems, circumstances, and desired outcomes in the exact way they describe them.

It is not enough to identify that someone’s problem is “getting clients through paid ads.” They might describe it as, “No matter how many landing pages I try, no one books a call.”

That is why we do actual research. Sales call recordings are one of the best sources. Reviews, emails, comments, forums, and customer conversations can also show us what people care about and how they say it.

For years, almost all of my auto insurance ads were some version of “save money on auto insurance.” When I researched customer reviews, price was not even in the top 15 things mentioned.

People were talking about being ghosted when they needed to make a claim, being lowballed, and paying premiums for years only to get screwed over when they finally needed help.

Those became much stronger messaging angles.

We should usually start with a direct offer: tell people what we sell, who it is for, show proof, and tell them what to do next.

Over time, we expand into more problems, circumstances, and outcomes. Some angles will be broad enough to support substantial ad spend. Others will be very specific and reach people the broader angles miss.

This also helps us stay in front of people who fit our ICP but are not currently shopping. They see us describing their world over time, so when they are ready to buy, they already know who they want to call.
