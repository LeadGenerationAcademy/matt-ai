Your offer is what you sell. In this context, we are talking about the front-end offer you sell to cold traffic: people who do not already know, like, and trust you.

It is the thing you put in your ads and sell through outreach. It might be your main service, or it might lead into a larger recurring service.

If we can sell an offer profitably to cold traffic, our business is much more in our own hands. We are no longer dependent on referrals, relationships, or someone consuming 50 hours of content first.

For someone to buy, three things must happen:

1. They must understand what your offer is.
2. They must believe it is a solution or potential solution to their problem.
3. They must believe you or your company can do it.

One of the best positions to be in is selling something people must buy, whether legally, functionally, or competitively. The more they already understand what it is and why they need it, the more time you can spend showing why they should buy it from you.

The offer should create obvious value by making them more money, saving time, reducing costs, reducing risk, or increasing speed.

Then we use the value formula to improve it:

- Desired result: How badly does your specific ICP want the result?
- Perceived likelihood of achievement: Do they believe it will work for them?
- Time delay: How quickly do they receive their first meaningful value?
- Effort and sacrifice: How much work do they have to do to get the result?

Your unique mechanism is simply the believable explanation for why your offer should produce the result. Its job is to make a reasonable person nod and think, “That makes sense.”

The offer also needs to be good for your own business. It should be standardized, repeatable, profitable to deliver, and built around a problem people will still need solved in the future.

Whatever makes the offer valuable needs to be clear before the sales call. The deadliest objections are the ones that stop people from booking in the first place.
