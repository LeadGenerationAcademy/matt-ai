ICP stands for ideal client profile or ideal customer profile. Some people call it an avatar.

Your industry is the overall market you serve. Your ICP is the person you sell to inside that industry.

Roofing is an industry. The head of marketing at a multi-state roofing company with a call center is an ICP. That is very different from a solo owner-operator roofer with a couple of helpers.

Everything we do with the offer, big idea, and messaging depends on this distinction.

To identify your ICP, look at your past customers and find two to five shared characteristics that roughly 80% or more have in common. These might include location, job title, revenue, employee count, or other characteristics specific to your business.

Then identify the problems they face, the circumstances they find themselves in, and the outcomes they want.

An agency owner trying to get their first client has very different problems from an agency owner doing $3 million per year. The larger owner might want to get out of sales, add a second acquisition channel, or scale their ads without their acquisition cost skyrocketing.

If you confuse those two levels of customer, your offer and messaging will speak to the wrong person.

As you collect more proof and get better results, you should also refine your ICP and move upmarket. In many service businesses, you scale by selling essentially the same thing to larger, wealthier clients who can pay more.
