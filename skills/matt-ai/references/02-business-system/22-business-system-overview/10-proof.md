The quantity, intensity, relevance, and type of proof all contribute to how likely someone thinks you are to get the result for them.

Buyers usually choose the option that feels safest. This becomes especially important as you move upmarket, where the buyer is increasingly an employee instead of the founder. They have a reputation and a job to protect.

There are four tiers of proof.

Language is the first. Understanding your ICP is proof. If you know their problems, circumstances, desired outcomes, and objections, and describe them in the exact language they use, you show that you have been there before. You understand their situation.

Content is the second. Great content shows that you know what you are talking about. If someone takes an idea from your content, applies it, and gets a result before paying you, they now have personal proof that your ideas work. Full courses, in-depth audits, case studies, and complete walkthroughs of you doing the work can all do this.

Evidence is the third. This is what people normally think of as proof: screenshots, testimonials, case studies, reviews, and results. We want quantity, diversity, and intensity. Show results for customers like your ICP, with enough context for someone to understand what happened and why it matters. Organizing evidence around specific objections makes it more useful.

Time in business is the fourth. A long, continuous operating history makes people feel safer. You earn this by staying in business and continuing to serve customers successfully. Every time you switch your offer or business, some of that starts over.

All four answer different questions in the customer’s mind: Do you understand me? Do you know what you are talking about? Have you done this before? Are you established and safe?

As you grow, keep collecting proof, taking language from sales calls, and turning objections into better marketing. The same offer should become easier to sell as your results and reputation accumulate.
