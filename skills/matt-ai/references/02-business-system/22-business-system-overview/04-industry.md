Your industry is the overall market you serve. Roofing, insurance, and residential HVAC are examples.

Your industry matters so much because it determines the potential of your business. The same offer, the same skill set, and the same team can get dramatically better results in a better industry.

Imagine you run a marketing agency that generates leads through Facebook ads. You build the funnel, do the appointment setting, and the client closes the deals.

Whether you do that for wealth managers or dog walkers, the process is basically the same. But who is going to pay you more? The value of a new customer is completely different for those two businesses.

We also need to distinguish a real industry from what I call a non-industry.

E-commerce, SaaS, agencies, coaches, consultants, and “small businesses” are common examples of non-industries. They seem like industries, but the businesses inside them can have very little in common.

If you find a winning ad for a collagen brand, you can’t simply recreate it for an apparel brand and expect the same results. Different products, branding, customers, price points, and value propositions make the work custom every time.

With a roofing agency, a winning ad, landing page, or appointment-setting message can be reused across similar clients. Your learnings and processes compound.

That is the primary reason we choose an industry: the scalability of fulfillment. We want delivery to become faster, more consistent, and more profitable as we get more customers.

When evaluating an industry, we look at how valuable a customer is to the businesses we serve, how easy those businesses are to reach, the size of the qualified market, the pain they have, their spending power, and whether the market is growing, stable, or declining.
