# Tier #1: Collecting Less Than 1X

Course: Step #4: Unit Economics
System: Business System
Skool link: https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/a8dd5ad9?md=e129eec103b64effa5b66518defa9c38

---
You are in Tier 1 when the cash collected from a sale does not cover the cost of acquiring and fulfilling for that customer.

For example:

- Cash collected: $2,000.
- Acquisition and fulfillment costs: $2,500.
- $2,000 ÷ $2,500 = 0.8X.

You are $500 short. That money has to come from somewhere.

You might use your savings, money from other customers, or credit. Whatever you use, you are funding part of every new sale yourself.

This is where getting more customers can become a problem. If every new customer leaves you with a $500 gap, another 100 customers means another $50,000 you need to find.

You can be very good at getting customers and still run out of cash.

Now, this does not necessarily mean the customer will be unprofitable over the entire relationship. They might have more payments coming. They might buy your recurring service later.

But you still have to pay for acquiring and serving them while you wait for that money.

This is why we keep coming back to cash collected. Someone agreeing to pay you $10,000 over the next year does not give you $10,000 to work with today.

A lot of people will start here. Your first challenge is getting someone to buy your offer at all. You will probably have delivery processes that need work, limited proof, and a sales process you are still figuring out.

That is fine as a starting point. We need to improve it.

Your immediate goal is to reach 1X: collect enough from the sale to cover the full acquisition and fulfillment costs.

That could mean collecting more upfront, improving the offer so you can charge more, lowering the cost of acquiring a customer, or making fulfillment more efficient.

Look at your actual numbers. Find out why the sale leaves you short and work on that.
