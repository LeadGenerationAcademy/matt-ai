# Step #3: Increase Price

Course: Step #4: Unit Economics
System: Business System

We are going to use a simple rule to test how much you can charge.

Raise your price by 20% every four new customers while your close rate is above 20%.

When it falls below 20%, hold the price and work on getting better at selling the offer. Once your close rate is back above 20%, continue.

##### The Process

1. Sell your offer to four new customers at your current price.
2. If your close rate is above 20%, increase the price by 20% for new customers.
3. Keep doing this until your close rate falls below 20%.
4. Hold the price there and improve your sales process, proof, and marketing.
5. Once your close rate is above 20% again, resume the increases after every four new customers.

At exactly 20%, hold the price and keep improving.

Track your close rate at the current price using completed, qualified sales calls. Give yourself enough calls to see what is happening. One person saying no does not mean you have found your price ceiling.

##### When Your Close Rate Drops

Your first response should be to find out why people are not buying.

- Review your lost sales calls and write down the objections.
- Practice answering the objections you struggle with.
- Collect more customer results and testimonials.
- Show proof from customers similar to the prospect.
- Make the value of the offer easier to understand.
- Answer common concerns in your VSL, sales page, and pre-call content.
- Improve your follow-up with people who are still considering it.
- Check whether you are attracting people who actually fit the offer.

Work on these at the new price. You are trying to become capable of consistently selling it there.

##### Four Increases More Than Double Your Price

Each increase is 20% of your current price.

If you start at $5,000:

- Starting price: $5,000.
- First increase: $6,000.
- Second increase: $7,200.
- Third increase: $8,640.
- Fourth increase: $10,368.

That is more than double your starting price after four increases.

This also gives you a way to gradually work through your beliefs about what you can charge.

You might currently think nobody would pay you $10,000. Going from $5,000 to $6,000 is a much smaller thing to test. As customers buy and you deliver results, you get evidence that you can charge more.

The next increase becomes easier to make because you have already done it before.

Keep the offer itself consistent while you test. You do not need to add another deliverable every time you raise the price.

If the work stays the same, your fulfillment cost can stay roughly the same while your revenue per customer increases. Watch acquisition costs too, because a lower close rate can make each customer more expensive to acquire.

As your results and proof improve, you can also become a credible option for larger customers with bigger budgets. Those customers still need to fit your service.

The extra margin gives you more room to hire good people and improve delivery. Customers can receive a better service, and you can build a more profitable business without constantly adding more work.
