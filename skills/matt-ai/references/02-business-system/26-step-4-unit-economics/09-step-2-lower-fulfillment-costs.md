# Step #2: Lower Fulfillment Costs

Course: Step #4: Unit Economics
System: Business System

Here is a list of things you could try to reduce your fulfillment costs.

##### Technology

- Automate repeated admin, such as creating client folders, assigning tasks, and sending updates.
- Connect your tools so nobody has to copy the same information between them.
- Use AI to help with repeated research, drafting, and analysis.
- Automate reports you currently build by hand.
- Remove software you no longer use or that duplicates another tool.
- Compare the cost of a tool with the time it actually saves your team.

##### Labor

- Move basic admin away from people whose skills are needed for more difficult work.
- Consider overseas hires for work that can be delivered remotely.
- Use contractors for specialist work you do not need often enough for a full-time role.
- Hire people who can complete the work with less supervision and fewer corrections.
- Look at the total cost of getting the job finished. Include time spent managing and fixing it.
- Find out what your team spends time on before deciding you need another hire.

##### SOPs, Processes And Training

- Write step-by-step instructions for work you repeat.
- Record yourself completing the task so someone else can learn it.
- Include an example of what the finished work should look like.
- Collect the information you need from customers upfront so your team spends less time chasing it.
- Make it clear who owns each task and what happens when it is finished.
- Use checklists to catch common mistakes before work reaches the customer.
- Turn questions you keep answering into training material.
- Update the process whenever you find a faster or better way to do something.
- Train the team on mistakes that keep causing work to be redone.

##### Templates And Reusable Assets

- Turn your best work into templates the team can reuse.
- Build a library of sections, designs, scripts, or other components you repeatedly create.
- Create standard responses to common customer questions.
- Keep templates and assets somewhere everyone can easily find them.
- Improve the shared version when you make something better so future customers benefit too.

##### The Offer Itself

- Standardize your front-end offer so you can deliver it through the same process each time.
- Clearly define what is included so extra work does not keep getting added.
- Ask customers which deliverables they actually use and value.
- Remove unnecessary deliverables from future versions of the offer.
- Sell custom work separately when it falls outside the standard offer.
- Focus on customers you can consistently get results for without rebuilding your service around each one.
