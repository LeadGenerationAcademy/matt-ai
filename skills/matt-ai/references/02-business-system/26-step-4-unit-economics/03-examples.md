### Calculating Tier

Say we sold qualified, exclusive leads for $100 each, and each lead cost us $25 in ad spend.

It would cost us $2,000 to acquire the client, and we would allow another $500 in labor for their first order, including setup and CRM integration.

If they bought 100 leads and paid upfront, the numbers would look like this:

Cash collected: $10,000.
Ad spend to generate their 100 leads: $2,500.
Labor: $500.
Cost of acquiring the client: $2,000.
Total acquisition and fulfillment costs: $5,000.

$10,000 ÷ $5,000 = 2X.

That would put us in Tier 3: Collecting 2X Or More, with $5,000 left before our other business expenses.

We would start covering these costs at 34 leads. To hit 2X on the first purchase, we would need them to buy 100 leads.

Our gross margin would be 70% after the lead generation costs and labor. So we would pass the 2X check, but still sit below the 80% gross margin target.

A note on cash collected. A $5,000 offer on a 12-month payment plan does not mean we collected $5,000. We collected one payment.

### Tier #1: Collecting Less Than 1X

Say our acquisition and fulfillment costs were $2,500 per client.

Anything we collected below $2,500 would put us here. We would be funding the difference ourselves.

Most people in Tier 1 are not running an unprofitable offer. They are running a profitable offer and just not collecting enough cash.

Say it cost us $5,000 to acquire and fulfill for a customer worth $30,000, and that $30,000 came in as 12 payments of $2,500.

That is a very profitable customer. We would still be collecting $2,500 against $5,000 in costs on the day we sold them.

### Tier #2: Breaking Even And Working Toward 2X

Say our acquisition and fulfillment costs were $2,500 and we collected exactly $2,500.

The sale covers the customer. $2,500 in, $2,500 out. In theory we could do that forever.

The problem is every dollar of it is already spoken for. There would be nothing left to go get the next customer with, so we would have to float that money for however long our sales cycle runs before we could spend it again.

Floating $10,000 or $25,000 is one thing. Floating a million is another.

As we collect more than $2,500, we start keeping cash after the customer is paid for. That is the move from breaking even toward 2X.

### Tier #3: Collecting 2X Or More

Say our acquisition and fulfillment costs were $5,000 per client and we collected $10,000 upfront.

$5,000 covers what we spent on ads, commission, tech and labor for this customer.

The other $5,000 goes straight into acquiring and fulfilling for the next one.

That next customer collects $10,000 too. $5,000 covers them, $5,000 goes toward the one after that.

It becomes a chain. We would no longer be waiting on anything to go get the next client.

This is the target.

### Lowering Acquisition Costs

If it cost us $2,000 to acquire a client, here is where that money would go.

At a 20% close rate, one customer would take 5 completed sales calls.

At a 50% show rate, those 5 completed calls would take 10 booked calls.

$2,000 ÷ 10 booked calls = $200 per booked call.

Shows would be the phase holding us back, so that is where we work. We send reminders before the appointment, use the thank-you page to explain what happens on the call, and personally contact prospects to confirm they are attending.

Say the show rate moved from 50% to 70%. Those same 5 completed calls would now only take 7.1 booked calls.

7.1 booked calls × $200 = $1,429 to acquire the client.

Our total acquisition and fulfillment costs would fall from $5,000 to $4,429, and we would move from 2X to 2.26X.

### Increasing Price

Each increase is 20% of your current price.

If you start at $5,000:

Starting price: $5,000.
First increase: $6,000.
Second increase: $7,200.
Third increase: $8,640.
Fourth increase: $10,368.

That is more than double your starting price after four increases.
