One of the biggest problems in marketing is a lack of understanding of how a funnel works. This is especially true when people run paid ads for the first time.

In that specific example, paid ads are only one phase: getting clicks. People will obsess or be terrified because they don’t know how to make a good ad creative, when getting someone to click is only the first of four things that need to happen.

You still need them to book a call, show up, and buy.

Someone can click on your ad, watch your video, and be interested in what you do without ever becoming a customer. There is quite a lot that happens between those things, and that is what this diagram shows.

We are going to break it into four phases so you can understand what each part is doing and where you need to improve.

##### Phase 1: Clicks

This is your paid ads and content. Someone sees something you put out, becomes interested, and clicks through.

In this funnel, the paid ads send people to your sales page. The content sends people to an opt-in page where they can get your lead magnet.

Obviously, who clicks matters. We want people who could actually buy what we sell. An ad that gets a lot of attention from people who will never become customers is not doing much for us.

![image](https://assets.skool.com/f/1d97a8218f0840a8a65c7970ccdf40ce/ca8a2436ac5c413585bc5495fe11086344d07f02ea384f218e48295fcc084b37-md.png)

##### Phase 2: Calls

Once someone clicks, we need to give them enough information and enough reason to book a call.

Your sales page and VSL explain what you do. Someone should be able to understand what you can help them achieve, how it works, and why they would want your help.

If they came through your content to get a lead magnet, they receive that resource and enter your welcome flow. This gives you a way to keep communicating with them. They might have only just found you, so they may need to learn more before they are interested in speaking with you.

You also have your application form. This is where you find out whether the person is qualified for your offer before putting them on your calendar.

If they are qualified, they can book. If they are not, you may have a downsell that makes sense for them.

Everything here contributes to getting calls booked with people you can actually help and sell to.

![image](https://assets.skool.com/f/1d97a8218f0840a8a65c7970ccdf40ce/7e0c68152b00453286c9f1a402c9ee99669be92c280b4fbc89a70da8dd2b167b-md.png)

##### Phase 3: Shows

A booked call does not mean someone will show up. They can forget, get busy, or lose interest between booking and the meeting.

This is why we have the thank-you page, breakout videos, pre-call flow, selfie video, retargeting ads, and manual reach-out.

Some of this reminds them about the appointment. Some of it explains more about what you do and answers questions they probably already have. A personal video or message also gives them some contact with the person they are going to speak to.

We want them to remember the call and still want to have it. Ideally, they have watched the material and understand what we are offering, so we can spend the conversation talking about their situation.

![image](https://assets.skool.com/f/1d97a8218f0840a8a65c7970ccdf40ce/b03bb28260b24db9b69453596fd022281cc13079a52743c0bd6b5b17a4fb4d8b-md.png)

##### Phase 4: Closes

Now you actually have the sales call. You find out more about their situation, determine whether you can help, explain the offer, and try to make the sale.

Some people will buy on that call. Others will need another conversation or have something they need answered first. That is where booking the next meeting, the follow-up call, and the follow-up flow come in.

The follow-up should continue whatever you discussed. If they needed a specific piece of information, send it. If you agreed to speak again, book that meeting.

Once they buy, they move into onboarding and you begin delivering what you sold them.
