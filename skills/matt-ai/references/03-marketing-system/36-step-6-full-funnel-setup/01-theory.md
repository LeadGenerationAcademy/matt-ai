The goal here is to build a funnel from scratch.

Build it with the mindset that this is the funnel you are going to run paid ads to. Paid ads are the hardest form of traffic to convert. If the funnel works for paid ads, it will work for content, organic, referrals and networking. The opposite is not true.

Even if you already have a funnel, cross-reference it against everything here and make sure it is the best it can possibly be.

Offers, paid ads, content and sales each have their own courses. Here we are only building the funnel, and it has four phases:

1. Clicks
2. Calls
3. Shows
4. Closes

If any phase falls apart, you do not get the client.

### Your Core Seven Come First

Success with a funnel, and with the ads and content running to it, cannot be separated from your business fundamentals. I call these the Core Seven:

- **Industry:** The market you serve, which affects your earning potential and your ability to scale fulfillment.
- **ICP:** The specific type of customer you want to attract, sell to and serve.
- **Offer:** What you sell to someone coming from cold traffic.
- **Unit Economics:** What you collect per customer compared with what it costs to acquire and serve them.
- **Big Idea:** The central idea that makes your offer make sense and become desirable.
- **Messaging:** How you communicate through your ICP's problems, circumstances and desired outcomes.
- **Proof:** The evidence that makes people believe you can deliver the result.

None of these are good or bad in isolation. They are only good or bad as they relate to each other, especially your ICP.

If I had to put a number on it, your Core Seven are about 80% of your success and your funnel is about 20%.

People spend hours fucking around with their funnel when they are in a terrible industry, their ICP sucks, their offer sucks or their economics make no sense.

### Why Your Ads "Don't Work"

Think about this. Your funnel is about 20% of your success. Clicks, which is what your ads are responsible for, are one of four phases in that funnel, so roughly 25% of it.

20% x 25% = 5%.

People are dumbfounded that their ads do not work, when you could make a real argument that the actual ad represents about 5% of your total success.

### Congruency

Every piece of the funnel should continue the same idea the person just saw.

One example: a static image ad, the headline above the fold of the sales page and the first 15 seconds of the VSL all carry the same message. The VSL opens with, "All these financial advisors you see on screen are in different states, have different teams, and have different amounts of AUM. But they all have one thing in common: they bought leads from us."

### Perception Is Reality

It does not matter what your actual offer is if you do not showcase it properly to the people who have not bought it yet.

You need to show it. Explicitly detail every part of your offer and why it is valuable to them, in your ads, landing page, VSL and sales process.

You can have the absolute best offer in the world. If you do not communicate every piece of it, it may as well not exist.

I see people all the time who have a pretty good offer, but none of their marketing details it. That is the same as their offer being terrible.

### Phase #1: Clicks

This phase is about getting the right people interested and sending them into the funnel. The pieces are your paid ads, your content, your Instagram profile, your Facebook page, your other profiles and your search results.

### People Check You Out Before They Act

So many people will see your ad and then click on your profile to see what they can find. If that profile is blank or dead, that obviously does not make a good first impression.

If the profile is well done and full of useful content, it builds a bit of trust and makes them more likely to take the action you want. Posting content consistently WILL make your ads perform better for this reason.

You would be amazed how many people see your ad, never touch your profile, back out completely and go straight to Google. Then they click on their preferred social media platform and continue the funnel from there.

### Instagram And Facebook Get The Most Effort

These are the profiles your ads actually deliver from, so they deserve the most attention.

Your Instagram needs:

- A professional profile picture
- A blue checkmark
- A high follower count
- An impressive above the fold
- A DM word people can use to work with you
- Highlights with testimonials and results, professionally designed
- Three pinned posts with professional cover photos

Your Facebook page needs:

- A professional profile picture
- A blue checkmark
- A professional cover photo
- A bio that makes it immediately clear what you do
- Instagram linked
- A call to action in the description, the call-to-action button and the links section

Set up LinkedIn, YouTube and Twitter as well, just in case. Keep them consistent with the business and offer people have already seen.

### Your Search Results

It is amazing how many people will see your ad, back out, open a new tab and search you on Google.

Both your company and you need to show up, and hopefully favorably. This matters even more if you are running ads from your own named profile instead of a company profile. Bad search results will crush you. No results are not good either.

We need to do a few things:

- Sign up to high domain authority websites for your company
- Post FAQ videos and testimonials on your social media profiles
- Have some version of your own name as a live website, such as [matthewblarsen.com](http://matthewblarsen.com)
- Show up on one or more third-party review sites

This WILL make a noticeable impact on the performance of your ads. It helps whether they search you right after seeing the ad or later in their research, which also affects shows and closes.

### Where Your Traffic Goes

Paid ads from Facebook, YouTube, Google or anywhere else will usually go directly to your sales page. Some people send them to an opt-in page first. Both can work, and the tradeoff is explained in the next phase.

Your content will usually carry two links:

- A link to your offer on your sales page
- A link to your opt-in page, so they can get your lead magnet and join your email list

### Phase #2: Calls

This phase turns interest into qualified booked calls.

### Opt-In Page Or Straight To The Sales Page

The funnel opt-in page is an optional page between your ad and your sales page. Someone clicks the ad, enters their name, email and phone number, then continues to the sales page.

I typically prefer sending paid traffic directly to the sales page. The higher level the ICP, the stronger that preference becomes. These people want to see what you do and decide whether it is relevant to them. Making them submit their information before they understand the offer is one more thing to do, and some of them simply will not do it.

The benefit is real, though. Someone can look at your sales page and leave because they got distracted or had an unanswered question. With their information, you can send the welcome flow and follow up by email, text or phone.

The tradeoff is that you lose some people before they ever reach the sales page. The opt-in rate on its own does not tell you whether the page is helping. We ultimately want qualified calls and customers.

As a rough rule of thumb, if the cost per booked call increases by around 25% or less, I will often consider that worth it because of the follow-up opportunity. Much more than that becomes harder to justify. Their information only has value if you actually use it and it produces something.

If you use one, keep it straightforward. Match the branding to the account they clicked from. Call out the ICP, give a clear result in the headline, explain how they get it in the subheadline and show relevant proof. Make it clear what submitting gives them access to, then send them directly to the next step.

### The Lead Magnet Opt-In Page

This is where someone exchanges their contact information for your free resource. Most of these people come from your content. They watched something useful, heard you mention the resource and clicked because they want it.

The promise here is different from your sales page. Someone may want to learn from you without being ready to speak with you. We give them a useful way into the funnel at that stage.

The headline should explain the result or problem the resource helps with. The subheadline makes it concrete. Calling it a free guide does very little on its own.

Who opts in matters. A resource that appeals to everyone collects information from people who will never buy. It should attract your ICP and "un-attract" everyone else. The problem it solves should also make sense alongside the problem your paid offer solves.

Keep it congruent with the content. If your video mentions a specific resource, the page should offer that resource with the same name and promise.

My own lead magnet opt-in page has a star rating, an ICP callout, a headline, a subheadline and a form. It has converted at 73% with YouTube as the primary traffic source.

Once they submit, deliver the resource immediately and start the welcome flow.

### The Lead Magnet

Your lead magnet should help your actual ICP solve a problem and give them a useful sample of how you think.

I like laying it out in terms of the steps or parts it takes to be successful. If I made a YouTube lead magnet, the parts would be the idea, title, thumbnail, 30-second intro, speaking on camera, making the slides, making the script, editing, lighting and audio, uploading, analytics and how to improve.

Follow the VAR formula:

- **Valuable:** Someone could reasonably feel it was worth paying $100 for.
- **Actionable:** They can use something inside it within 24 hours to make a concrete improvement.
- **Readable:** One idea and one relevant image per slide.

Within each part, use theory, examples and action steps. Explain the idea, show what it looks like and tell them how to apply it.

The most useful outcome is that they apply something and get a result. Now they have personal proof that your ideas work, which is a completely different level of confidence from you claiming you know what you are doing.

This same material can also become the basis of your long-form course.

### The Welcome Flow

This is the automated email sequence someone receives after opting in. It delivers the resource, introduces your company, explains what you do and gives them a reason to book a call.

Someone downloading your lead magnet does not necessarily understand your offer. The welcome flow needs to make the connection clear, or you end up with plenty of people learning from you who never consider becoming customers.

The framework is four emails in the first three hours:

1. Deliver the resource immediately.
2. After one hour, explain what you do.
3. After two hours, explain how you do it.
4. After three hours, make a direct offer with a clear call to action.

Order matters. They need to understand what you can help them accomplish before your process means anything to them.

Pull the FAQs and objections from your actual sales conversations. Do not cram every FAQ into four emails. Choose the ones that most affect whether a new lead understands the offer and wants help, and support each explanation with proof.

None of it matters if the emails never reach the inbox or never get opened. Deliverability, subject lines and preview text deserve serious attention.

### Above The Fold

This is the part of your sales page someone sees on their phone without scrolling. It is one of the most important parts of the entire funnel, because it is often their first proper look at your business.

You cannot assume they will scroll down. Some people decide based entirely on what they see here.

Start with congruency. The name and branding at the top should match the account they clicked from. The ICP callout, headline and subheadline continue the promise from the ad.

The headline needs to communicate a result they want in language they understand. Write for the person arriving with very little context.

The subheadline explains how they get the result or makes the offer more specific. The star rating gives an initial indication that other people have worked with you.

The VSL and the CTA button both need to be above the fold on a phone. A person already interested can start the application. Someone who needs more can watch or keep reading.

### The VSL

Someone coming from a paid ad may have never heard of you. The video needs to give them a reason to pay attention, explain what you do and answer the questions that would stop them from booking.

You cannot assume they will stay until the end:

- Show proof in the first five seconds
- Explain the full offer within the first 15 seconds
- Show a second form of proof within that opening

After the opening, work through the FAQs and objections in order of importance, and support each answer with proof relevant to that particular question. The length and bluntness of the VSL depend on your ICP.

The thumbnail matters too. A strong explanation cannot help someone who never presses play.

### The Application

This is the form someone completes before they can access your calendar. You want people on the calendar who have a problem you can solve and are in a position to buy.

The most important decision is what counts as qualified, and it comes from your ICP, offer and economics.

Use at least four disqualification questions:

- **Budget:** Whether the financial side could make sense.
- **Authority:** Whether they can make the decision.
- **Need:** Whether their problem fits what you do.
- **Start date:** Whether the timing is suitable.

Decide which answers qualify before anyone goes through the form. You do not want every closer inventing a different definition of qualified after looking at the same application.

Add at least four questions that help the sales process, about their current situation, desired result and why they are looking for help.

Collect their name, email, phone number and website, and make it available to whoever takes the call. It looks unprofessional when a prospect explains their situation and then gets asked to repeat everything because nobody read the form.

Qualified applicants continue to the booking page.

### The Rest Of The Sales Page

The rest of the page needs to give someone everything they need to book a call without watching one second of the VSL. If an important explanation only exists inside the video, anyone who skips it never gets it.

There are plenty of formats. A sales letter, a breakdown of how it works, before-and-after examples, a timeline or several together. The requirement is that they understand what you do, what they get and why they should believe it will help them.

Be explicit about every part of the offer that makes it valuable. Put proof where it supports what you just said: logos, case studies, testimonials, videos and screenshots. Order the page by the questions someone needs answered.

Go through the whole page with the video ignored. Anything essential that disappears needs to be on the page too.

### The DQ Pathway

If someone does not qualify on your application, disqualify them. Either show them an end screen that says sorry, or send them to a DQ or downsell offer. The downsell is optional.

### The Call Booking Page

This should be a straightforward step, but a calendar with poor availability loses people who were otherwise ready to book.

The framework:

- As many available slots as possible
- Appointments available as soon as possible
- A booking window of no more than 72 hours, or seven days at massive scale

Availability has two parts. There needs to be a time that fits their schedule, and it needs to be soon enough. Shortening the window does not help if there is almost nothing available inside it. Look at what a person applying today would actually see.

The gap before the call matters because people get busy, forget or lose interest.

### Application Recovery

This is for people who complete a qualified application but never book. A surprising number of businesses do absolutely nothing with them.

They may have been interrupted, found no suitable time or had a question first. You do not know until you reach them.

The welcome flow handles the automated nurture. We also want a manual process: a manual SMS each day and a manual double call each day while they remain in recovery. Reference their application, ask whether they have questions and include the booking link.

Speed matters, and someone needs to own it. If the same problem keeps coming up, use it to fix the funnel. Repeated confusion points to the sales page. Repeated scheduling problems point to the calendar.

### What To Track In Phase #2

- **Lead magnet opt-in rate:** 50%+ of visitors to the lead magnet opt-in page, for organic and content traffic.
- **Application rate:** 6–10% of sales page visitors submit a completed application.
- **Booked call rate:** 3–5% of sales page visitors complete a booking.
- **Qualified rate:** 90%+ of booked calls genuinely meet your qualification criteria.

Compare traffic sources separately and keep each definition consistent. Also track the percentage of qualified applicants who finish booking, which shows how much you lose between the application and the calendar.

### Phase #3: Shows

A booked call does not mean they will show up. This phase keeps them interested between booking and the appointment so they actually attend, ideally with their biggest questions already answered.

### The Thank You Page

This is the page someone sees immediately after booking.

The headline confirms their call is booked. The branding matches every page before it. Then tell them what to do next:

- Watch the main video, which answers their most important questions
- Look for your text and reply to it, which confirms their number is real and lets you gauge their intentions
- Accept the calendar invitation, because it does not always go to their inbox
- Scroll down and watch the FAQ videos

Those FAQ videos cover the biggest questions and objections from your sales process, most important first and clearly labeled.

### Pre-Call Reminders

These are the automated reminders from Calendly or whatever booking software you use. Turn on email and SMS reminders for 24 hours, four hours and one hour before the call.

Someone can be interested and still forget a call they booked three days ago. The reminders should make it obvious who the appointment is with, when it starts and how to join.

Check that the date, time, timezone and joining link are right, and that a moved appointment updates the reminders. Make a test booking, then let the software handle it.

### The Pre-Call Flow

This is the automated email sequence between booking and the call. I want to force-feed them information before the call.

The framework:

- Find your top 18 FAQs and objections from actual sales conversations
- Put them in order of importance
- Write a useful, direct email for each, with proof and a link to an asset that explains it further
- Send the first when they book, then one every four hours while the call is still ahead
- Send no more than 18 emails across the 72 hours
- Stop the sequence when the call starts

The order matters a lot. Someone who books for later today might see one or two emails. If the answer that most affects whether they believe the offer is buried at the end, most people never see it.

The flow also needs to respond to the appointment. A moved call should not keep the old schedule. A canceled call is no longer upcoming. A missed call moves them into the no-show process.

### The Selfie Video

This is a short, natural video from the person the prospect is going to speak with. It puts a face to the name before the appointment.

Keep it to 60 seconds or less and film it like a normal video message. If a closer takes the call, use that closer. Mine sounds like this:

"Hey there, it is Matthew Larsen from Lead Generation Academy. Saw you booked a call with me. I am just reaching out to put a face to a name, and to let you know I am excited for our call. I sent you a link to check out between now and then. I'll talk to you soon, and feel free to text me at this number if you have any questions in the meantime."

Send it with the appointment-setting reach-out so they can reply in the same conversation.

### The Appointment-Setting Reach-Out

This is the personal contact you make after someone books, usually by text or phone. It turns a booking on the calendar into an actual conversation.

Reach out within five minutes of the booking. At that point they were just looking at your offer and deciding to speak with you. Someone needs to be responsible for this so it does not depend on whoever notices first.

Put everything in the first text: your name, your company, the call they booked and their options. The best message I have found is this:

"Hey (Name). It is (Your Name) from (Your Company). I saw you booked a call. We have three options:

- We can do the call at the time you booked
- I can phone you in (insert time)
- We can text back and forth here

Let me know which works best

(Your Name)"

The earlier time needs to be real, with someone ready to take it. Call twice in succession as part of the reach-out. Once they reply, respond to what they said and update the appointment.

### The Loom And One-Pager

After someone books, send a one-pager explaining the offer and a short Loom walking through it.

The one-pager explains the offer, how it works, the proof and the next step. Someone should be able to glance at it on their phone and understand the basic idea. Try to fit the entire sales page onto it and the main explanation gets lost.

The Loom walks through the same material in more depth, three to twelve minutes. Explain why the steps are there, how they connect and what the proof shows. Reading every word of the one-pager out loud adds very little. Send both links together and say what each one is.

### Retargeting Ads

These are ads shown to people who have already interacted with your business. In this phase, they answer questions, show proof and keep the offer in front of people before the call.

Build a list of up to 100 FAQs and make short videos answering them. Do as many as you can support with real questions, but do not randomly make shit up to reach a number.

Put the question at the top of the video and answer it directly in 60 seconds or less.

Match the ad to the stage the viewer is in. A message that assumes they have an upcoming appointment should only go to people for whom that is actually true.

### The No-Show Flow

This is the email sequence someone enters after they miss their call.

First, make sure they actually missed it. Someone who moved the meeting, canceled or had the conversation another way should never get an email saying they failed to attend.

The content can reuse many of the questions and proof from the pre-call flow, but the job has changed. Now you are giving them a reason to come back and making it easy to book again.

The framework is 24 emails across eight weeks:

- One email per day for the first two weeks
- Three per week in weeks three and four
- One per week in weeks five through eight

Put the answers most likely to restore their interest first. Reference the missed call for context, then give them a fresh reason to book. Once they rebook, stop the sequence, and track whether they attend the new appointment.

### What To Track In Phase #3

- **Speed to lead:** Under five minutes from booking to the first personal reach-out.
- **Show rate:** 50–70%.

Calculate show rate as attended calls divided by attended calls plus confirmed no-shows, for appointments whose time has passed. Keep cancellations and reschedules separate and leave out future bookings. Also track whether people who rebook after a no-show actually attend.

### Phase #4: Closes

The person has shown up. This phase turns that conversation into a customer and makes sure people who do not buy yet still have a path back.

### Pre-Call Research

Spend five to fifteen minutes reading their application, reviewing previous conversations and looking through their website and profiles.

Do not make someone repeat information they have already given your team. It looks SO unprofessional when the prospect did the work of explaining themselves and the person on the call clearly has not read it.

Write down two or three observations about their business that are relevant to your offer, and treat them as things to investigate. Choose proof that fits the person, which is much easier when you have a bank of examples ready. Then work out which questions still need answering.

Put it in a short brief the closer can actually read. You can automate most of the gathering, but the closer still needs to read it. The bar here is set very low. A few minutes of real preparation shows you care enough to understand their business before selling to them.

#### The Sales Call Plan

This is the repeatable process you follow during the call: the introduction, questions, demonstration, objections, close and what happens if they do not buy.

It does not have to be a strict script. As the founder, you may feel you can do this without a plan. That becomes much harder to reproduce when someone else takes the calls. You will get nowhere if every closer does whatever they want.

The framework:

1. A 30-second highlight reel introduction that gives them a reason to take the conversation seriously
2. Questions that build on the application and research
3. A demonstration that uses the presentation and proof relevant to what they told you
4. Handling the objections that remain
5. Several prepared ways to ask for the sale

If they are ready, move into signing and payment. If another conversation is needed, book it while you are together. Send the agreed follow-up within two minutes.

### The Sales Call Presentation

The plan is how you run the conversation. The presentation is the material you use inside it to explain the offer.

It should answer the who, what, when, where, why and how as quickly as possible. Start with the objective in plain language. Explain how the problem is currently being handled and where that falls short. Then explain what is different about your approach and why you expect it to work, with steps and relevant proof.

Address your top six to twelve objections where the answers make sense. A concern about timing belongs with the timeline.

Be clear about the cost, risks and expected timing. Explain the mid-term and final milestones so they know what to expect as the work happens.

### The Follow-Up Flow

This is what someone receives after a sales call if they have not bought.

The primary decision is not whether you will follow up. You must. The decision is whether your team is genuinely going to do it manually or whether you need an automated flow to make sure it happens.

Manual follow-up lets you use the actual conversation. Send the case study that answers their concern and follow through on what you agreed.

The automated flow starts with strong case studies and an industry insight, then addresses the biggest FAQs, then continues with case studies and insights. It does not complete your personal commitments for you. If you promised to send something, send it. When someone replies, respond. If they buy or ask you to stop, end the follow-up.

### One-Pager, Contract And Payment

Once someone decides to move forward, make it as easy as possible to sign and pay. Have the one-pager, contract template and payment link or invoice ready before the call.

The one-pager explains what they are buying and what happens after they pay. Include a timeline for the first 90 days, shortened for an intro offer, with milestones at 24 hours, 48 hours, seven days and beyond. Every milestone needs to be something you can genuinely deliver.

Check that the service, price and timing in the contract and payment request match what you agreed.

Put all three into one message, send it by email and text, and label each link. Once the purchase is complete, move them into onboarding.

### The Winback Flows

The 90-day, 180-day and 365-day winback flows go to people who booked a call but have not bought. The timing is measured from their booking date.

Someone who was not ready three months ago may be in a different situation now.

All three use the same structure. Reintroduce the company, reintroduce yourself, make a direct offer with a clear call to action, then follow with case studies and insights. Adjust the wording for how much time has passed and use your current proof.

Check their status first. Anyone who bought is excluded. Anyone already speaking with your team should not get a message reintroducing the company. Once they rebook, stop the winback messages.

### What To Track In Phase #4

- **Close rate:** 15–30%.
- **Revenue per lead:** Revenue from a group of leads divided by the number of leads in that group.
- **Revenue per sales-call prospect:** Revenue from prospects who attended a sales call divided by the number of those prospects.

For close rate, divide buyers by unique prospects who attended an initial sales call. Count a prospect once even with several follow-up calls, and track first-call closes and later closes separately.

A higher close rate is not automatically better if the revenue those prospects produce falls.

### Before You Send Traffic

Your funnel has four jobs. Get the right people to click, get qualified people to book, get them to show up and help them become customers.

Work through the Completion Checklist and build each part. If you already have a funnel, compare it against this and fill the gaps.

Before sending traffic, go through the entire process yourself. Submit an application, book a call, check the messages and follow the links. Test what happens when someone does not book, misses the call, rebooks or buys.

Once it is live, track what happens in each phase. Find where you are losing people, make one specific improvement and measure the result. Keep using what prospects ask and tell you to improve the funnel.
