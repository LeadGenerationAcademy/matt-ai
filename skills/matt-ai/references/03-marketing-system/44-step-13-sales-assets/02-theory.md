Before someone buys from you, there is always a thought: okay, I see he has done it for X business, but will it work for me?

Great content is proof that you actually know what you are talking about.

If someone takes an idea from your content, applies it and gets a result, they now have personal proof that your ideas work. This is much better than you claiming or harping that they have to do X, Y and Z.

### Three Monster Content Assets

There are three of them worth making:

- A full course.
- An in-depth audit or case study.
- A complete walkthrough of you doing the work.

Make them extremely deep. Four hours, eight hours, whatever.

The point is not the length. The point is that you know enough to talk intelligently about your craft without hiding behind a teleprompter and editing.

Anyone can script five minutes.

### Proof Has To Match The Level Of Customer You Want

Case studies are especially important because the proof needs to match the level of customer you are trying to attract.

Different levels of customers have different problems before they work with you, and they should have different outcomes afterward.

A case study designed to resonate with a smaller, less sophisticated company will not necessarily prove anything to a much larger company with completely different problems.

The case study needs to show the prospect that you have already worked with someone like them, understood the problems they had, and helped them move from the relevant before state to the relevant after state.

There is no bigger status symbol than crushing every objection before the prospect even has time to bring it up. At a minimum, every one of your six to 12 core objections should have a case study showing how you overcame that problem for someone.

### Showing The Work Instead Of The Result

For the HVAC agency example, that meant recording client audits and walking through campaigns so people could see how we do the work.

The audit is going through their ad library and auditing their ads frame by frame. The other shape is how I would make ads for X brand, where you build it out yourself instead of reviewing what they already have.

Both are content that demonstrates your expertise rather than how-to videos.

### They Do Not Want To Do It Themselves

People don't even want to do it themselves, or usually even know how. They just want to know that you know how. If you can convince them of that, they will hire you.

A full course is what convinces them. It lets them spend hours with you, hear your voice and see your face. You'd be amazed at what percentage of people will watch this video who ultimately learned about you through something like paid ads before they buy.

It is a great form of social proof to have a long multi-hour course.

You can use this to train your employees and recruit too. People who watch this video will view you as an expert and be bought into your philosophy.

Eventually you would want about four of these that rank in search and get views forever. You will do one first.

### Your Free Stuff Is What They Judge You On

Before anyone buys from you, they have no idea how good or bad your service is. All they have to judge you on is your free stuff. If your free stuff is bad, or you have none at all, they assume your service is bad. If it's great, they assume your service is great.

So your Lead Magnet isn't a box to tick. It's one of your products, and they're paying in time instead of money. It's the information-only side of your core service, and if they like what they see, they're more likely to come and ask about the paid version.

Which is why it has to be your Magnum Opus. Every piece, part and step of what you do, in extreme detail, with nothing held back.

Every individual idea inside it runs through the VAR Rule:

1. **Valuable:** Even though it is free, would you pay $100 for it? If you have to think about it, then no.
2. **Actionable:** Is there a clear action they could take within the next 24 hours that would help their business?
3. **Readable:** One idea per slide, one image per idea, one example per idea, and a summary checklist of action steps.

This also doubles as internal training, so put a lot of effort into it.

Eventually you would want other lead magnets too, and those can be smaller. Anywhere from a single-pager template to a 30 to 50 page workbook. All are good, but we ultimately want them to get the master lead magnet too. Think of the smaller ones as activation points. We get them to do something that brings them closer to buying from us. They do something, and it works. That is a great form of proof.

The course is the video version of this same lead magnet.

### These Assets Do Not Bring In New Traffic On Their Own

In the finance agency, the business owner made 16 YouTube videos in the entire history of the business. Those videos barely get any top-of-funnel traffic or new viewers on their own. But when the call-booking form asks whether they have watched any of the YouTube videos, 68% say yes.

The number of people who come through paid ads or other content or outreach, then happen to stumble across the channel and watch before booking a call, is huge.

This is a prime example of what it means when the four methods of driving traffic, paid ads, content, outreach and partnerships, work together to make each other more effective.

It is also media leverage. You record the video once and thousands of people see it. You don't have to get on a Zoom call one at a time and show everyone.
