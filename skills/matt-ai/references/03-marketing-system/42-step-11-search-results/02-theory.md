It is insane how many people see your cold email, paid ad, content, or whatever else, do not click or respond, and immediately search for you on Google.

If they find bad information or nothing at all, they will probably assume you are a scam.

You need to show up as much as possible when someone searches for both your personal name and your company name.

Sometimes they will search for you as the founder.

Sometimes they will search for the company.

You need to show up for both.

People should also be able to enter your funnel through paid ads, outreach, content, partnerships, or any other source, leave the funnel to research you, and then continue through the same funnel from whatever website or platform they find.

Everything should be extremely unified.

### Example

Someone sees your Facebook ad but does not immediately take action.

They search your name on Google and find your LinkedIn profile.

They click the CTA on your LinkedIn profile and return to the exact same funnel promoted in the original Facebook ad.

The messaging, proof, Offer, and next step should all remain consistent.

Regardless of what someone searches or where they find you, they should always be able to continue through the same funnel.

### Claiming Profiles On Established Websites

You and your company need to appear when someone searches either name. Not showing up at all makes you look new, unknown, or possibly not even real.

The fastest way to fix that is claiming profiles on the established websites that already rank well in Google, the general ones and the ones specific to your industry.

Use the same name, company name, description, website, address, and phone number everywhere. Inconsistent profiles cause more confusion than having no profile at all.

Add proof wherever the platform allows it, what you sell, testimonials, case studies, photos, answers to common questions.

If you're scared to put yourself out there and ask for honest reviews, that's usually because you already know what you're selling isn't good enough. Fix what you're selling. Don't avoid the reviews.

### Getting Proof on the Review Sites

Third-party review sites give prospects independent proof, something that isn't just you talking about yourself.

Claim your profile on the big ones, Clutch, UpCity, GoodFirms, Trustpilot, and fill in the same identity information you used everywhere else, your services, industries, pricing, case studies.

Don't collect five reviews and call it done. Build review requests into your actual process, project completion, customer success, account management, offboarding, so every customer gets asked at a natural point of satisfaction.

Respond to every review, the good ones and the bad ones. That's what shows a prospect you're actually paying attention to your customers.

### Owning Your Own Name

You should own a domain with some version of your own name on it. This protects your name and gives you a website nobody else controls.

Build it out with your real bio, a video of you actually introducing yourself, testimonials, case studies, media appearances, and a way to contact you.

Then go check your work. Search your own name in an incognito browser, and search the variations too, Reviews, Company, Industry, Scam. Whatever comes back is what a prospect sees before they ever talk to you.

### Confirming Your Website Actually Works For Search

Most of the work to establish your company in search already happened back in the profile steps. This step is just making sure the actual website is doing its job.

Go through your homepage, About page, service pages, product pages, case studies, and contact page. Confirm the title, description, and headline all make sense, and screenshot it for AI to review if you want a second opinion. You decide what actually gets changed, not the AI.

Get Search Console and Analytics connected, submit your sitemap, and confirm Google can actually read it. Then search your company name and make sure your real site, title, and description show up clean, with nothing broken or outdated sitting underneath it.

### Building Your Own Audience

People connect with people more than they connect with companies. That's why your primary content goes out through your own personal profiles, not just the company's.

Claim your name on YouTube, TikTok, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, X, and set up your own newsletter. Keep your name, headshot, bio, and contact info consistent across every single one.

This is where your videos, your opinions, your frameworks, your case studies, and your newsletter actually live. Your company can change or get sold. Your name and the audience built around it stays yours.

### The Company Still Needs Its Own Profiles

The company still needs its own profiles, but they're not doing the same job as your personal ones.

When someone searches your company and clicks through, they should immediately find proof or get an answer, not just a logo and a tagline.

Claim the company's YouTube, TikTok, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, and X, set up its own newsletter, and use that space for FAQs, objections, testimonials, case studies, interviews, and demonstrations of how the product actually works.
