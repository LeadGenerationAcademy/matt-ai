We have to build every page in the funnel. Each one is a different part of it, and every page is one step. We really can't have any missing.

### Funnel Top Five Principles

1. **Start With the Offer:** Your offer determines most of the result. The funnel helps you capture the rest.
2. **Use the Right Structure:** The funnel must make sense for the ICP, offer, price, and sales process.
3. **Create Immediate Clarity:** The ICP, offer, promised result, and next step should always be obvious.
4. **Remove Friction:** Every unnecessary step, question, or decision gives the prospect another opportunity to leave.
5. **Maintain Congruency:** The ad, sales page, VSL, application, booking page, and thank-you page should feel like one continuous conversation.

### How Traffic Enters The Funnel

Where someone lands first depends on where they came from. If they're coming from content, YouTube, Instagram, or whatever the source, they usually go to your opt-in page first, and your opt-in confirmation page is what explains the offer to them.

Most of the time, when you're using paid ads, you'd run to the sales page directly instead. I typically don't like to run to an opt-in, but it's something you can measure.

### The Opt-In Pages

Your opt-in page is where a lead lands after they click a link to get your lead magnet. It should be very simple: you capture their contact info, and they get your lead magnet in return.

The opt-in confirmation page is the page the lead gets redirected to after they opt in. It should confirm their lead magnet is on the way, then push them to open it and book a call for your main service.

### The Sales Page

The above-the-fold section is very important because that's the first impression, and we need to make a good one. If you look at a heat map on your page, using something like Microsoft Clarity or Hotjar, you'd quickly learn that 70% of people don't scroll past the above-the-fold section at all.

Match this section to two things: how high level your client is, and what type of offer you're selling. Higher level clients and DFY offers should keep it short. Lower level clients, DWY offers, and courses can run longer.

Always start short so people move on to your VSL, but add a full sales letter here too if you're selling DFY to a higher level audience, they're too busy to sit through a video.

The rest of the sales page needs to explain how the offer works. Build it with the understanding that they shouldn't have to watch even one second of your VSL.

Not everybody likes the same things, and not everybody consumes in the same way, so we need to explain everything in the VSL in different design sections on the page.

Finally, on the sales page, we want to build an overwhelming, non-stop wall of social proof in different ways, logos, video testimonials, written testimonials, case studies, and reviews, so the result feels achievable and they book a call.

### The Application And Booking

Clicking the call-to-action button on the sales page sends them to an application. It has at least three questions that disqualify unqualified people.

We don't want unqualified people to be able to book a call for two reasons. It's a waste of time. And if a lot of the calls are coming from paid ads and we let unqualified people book anyway, the conversion event will still fire, that clogs our Facebook pixel up with bad data, and Facebook ends up optimizing for unqualified leads.

After the application happens, they'll go to the call booked page. You can have this page if you want, or you could either embed Calendly in the application form or go to the Calendly link directly. It's up to you.

The call booked confirmation page, or the thank-you page, either name is fine, is where they go after they book. This is where you tell them the next steps, get them to accept the invitation, answer their top FAQs, and show more proof. Preferably different, but the same proof is fine.

### The DQ Page (Optional)

The disqualification page is where unqualified leads go. If you have a downsell or cross-sell, that's where it goes here. A lot of people will run an automated webinar on this page that sells a course. If you don't have that, it's up to you, you're not expected to make that.

Every page has its own purpose, and every page is one step in the funnel. Every single page is important, and we really can't have any missing.
