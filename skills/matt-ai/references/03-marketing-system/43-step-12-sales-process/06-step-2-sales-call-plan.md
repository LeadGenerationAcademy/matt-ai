You don't need a sales script, but you do need a plan: a standardized series of steps you take on every call.

As the founder taking every call yourself, you can often get away with winging it because you carry all the knowledge and authority.

Once you hire your first sales rep, that's no longer true. You need a set system to plug them into.

### Framework & Criteria

- Highlight Reel Introduction (30 seconds)
- Transition
- Five Questions
- Transition
- Demo
- Transition
- Objection Handling
- Transition
- 3+ Ways To Close
- If No Close, Book Next Meeting
- Follow Up Within 2 Minutes

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Make A Copy Of The [Sales Call Plan Template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1SoluV8ZhrksZTlpNAfY1qYEensGSo5UJPfbD_-xEWlE/edit?tab=t.rgv24l4amc17).** This becomes your working document across all five tabs, plus your consolidated final plan. If you already have your own version you like, use it, but this template is what I always start with. [in this skill: assets/templates/sales-call-plan-template/]
2. **Read The Instructions And Matt's Example On The Intro Tab.** The goal is a 30-second highlight reel that takes command of the call right away, builds authority, then transitions into asking questions, since whoever is asking the questions is the one in control.
3. **Write Your Intro.** End it by asking what an ideal outcome for the meeting would look like, the same way my example does, and a lot of the time they'll just tell you straight. Write it for yourself first. Once you hire sales reps, rewrite it to transfer as much of your authority to them as possible, like how long they've worked with you and the results they've gotten under you.
4. **Read The Instructions And Matt's Example On The Five Questions Tab.** The goal is to flip the script so the prospect is qualifying themselves to you, by naming a disqualification rate upfront (my example says "not a good fit for everyone, I turn away about 60%") before asking the questions. Then ask if you can ask a few questions to see how they compare to your most successful clients.
5. **Use The Five Questions Exactly As Written.** Don't write your own. These five are the same on every call, and they're already in the template. Every answer gives you information while the prospect sells themselves further. If they have an issue with the investment range in the fifth question, the call is over, though your application should already be screening for budget.
6. **Read The Instructions And Matt's Example On The Demo Tab.** This should run 5-7 minutes of a 30-minute call, prebuilt as slides or a Miro board, and should preemptively overcome objections so you don't have to handle them later. Write down everything you need to cover to stay consistent across calls. A simple who, what, when, where, why and how works fine, since your pre-call flows and retargeting ads will have already covered a lot of it.
7. **Write Your Demo.** End with a transition question asking if this is what they expected and how they'd rate it from 1-10. If they say 7, ask what they like about it, then ask how you could get that 7 to a 9. Their answer is extremely valuable to you, and they convince themselves even further by giving it.
8. **Read The Instructions And Matt's Example On The Objection Handling Tab.** List the 6-12 objections you hear repeatedly, then list three ways to overcome each one, using metaphors, examples, or another approach. With only one answer, it gets awkward the moment a prospect voices the objection differently than your answer expects.
9. **Write Your Objection Handling.** For a price objection, a metaphor could be "Nobody complains about handing over $20 if they know they're getting $100 back," followed by asking what's making them unsure about the return. These written answers are what you drill your sales reps on. Doing this repeatedly across real calls is what eventually makes overcoming these objections feel effortless.
10. **Read The Instructions And Matt's Example On The Closing/Next Steps Tab.** After handling objections, give a clear closing message covering the ROI timeline, what access they get, how to pay, what to do before onboarding, and what happens on the onboarding call. If they won't pay on the call or within five minutes after, book another meeting instead of letting it go cold.
11. **Write Your Closing/Next Steps.** Ask for the close at the first lull once objections are handled. Recap the call, tell them exactly what happens next and where you'll send the payment link and contract, and cover the five quick wins they'll get during onboarding. If you're not sure what to write, just tailor my example plan to your own business.
12. **Copy Each Of Your Written Sections Into The Your Full Sales Call Plan Tab.** This consolidates all five pieces into one page so the entire plan can be reviewed or trained on at a glance. When everyone follows the same plan, you can compare your reps apples to apples and see who's actually good at sales. If a rep closes at an abnormal rate, review their call recordings and add what's working to the plan. This plan has worked for me and 75 of our sales reps.
