### Pre-Call

Everything starts with your CRM.

Set it up with nine deal stages, from Application Submitted through Call Booked, Call Shown, Offer Sent, and on to Closed Won, Closed Lost, Unqualified, and No Show.

Add five statuses on top of that, tracking where someone sits overall, from Opt In up through Active Client or Churned Client.

That's the whole skeleton your sales process runs on, and it doesn't need to be more complicated than that.

The problem is that CRM compliance falls apart the moment you're not the one running every call anymore. Reps get busy and updating the CRM is the first thing they skip.

So you hire a CRM Champion, a cheap VA whose only job is checking in with your sales team on whether the CRM actually got updated.

Then you need a plan for the call itself, the same standardized steps every rep runs every time.

When you're the one on every call, you can get away with winging it because you're carrying the authority yourself. A rep doesn't have that, so they need a sales call plan to follow instead.

### On-Call

This is the same process, now actually being run live.

The Intro builds your authority fast, then hands control of the call to you by moving straight into the Five Questions.

The Demo runs five to seven minutes, built specifically to answer objections before the prospect ever brings them up.

Whatever objections still come up get handled with the answers you already prepared ahead of time.

Closing happens at the end of the call, and it's not soft. Cover the ROI timeline, what access they get, how to pay, and what onboarding looks like.

If they won't pay on the call or within five minutes after, you book another meeting instead of letting it die.

### Post-Call

The moment someone closes, speed matters more than anything else.

Your One Pager, Contract, and Payment Link need to go out within five minutes, wherever you are.

I was once ready to pay someone thirty thousand dollars for this very module and didn't get the payment link until the next afternoon. That's money you already earned, sitting there because someone was slow.

Once they pay, onboarding should kick in automatically.

One email a day for the first seven days, then three a week for three more weeks, walking them through the service.

Alongside that, give them five quick wins they can knock out in the first twenty-four hours, so they're not sitting around with buyer's remorse before your service has had the chance to prove itself.
