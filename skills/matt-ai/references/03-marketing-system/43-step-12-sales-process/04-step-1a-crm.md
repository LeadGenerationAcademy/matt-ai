### Framework & Criteria

1. **Deal Stages:** Set up your CRM with the following deal stages:
  1. Application Submitted
  2. Call Booked
  3. Call Shown
  4. Offer Sent
  5. Call Completed
  6. Closed Won
  7. Closed Lost
  8. Unqualified
  9. No Show
2. **Statuses:** Set up your CRM with the following statuses:
  1. Opt In
  2. Prospect
  3. Active Client
  4. Churned Client
  5. Unqualified

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Choose A CRM.** Any CRM can be good. They're all functionally the same, so decide based on preference and cost. I usually use Close because it's good for the price and I like how it's set up, but eventually you'll probably move to a high-powered all-in-one like HubSpot. When you're first starting out and taking all the calls yourself, a spreadsheet works fine.
2. **Add Application Submitted As A Deal Stage.** Deal stages track the deals in your actual pipeline, and this one marks someone who has submitted an application but hasn't booked a call yet. Keep it separate from your opt-ins. An opt-in is usually just someone trying to get your lead magnet, while an application means they've shown real intent. Anyone who submits an application should have this deal stage created for them automatically.
3. **Add Call Booked As A Deal Stage.** This marks someone who has booked a Sales Call. When they book, your CRM should automatically move them from Application Submitted to Call Booked.
4. **Add Call Shown As A Deal Stage.** This separates someone who actually attended the call from someone who booked and never showed up. There's no good way to automate stages like this one, so you or your sales reps will have to update them by hand. Once you have more than three people taking calls, hire a CRM champion to keep this clean, so nobody slips through the cracks and your forecasting stays accurate.
5. **Add Offer Sent As A Deal Stage.** This marks the point where you've pitched the offer. It matters most on a multi-call close, where the offer usually gets presented on the second call, but you can also use it on a first call once you've done everything you need to.
6. **Add Call Completed As A Deal Stage.** This marks a call that happened and finished, whether or not they bought.
7. **Add Closed Won As A Deal Stage.** This marks someone who bought and is now a paying client.
8. **Add Closed Lost As A Deal Stage.** This marks someone who isn't a client and obviously isn't going to buy, like someone who told you outright that this isn't for them.
9. **Add Unqualified As A Deal Stage.** This marks someone who didn't meet the criteria to move forward in your Sales Process.
10. **Add No Show As A Deal Stage.** This marks someone who booked a call but didn't attend it.
11. **Add Opt In As A Status.** Statuses give you the context on where each person stands, and everyone in your CRM gets one. Opt In covers the people who grabbed your lead magnet or ended up on your email list some other way, before they've become an active Prospect.
12. **Add Prospect As A Status.** This covers anyone actively moving through your Sales Process. Once someone becomes a Prospect, they also get a deal stage.
13. **Add Active Client As A Status.** This covers anyone who has paid and is currently a client.
14. **Add Churned Client As A Status.** This covers any client who has stopped working with you and is no longer active.
15. **Add Unqualified As A Status.** This is the status-level version of the Unqualified deal stage, for anyone disqualified outside of an active deal. These 9 deal stages and 5 statuses are all you need, and keeping it this simple is what keeps your CRM clean. Once you're taking hundreds of calls a month, it will get complicated enough on its own from sheer scale.
