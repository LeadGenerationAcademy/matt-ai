Everyone who tells you cold email does not work means it does not work for them.

It works, and it is one of the best ways to get clients in a B2B business. It is also a skill like any other. You have to learn it, practice it, and execute it. You do not get to try it once, fail, and declare the channel dead. Imagine falling down as a kid learning to walk and deciding walking just is not for you.

Cold email is not hard. It is tedious. Most of what is ahead is buying things, pasting DNS records, and waiting. Put on headphones, get a coffee, and knock it out.

### Buy The Setup Or Learn It

You can buy the done-for-you cold email setup from Smart Lead, ListKit, or Instantly. They are all basically the same and there is no real preference between them. They manage the deliverability, give you the lists and the leads, and it costs maybe 50% more than something that was not expensive to begin with.

That is the recommendation. If you take it, skip ahead and start at Step #5A when you write your copy.

If you want to own it, learn it, and run it yourself, do every step.

### You Have To Call Them

This one is not optional and there is no step page for it, because it is a hire.

The sequence is simple. You blast the list. Someone replies. Your Slack lights up. A human calls them within minutes and sets the call.

Text first, using your own name, saying they replied to your email. That way your name shows on the screen when the phone rings instead of an unknown number. Then double call to get through do not disturb.

You will book three, five, or ten times more calls doing this. People get so many emails now that a reply sitting in an inbox for six hours is a dead reply.

Do not do the calling yourself. Hire a cold caller. And if you already know you are not going to hire one, skip cold email entirely, because you are not going to succeed at it.

### Why You Never Send From Your Main Domain

Cold email gets marked as spam far more than anything else you send. Enough of that and the domain is burned, and from then on everything you send lands in junk, spam, or promotions.

So you buy separate domains that sit next to your real one. If your main domain is [leadgenerationacademy.com](http://leadgenerationacademy.com), you buy [tryleadgenerationacademy.com](http://tryleadgenerationacademy.com) and [getleadgenerationacademy.com](http://getleadgenerationacademy.com). All .com, no dashes, no numbers, no clever misspellings, no buzzwords. Every one of them redirects back to your real website, so when someone pastes your domain into a browser they land somewhere real.

The math sets the number. One inbox sends about 30 to 40 emails a day. Thirty inboxes across thirty domains gets you to roughly 1,000 emails a day, Monday to Friday. Back in the day that booked one or two calls a day. Spam filters are better now and more people are doing this, so treat that as the floor, not the promise.

### Deliverability Is The Whole Game

If your emails land in spam, fewer people see them. Fewer people seeing them means fewer replies, fewer replies means fewer calls, and fewer calls means fewer sales. Every single thing in the setup traces back to this.

SPF, DMARC, and DKIM go on every domain before you connect anything to a sending tool. That part is boring and it is not negotiable.

Then you warm the inboxes for 14 to 30 days. The tool adds them to a pool where they email each other, open a percentage, and reply to a percentage, so Microsoft and Google start reading them as a person instead of a machine.

Send Monday to Friday, starting 9am Eastern, because that is when real people send email. Stop the sequence the moment someone replies or applies. Continuing to hit someone who already responded is how you get reported.

### Your List Has To Be 80% Right

How you build the list depends entirely on who you sell to. Apollo scraped with Apify covers a lot of it. A Google Maps scraper is usually better for local businesses. There are dozens of other ways and it would be irresponsible to hand you one method as though it fits everybody.

What does not change is the check. Every lead needs a first name, email, website, and company. Then you pull a sample, 25 or 50 or 80 of them, and go through it by hand.

At least 80% has to actually be your ICP. It is never going to be perfect. If it is under 80%, your source or your filters are wrong and you fix that before you send a single email to it.

### Three Emails, Not Ten

The whole sequence is three emails.

The pitch. A follow-up with supporting details and an example. Then a short ask about whether this should just become a general follow-up.

Almost every qualified reply you get comes from one of those three. Anything past that is asking to be reported as spam. If they have not answered by the second follow-up, they were never going to.

This is the opposite of normal sales follow-up, and that is on purpose. These people never asked to hear from you. Once they reply, the rules flip and you follow up as long as it takes.

### Every Word Gets Spun

Spam filters compare your emails to each other. Send the same thing a thousand times and it does not matter how warm your inboxes are.

Spintax fixes that. You take a word and give it a pile of variations, and the tool picks one at random every time it sends.

Do it to every word, not a handful. At least 20 variations each. Spin your own name too, Matthew Larsen, Matt Larsen, Matthew L, Matt L, and so on. Their first name randomizes for free because it comes off the list.

Get enough variation stacked up and you can send millions of emails without two of them being identical. Use AI to generate it, because doing it by hand is a waste of your week.

### Track What You Send

Every day you log how many emails went out and how many DMs went out, in the inputs spreadsheet.

The easiest way to actually get this done is an end of day report. It does not automate well, so stop looking for a way around it.

This matters because bad metrics leave the inputs out, and leaving the inputs out hides the fact that nothing is being sent. No cause, no effect.
