The cold email filters are very good. We're going to add something called spintax.

The way you would do this is different for every cold email tool, so you need to reference whatever cold email tool you've chosen and write it in that way.

Spintax work like this: you have a word, and then a bunch of variations of it. When you add enough spintax, essentially no cold email is the same.

Let's say you have 50 different versions of spintax, and you spintax every single word, you could send millions and millions and millions of emails, and none of them would be the same.

You also want variations of your own name, like:

- Matthew Larsen
- Matthew L
- Matt Larsen
- Matt L
- Matthew B Larsen
- Matt B Larsen
- etc.

Plus the infinite randomness of their first name, since you'll already have that from your cold email list.

Make sure you spintax every single word, I would definitely use AI to do it. You're going to write your cold email copy in Step #5A however you want it, and then spintax every word.

### Framework & Criteria

1. **Spintax:** different for every cold email tool, reference whatever tool you've chosen and write your spintax in its specific format.
2. **Minimum Variations:** at least 20 variables per spintax, plus their first name.
3. **Name Variations:** create several versions of your own name, for example Matthew Larsen, Matthew L, Matt Larsen, Matt L, Matthew B Larsen, Matt B Larsen.
4. **First Name Randomness:** comes for free from your cold email list, since every lead already has their own first name.
5. **Coverage:** spintax every single word in the copy, not just a few.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Check your cold email tool's spintax format.** This is different for every tool, so reference whichever one you've chosen and write your spintax to match its specific format.
2. **Create at least 20 variables per spintax, plus their first name.** This is the minimum to get real randomization going, fewer than that and patterns start repeating across your sends.
3. **Create several variations of your own name.** Things like Matthew Larsen, Matthew L, Matt Larsen, Matt L, Matthew B Larsen, and Matt B Larsen all count as separate versions to spin between.
4. **Use AI to generate spintax variations for every word in your Step #5A copy.** This is the fastest way to actually hit that 20-variable minimum per word, doing it manually word by word would take forever.
5. **Apply spintax to every word across all three emails.** Don't skip words, the more of the copy that's spintax, the less likely any two emails you send ever look the same. Your recipient's first name will already vary naturally since it's pulled straight from your cold email list.
6. **Ask ChatGPT, Claude, or the AI built into these platforms if you get stuck on any step.** Interfaces change constantly, so if something on screen doesn't match what's described here, let AI walk you through the current version.
