Before you make this offer, white-label and referral partnerships are typically the exact same type of person. It's just a matter of how they prefer to work, do they want to refer to you, or do they want to white-label you?

You should also use your brain and ask, "Does my offer make sense to white-label?" Sometimes it will, and sometimes it won't.

When you're building this offer, it'll be the same as it is for your clients. It's the same offer, but you're going to want to knock 25% off the price for your white-label partner because they're going to have to make money too. If they make 25% margin, that's good for them.

This is also acceptable because you didn't have to pay any acquisition cost, pay for paid ads, or pay any sales commission on this. You're never going to get a good white-label partnership if you're selling it at the same price, so make sure you have it 25% off.

Then you're going to have to come up with a very keen explanation about how you're not going to embarrass them. We're going to talk about that more in Steps #2B and #2C when we create the actual presentations for these. You're going to need something like time in business, case studies, all that kind of stuff.

Finally, the third piece here is a risk-free way for them to introduce you to their clients in a white label. White label basically means they're going to pretend they're doing it themselves, they handle the relationship, and you're just billing them.

### Framework & Criteria

1. **Price:** The same offer as your clients, priced 25% off, since the partner isn't paying acquisition cost, ad spend, or sales commission, and needs their own margin.
2. **Credibility:** A clear explanation of why you won't embarrass them, such as time in business or case studies.
3. **Risk-Free Introduction:** A risk-free way for them to introduce you to their clients, where they handle the relationship and you just bill them.

### Example

**Offer:** Landing pages for Shopify ecom brands

**Price:** $2,500 per page is what your clients pay. $1,875 per page is what the white-label partner pays, which is 25% off.

**Credibility:** 77 agencies white label us. 200+ pages shipped a month.

**Risk-Free Introduction:** We redesign the above-the-fold section of one of their client's pages for free. Their client decides from there, the partner keeps the whole relationship, and we just bill the partner.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Decide whether your offer actually makes sense to white-label.** White label means the partner pretends they are doing the work themselves, handles the relationship with their client, and you just bill them. Sometimes that makes sense for your offer and sometimes it won't.
2. **Use the same offer you already sell to your clients as the base.** The white-label offer is the exact same offer your clients get. What changes is who you sell it to and what they pay for it.
3. **Price it 25% below what your clients pay.** The partner has to make money too, and a 25% margin is good for them. You can afford it because you didn't pay any acquisition cost, ad spend, or sales commission to get this client.
4. **Write an explanation of why you won't embarrass them.** This will almost always be their main objection. They need to know your work gets results, gets delivered on time, and that you won't suddenly ghost them. Answer it with your time in business, case studies, testimonials and reviews.
5. **Build a risk-free way for them to introduce you to their clients.** Pick something free that doesn't take you long to produce, like an audit or one free piece of work. This makes it easy for the partner to offer you to their client, and it can get you 2-3x better results.

### Completion Audit

- Did you decide whether your offer actually makes sense to white-label?
- Did you use the same offer you already sell to your clients as the base?
- Is it priced 25% below what your clients pay?
- Did you write an explanation of why you won't embarrass them?
- Did you build a risk-free way for them to introduce you to their clients?
