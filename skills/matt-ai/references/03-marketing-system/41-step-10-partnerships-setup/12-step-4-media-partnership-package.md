Media partners have one question. Why should they care?

The bigger their audience, the more it matters. There is risk in having you on. If they have a huge podcast and you make a boring episode, that is bad for their brand.

Your story, your portfolio, and the top 10 things you would talk about are what answer that question. What they are getting out of it is interesting content, and the content is you.

### Framework & Criteria

1. **Result / Dream Outcome / Headline:** What their audience walks away with, in one line.
2. **Your Details:** Your name, company name, website and YouTube channel.
3. **What I'd Talk About:** The top ten things you would talk about if they chose you.
4. **What's In It For You:** The fee, and what the episode or issue does for their audience.
5. **Traffic I Bring Myself:** Whether you will promote it yourself, on what channels, and to how many people.
6. **Timeline:** When you are available and how fast you can turn it around.
7. **Cost:** What you pay them, or what they pay you, whichever way it runs.
8. **Risk-Free Offer For Their Audience:** What their audience can get for free off the back of it.
9. **Where I've Appeared + When + Audience + Result:** Ten past appearances, every line linked to the actual episode, issue or talk.
10. **Five Minute Video:** A little video of yourself, for bonus points.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Write your story.** There is one criteria for pitching media partners, and that is why they should care. The bigger their audience, the more this matters.
2. **Make a copy of the [Media Partnership One-Pager Template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Wy1Ha1IQeAfgxetjSsUkkmPSQ01sRXgWbcpRaJhyzw0/edit?usp=sharing) and go to the Media One-Pager Template tab.** Media partners have an audience instead of clients, so this tab has different fields from the other two. [in this skill: assets/templates/media-partnership-one-pager-template/]
3. **Write your Result / Dream Outcome / Headline.** Keep it to one line about what their audience walks away with.
4. **Fill in your name, company name, website and YouTube, and hyperlink your website and YouTube channel.** They should be able to see your real work in one click.
5. **Write down the top ten things you would talk about.** Make these specific topics. If they have a huge podcast and you make a boring episode, that is bad for their brand.
6. **Write What's In It For You.** Lead with what the episode or issue gives their audience. What they are getting is interesting content, and the content is you.
7. **Write the traffic you bring yourself.** Say whether you will promote it, on what channels, and to how many people. You will get far more media partnerships if you bring your own attention.
8. **Write the Timeline.** Put when you are available and how fast you can turn it around.
9. **Decide whether you are willing to pay to be featured, then write the Cost.** In some cases the promotion you get will far exceed what it costs to be featured. Put what you pay them, or what they pay you, whichever way it runs.
10. **Write the risk-free offer for their audience.** Something their audience can get for free off the back of it, with no call and no card.
11. **Add ten past appearances with where, when, the audience size and the result, and hyperlink every one.** Link each line to the actual episode, issue or talk. If you have no past appearances, your YouTube channel works.
12. **Confirm it still fits on one page.** If it has run onto a second page, cut until it does not.
13. **Record a five minute video of yourself.** This one is for bonus points. It lets them see how you come across before they put you in front of their audience.
