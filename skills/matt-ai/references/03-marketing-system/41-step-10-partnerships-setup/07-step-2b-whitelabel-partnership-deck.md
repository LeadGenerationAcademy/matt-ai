---
You're going to want to make about an 11-page deck with this offer. We've provided a template below.

You shouldn't have to sell this with the same degree of intensity that you sell clients. Tell them who, what, when, where, why, how, the timeline, how it works, what's in it for them, how you're not going to embarrass them, and the offer.

You should be good. I would definitely go on the side of clarity in this over salesmanship or marketing.

### Framework & Criteria

1. **Who:** Who you are.
2. **What:** What you're offering.
3. **When:** When this applies or takes place.
4. **Where:** Where this applies or takes place.
5. **Why:** Why this partnership makes sense.
6. **How:** How this works at a high level.
7. **Timeline:** The timeline involved.
8. **How It Works:** The mechanics of how it actually works.
9. **What's In It For Them:** What's in it for them specifically.
10. **How You're Not Going To Embarrass Them:** Why they can trust you not to embarrass them.
11. **The Offer:** The actual offer itself.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Make a copy of the [Whitelabel Partnership Deck Template](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1947FLvdj4dsWBpEXxRlLcSvRci2gRgF_C5g1HjykJwo/edit?slide=id.p#slide=id.p).** This is where you turn the white-label offer from the previous step into the deck you send to partners. The first slide links to a finished example deck you can check whenever you get stuck. [in this skill: assets/templates/whitelabel-partnership-deck-template/]
2. **Write the Who section.** Say who you are, what your company does, and how many businesses already work with you. The example deck opens with "77 Agencies Already White Label Our Pages."
3. **Write the What section.** Explain exactly what you are offering them and that it goes out under their name. If partners usually add your service to their existing retainer, say that here too.
4. **Write the When section.** List the situations where they should send you a client. In the example, that includes a client about to churn and a brand new client.
5. **Write the Where section.** Describe which of their clients this is built for, like the platform they are on or how much they already spend. This tells the partner exactly which clients to send you.
6. **Write the Why section.** Explain the problem their clients have that you solve, and how that problem hurts the partner. The example deck says their clients are churning because of the page the ads point at, and the agency is the one who gets fired for it.
7. **Write the How section.** Give a high-level view of who does what. Make it clear they own the relationship with their client and you never contact their client.
8. **Write the Timeline section.** Lay out the specific days from the first free piece of work to the finished delivery. The example goes from a free above-the-fold redesign in days 1-5 to the page going live on day 21.
9. **Write the How It Works section.** Go through the mechanics step by step: what they send you, how many revision rounds they get, when you invoice them, and whether there are contracts or minimums.
10. **Write the What's In It For Them section.** Everyone cares about themselves first. Cover how it benefits them money-wise and how it benefits their clients.
11. **Write the How You're Not Going To Embarrass Them section.** Use the explanation you wrote in Step #2A. Dated case studies with the result and how long it took work best here.
12. **Write The Offer section.** State the white-label price from Step #2A, how you bill it, and the free offer their client can try first.
13. **Favor clarity over salesmanship throughout the deck.** You shouldn't have to sell a partner with the same degree of intensity you sell clients. Rewrite anything that reads like marketing into a plain explanation.
