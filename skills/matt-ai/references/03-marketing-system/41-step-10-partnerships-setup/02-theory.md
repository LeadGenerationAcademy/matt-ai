Partnerships aren't common for a lot of people, but they're one of the most powerful ways to grow, especially as you climb in revenue.

This is easier than it looks, so don't overthink it. The only question you need to ask is: who's already got my clients?

For example, if you ran a paid ads lead generation agency, who's already got your clients?

- Cold outreach agencies
- Content agencies
- SEO agencies
- Web development agencies
- CRMs

That's the exercise. Once you see it, you'll find these categories everywhere.

### Types Of Partnerships

There are three types of partnerships:

- White label
- Referral
- Media partners (relevant communities, newsletters, events, speaking gigs, YouTube channels, influencers, and anyone who isn't working with your clients directly but has their attention)

About 90% of the time, white label and referral partners are the exact same type of person and the same ICP. The difference isn't who they are, it's how they prefer to work with you, whether they want to refer you out or put their own name on your work.

You'll secure these partnerships mostly through outreach, and just like with clients, having a great offer is what actually gets you partners.

### White Label & Referral Partnership Offers

These offers need to hit three criteria:

1. What's in it for them beyond a referral fee?
  - Everyone gets a referral fee, that's not what wins the partnership.
  - At my CRO agency, which I sold in 2023, I had over $400k in MRR from white label partnerships, mostly Facebook ad agencies.
  - Their pitch to their own clients was that they were churning because of a bad funnel or landing page, not bad ads, and fixing that made the ads perform better, lowered churn, and raised LTV. That's what was in it for them.
2. How do they know you won't embarrass them?
  - You can't guarantee it, but you have to convince them of it anyway, that you won't mess things up for their clients.
  - This is usually done with proof and a track record, time in business, case studies, that kind of thing.
3. What risk-free offer can you give their clients?
  - At that same CRO agency, I offered to redesign the above-the-fold section of anyone's landing page for free, then let them decide if they wanted to continue.
  - That takes the risk off the partner, since they're the one putting their name on the line to refer or white label you.
  - If the client decides to buy after that, they're not buying on the partner's word anymore, they're buying because of the experience they had with you directly.

### Media Partnership Offers

Media partnership offers use the same three-part structure, but the second criterion is different:

1. What's in it for them? Same as before, a referral fee.
2. Will you bring your own traffic and promote it yourself? This is the one that changes, and it matters a lot to a media partner.
3. What risk-free offer can you pitch alongside it?

Two of the three criteria are identical to white label and referral partnerships. The only real difference is the second one.

### Building And Enriching Your Dream 400 List

Once you have your categories, you build them out into a full Dream 400 list, 300 ideal white label or referral partners and 100 ideal media partners. Pick these carefully, more carefully than you'd pick your own clients. This is a power law situation, somewhere around 1-2% of these partners will end up driving 97% of your actual results. A lot of these will go nowhere, a friendly "yeah, we're partners" and nothing more, and that's fine, it's still worth doing.

Once the list exists, you enrich it, pulling each founder's email, LinkedIn, phone, Instagram, Twitter, and anything else you can find, using a tool like Clay or whatever gets the job done. Then you clean it, through something like Million Verifier, a VA checking it manually, or both, since bad data just wastes the outreach you're about to run.

### Running Outreach

With a clean list, you run a coordinated omni-channel campaign, email, phone, LinkedIn, and Twitter, or whatever else makes sense for your list. Early on, a spreadsheet is good enough to run this, you can build something more advanced later once it's proven out.

At 400 partners, that works out to about 40 touches per channel per day, working through the entire list every 10 working days, Monday to Friday, every two weeks.

### Staying On Top Of Responses

Speed to lead isn't as critical with partnerships as it is with clients or ad funnels, it's more forgiving, but you still don't want to miss anything.

Set up your partnership call booking and route notifications from your email, LinkedIn, and any other outreach channel into Slack so nothing slips through.

Run this yourself for the first two weeks so you actually learn what's working, then hand inbox management off to a VA or team member, who manages the day-to-day and passes any real response back to you.

### Tracking Your Outreach

Log your outreach volume daily, across every channel, in your inputs spreadsheet. This matters because bad metrics often leave inputs out entirely, and that hides the fact that nothing is actually being done. No cause, no effect.
