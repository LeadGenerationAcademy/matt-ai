I ran a landing page and CRO agency for ecommerce stores on Shopify. Early to mid 2022 through mid 2023. I owned 100% of it and took it from zero to $900,000 per month in recurring revenue.

iOS 14 had just come out and completely tanked everyone's ad performance. Ecom brands needed a way to get their CPA back down and CRO was the answer. There was very little competition at the time, which surely helped.

The first thing I did that led to a tremendous amount of growth was partnerships. White label and referral specifically.

### The Brainstorm

All I did was ask one question, and it got me 80% of the way there. Who sells to the same customers as me, but is not selling a competing service?

Ecom stores needed Facebook ads, Google Ads, SEO, email marketing, influencer marketing, and UGC. A few of my partners ended up being landing page and CRO SaaS tools or platforms, and some were affiliate marketplaces. Almost all the rest were other marketing agencies in ecom.

For every service I had three go-to options at different price points. A US-based SEO company, an Eastern Europe-based one, and a Philippines-based one. I always had something for every budget.

Facebook ads stood out far above the rest. Most ecom brands get 90% or more of their customers from Meta, which is exactly why landing pages and CRO are so valuable. They are part of what makes Meta ads work.

### The Pitch

Every good partnership pitch has three things and most people only do the first one.

**The fee.** Anyone will give them a referral fee. It does not make you special. Only focusing on it is stupid.

**How it helps their LTV or CAC.** Your ads are actually going to convert and your clients will churn less. If you are running ads to a landing page that has almost no chance, it does not matter if you have the best click-through rate and the cheapest cost per click in the entire world. It is not going to work and people are going to churn.

**A special offer for their referrals that eliminates all risk.** I gave a free above the fold mockup and offer plan for every one of their clients, done while basically pretending I worked for them, which is what white label is.

Same three parts for white label and referral. It was the same offer for both.

### The Whitelabel Offer

$5,000 per month, unlimited landing pages, maximum two in production at a time. In effect that meant four to eight pages a month.

It was sticky. Once a paid ad agency starts offering landing pages it is hard for them to just stop. Their clients expect it and it is built into the retainer by then. They also could not hire someone internally for $5,000 a month and get the same quality and results.

### The Referral Offer

Some agencies did not want to white label. Not enough need for it, did not want to deal with it, or too few clients for the volume to make sense. Those ones just referred people to me and took $500 per sale.

I made it a habit to pay everyone within an hour of closing. Too many people either forget to pay or make it so tedious that it leaves a bad taste in the referrer's mouth, and then they stop referring long term. Paying inside the hour turned it into a dopamine loop instead.

### Referring Out

It ran both directions. I referred huge numbers of people out and I always made sure I got a fee for it.

My onboarding document and onboarding call had a form for intros. Do you need a Facebook ads agency, a Google ads agency, a TikTok ads agency, an influencer agency, an SEO agency, an email agency, an accountant, a bookkeeper, a lawyer, a broker to get you into retail, tracking or attribution, an agency ad account, AB testing tools. Ten to fifteen options. Some clients asked for five or ten at once.

Instead of the standard 10% recurring commission for life, I negotiated 100% of the first month retainer and nothing after that. On a partner charging $5,000 a month, that is $5,000 up front instead of $500 a month.

Explaining why mattered. The cash flow goes straight back into acquiring more customers, which sends them more referrals. When you explain it that way instead of just being greedy, everybody is game and they give you a much better fee.

### Prospecting

6,000 cold emails per day to potential partners, separate from the 20,000 per day I was sending to Shopify store owners. Thousands per day to every type of ecom agency.

Partners were far easier to reach than clients. Ecom stores get buried in outreach and it is brutally competitive. Ecom agencies get far less of it, so my response rate was much better and they were much more likely to stick with me.

### Inbox Management

I had nine appointment setters at any given time. Three in Mexico and South America, three in South Africa and Eastern Europe, three in Indonesia, Malaysia, or the Philippines. Three online at all times in eight hour shifts, 24/7.

Any reply to any cold email fired a notification in Slack and a script in Google Sheets. The setter called and texted right away. The text went out with the first name so it showed up as a person instead of an unknown number, and they double called to get through the weaker do not disturb settings.

Calling instantly was the number one difference maker. Two to five times the result for the same effort.

### What It Produced

77 paid ad agencies at peak, each paying me $5,000 per month. About $385,000 per month in recurring revenue with very low churn, because they were locked in.

Getting that $385,000 from partners was five to ten times easier than getting the same number from clients.

Referral partnerships added another $100,000 to $200,000 per month, most of it one-time revenue from landing page builds.

40 referral booked calls per week at a close rate over 50%. About 20 new clients a week.

Referring out averaged $3,500 per successful referral against a blended CAC of $1,077. One referral covered the acquisition cost of over three other customers. Several times I got more than $15,000 in commissions from a single client.

That process completely eliminated my customer acquisition cost, and it was the number one thing that allowed me to scale that fast.
