# 12+ LTV:CAC

Course: Core Business Thesis & Philosophy
System: Core

If you want a business that shoots off cash, my target is at least $12 in lifetime gross profit for every $1 spent acquiring a customer. Customer acquisition cost, or CAC, is what you spend to get that customer.

Gross profit lifetime value, or LTV, is everything they pay you over the relationship, minus the direct cost of delivering what they bought. If they pay you $15,000, fulfillment costs $3,000, and acquiring them costs $1,000, that is 12:1.

Increase your price, decrease churn by keeping customers longer, decrease fulfillment costs, or decrease acquisition costs.

The previous target is about cash available now. This one is about how profitable the customer becomes over time.

This is completely separate from, yet highly related to, the 2x cash collected on the front end versus acquisition plus fulfillment cost. You really don't necessarily want one or the other. If you don't have that cash collected version, this LTV-to-CAC might be okay on paper, but your cash flow is going to be fried. If you have that 2x plus cash collected without the corresponding good LTV, you're not really going to make that much money. You need both, but they're separate still.
