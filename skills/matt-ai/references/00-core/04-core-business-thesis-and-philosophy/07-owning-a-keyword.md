# "Owning" A Keyword

Course: Core Business Thesis & Philosophy
System: Core
Skool link: https://www.skool.com/leadgenerationacademy/classroom/889a0260?md=d2c6f151124c4b158edb00769409a468

---
You can make huge gains in content and paid ads when you begin to own a word in the mind of your target market. If someone thinks of lead generation, I want them to think of Matthew Larsen immediately. That is what I mean by owning a keyword.

You do this by generating fans, not just followers, and giving value instead of only worrying about views.

Your aim is to create a bingeable series of content that solves every single problem your target market would face.

Some insane things happen to you when you start to own a keyword, but it is not an easy process.

Just think of how much competition you have and how much you stand out. That is why I would say this is highly relevant to developing your own philosophy. If you have your own philosophy, which is another part of this core business thesis and philosophy, this will become easier.

Imagine how much money you would make if you owned an entire keyword in the audience, just like:

- Shelby Sapp owns sales for women.
- Jeremy Haynes owns $1 million per month.
- Ed Lawrence owns YouTube.
- Alex Hormozi basically owns business.

This is why making a lot of niche content, and content that's even more niche than perhaps you're used to or perhaps you like, is a good way to help you own this. If you're an email marketing agency for e-commerce stores, just email marketing is a very competitive market with tons of competitors, making it very difficult to own. Compare that with something like email marketing for subscription brands. It's a little bit easier. Then you could even go further than that.

What you're trying to do is usually create a bingeable series of content based around the problems and circumstances your ICP finds themselves in, completely solving their problem by focusing on giving value and not chasing views. This is about keeping production value minimal while giving a lot of value and good advice. It's also about keeping topics focused, and a lot of the time, these are going to be niche topics that won't get a lot of views, but they'll get the right views.

Then it's about keeping this up and staying very, very focused on this process over time so that you create a lot of fans and not followers.

Fans are what pay the bills. Followers don't.

I can barely scratch 3,000 views on a YouTube video, and I made $9 million off YouTube in 2024 and $7 million off YouTube in 2025. That is the power of this. I basically owned agency lead generation for a time.

The point is that you want to get so good at creating content that you elevate your standards every single time you post. You want to become the hook or thumbnail that makes people click and watch because they know it's going to be so good and so likely to solve their problem. It basically doesn't fucking matter what you put in your thumbnail or what you say for your hook. They're just going to consume it because it's you.

The mindset for achieving this is: How do I make every piece of content I put out 10 times better than the previous best? You'll probably not achieve this, quite frankly, but the mindset is what matters. Even if you can't achieve 10 times better, you might achieve 5 times, 2 times, 50%, or 25% better. When you do it every single day, you will achieve an insanely good result.

You can't use AI for this at all. AI has surface-level value and understanding of everything, and everybody's using it. If you use AI, you're just going to be like everybody else, and you're not going to own shit.

**Concepts:**

- Fans, Not Followers
- Value, Not Views
- Binge-able Series Of Content
- Expectations
- Algorithm
- Your Face Is The Hook/Thumbnail
- 10x Standards
