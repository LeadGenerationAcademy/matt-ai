Reverse engineering means starting with how much net profit you want to make and working backward to see exactly what it takes to get there, including how much you'd need to spend on ads every day.

Most people find out they're not doing as good as they thought. Once the numbers are in front of you, they become very easy to improve.

### Framework & Criteria

1. **Net Margin:** The percentage of your revenue you keep as net profit.
2. **Monthly Churn Rate:** The percentage of clients you lose each month.
3. **Median Monthly Retainer:** The middle value of what your clients pay you per month.
4. **Close Rate:** The percentage of booked calls that become clients.
5. **Cost Per Booked Call:** What you pay to get one call booked.

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Make a copy of the [Reverse Engineering Sheet Template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1oe9aSPQvqiGhuWr-LV3osKEJ5NiVIRpwhAO8DCanwJI/edit?usp=sharing).** You need five numbers to reverse engineer a net profit goal accurately. This sheet works backward from your goal using all five. [in this skill: assets/templates/reverse-engineering-sheet-template/]
2. **Enter your Net Profit Goal.** Everything else on the sheet works backward from this number.
3. **Enter your Net Margin.** Use your real number, not the one in your head. Nobody ever underestimates their net margin. It's always higher in their brain than it is in reality.
4. **Enter your Monthly Churn Rate.** Don't take your own word for this one. If you think your churn is 5% but your average client stays three months, your churn is actually 33%. A 16.7% churn rate is a six-month average client relationship, which is very standard. Anything below 7% is excellent.
5. **Enter your Median Monthly Retainer.** The middle value of what clients pay per month, not the average.
6. **Enter your Close Rate.** The percentage of booked calls that turn into paying clients.
7. **Enter your Cost Per Booked Call.** What you pay in ads to get one call booked.
8. **Look at how much you need to spend on ads per day to hit your goal.** This is reverse engineering in a nutshell. You'll almost always find out you're not doing as good as you thought you were. That's a big eye-opener.
9. **Change one metric at a time and watch how much the daily ad spend changes.** These five metrics are the leverage points in your business. A very average business can need $2,125 per day on ads to make $1 million in net profit, while a very good business needs $85 per day for the same million. It's just arithmetic.
10. **Keep these five numbers in front of you.** Almost magically, things start to improve once you're actually measuring them.
