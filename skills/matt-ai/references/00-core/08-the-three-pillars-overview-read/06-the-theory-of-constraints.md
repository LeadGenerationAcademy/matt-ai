The Theory of Constraints is about identifying the part of a system that currently limits its performance. Improving that part gives the rest of the system room to produce more. Improving something else may do very little while the same constraint remains.

That is what the diagram is illustrating.

You could have a business and funnel designed at a 10/10 level, but if your ability to execute is at a 2/10 level, you will struggle to get the results they are capable of producing.

Perhaps you never finish building the funnel. Perhaps you stop following up, or keep changing direction before you have done enough work to see what happens. The other two pillars cannot make up for work that never gets done.

In that situation, you are the constraint.

But the constraint can also be in the business or the funnel. If you are doing the work consistently but losing money on every customer, you need to address the business economics. If the service sells and you deliver it well, but hardly anyone finds you, you need to work on the funnel.

As you improve one constraint, something else can become the thing holding you back.

Find what is limiting you → improve it → identify what is limiting you next.

We will work on all three pillars throughout the course. We start with you because improving your ability to do the work helps you make the changes the other two need.
