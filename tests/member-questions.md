## Offer

### T01 (ids 104, 107, 110)
Member: We run a DFY YouTube agency. Our offer is "Get 100k views in 3 months or get a full refund." Running Meta ads to it. Thoughts?
Matt: bruv commits every sin in the book with this offer. long time frame, a range (10-20) and then a work for free until you do guarantee (why would they want you to work for free if you just failed them). i would never do a youtube offer for people who are not already actively doing youtube. i think it is very difficult to do DFY for beginners at anything. 5-10x as much work as you have to do your actual stuff but also deal with their self limiting beliefs and other crap, their inexperience, learning curve, etc.

### T02 (ids 260, 262, 267)
Member: We redid our landing page (DFY YouTube for coaches, "$10M YouTube Acquisition System"). Do you think the offer is clear and specific? Would you put the guarantee in the subheadline?
Matt: "We plan, script, edit, design and publish every video, and we track every dollar it makes. All you do is send a voice note and film." Make this your subheadline. Takes a long time to find out any details about your 10m acquisition system, scrolling down or in the VSL. Would have the guarantee in the headline if I use a guarantee: X Result Or Y Guarantee as the headline format. It is better. I don't like the offer tho. "$10m YouTube Acquisition System" is completely meaningless and vague for an ad viewer. If you want this offer I'd do a webinar funnel not a call funnel.

### T03 (ids 295, 302, 304)
Member: Our best DFY YouTube clients already do YouTube, or did and went dark, and spend at least $20k/mo on ads. Should the ads target people who already do YouTube or people who don't?
Matt: i would never recommend running this to people who don't already do youtube as an ad. i think that is terrible. i would definitely explicitly call out coaches who already do youtube and make the offer for people already doing youtube. it is such a waste to try and convince people who are not doing youtube already to try and do it. u need to reflect this in the ad and on the lp.

### T04 (ids 1000, 1084, 1098)
Member: Did anyone here ever run a DFY high ticket offer with an AOV of $25k to $50k paid in full or financing? Planning to launch something like that.
Matt: Incredibly risky to do financing on that AOV. Financing will take 8-12% off the top and if they chargeback or anything dumb happens, they don't give back the 8-12%, which at that level puts you out 5-8k. Almost every financing company collapses eventually, especially if it is B2C financing. i am also against it because i simply would not want to work with/do business with someone who needs financing. Generally speaking a recipe for disaster.

### T05 (ids 1638, 1639, 1645, 1649, 1659)
Member: Is performance-based pricing a good idea for restoration companies and home services? Clients lie about where leads came from and bill offline so you can't track it.
Matt: Yes I would agree with that. performance only is the bell curve midwit meme. Great for beginners to get off the ground when no one really trusts them yet, great for the best of the best because they have the leverage to enforce it and the skill to succeed with it, pretty bad for anyone in the middle. life is very difficult without some level of recurring revenue. Your business doesn't need to be 100% recurring ofc but. Seems that a lot of service providers are obsessed with performance offers, but a lot of clients don't really even like that. A lot of clients would just like to know it's going to cost this much, and then they can plan for it. I tried performance-only percentage of the lift in CRO when I had an ecom agency. I would always get fired when I was too successful because they didn't want to pay me like $90K when the test went too well. Went back to retainers only after long.

### T06 (ids 1463, 1496, 1499, 1505, 1510, 1519)
Member: How would you structure a pay-per-lead offer when buyers are small local businesses who can't afford a 100-lead first order at 100 euros per lead? The course example needs about 100 leads upfront to hit 2X on the front end. If my clients can only buy 10 to 30 leads at a time, would you accept being under 2X on the first order, or change something else? Also: how do you handle lead replacement, guarantees, and auto-charge authorization?
Matt: The dirty secret of this is you make zero money ever with small buyers. But, sadly, you need to go through this phase to build proof to get the big buyers. Would never work with someone who won't pay at least $5k up front. Replacement: I would tell them the qualifications on the form and then we only replace it if the phone number is disconnected. Guarantee: I would never offer a guarantee here. It would ruin you and pay per lead is enough. Auto charge: It is very difficult to get them on an auto charge authorization on the first 1 or 2 orders. We try to get them on one on order 3. Trying to do that before then will kill your CVR. You basically make zero money until the third order with anyone in pay per lead. All the biz op people promoting pay per lead agencies I would say have never done it before at a high level and they kind of flooded the industry with noobies. Pay per lead is not for someone who doesn't know what they are doing. Probably the worst model for a beginner. I only do pay per lead as a way to build my email list and then I make 100% of the margin emailing people about credit cards, loans, etc. and cross selling at 100% margin. I don't make any money anymore on the lead arbitrage, too thin. i generate for $13.35 and sell for $14.45 nowadays and team costs have to go out of that.

## Funnel and Ads

### T11 (ids 235, 236, 245, 252)
Member: I have an electrician client who wants to run ads for EV charger installs. Should I use (A) a simple book-a-call funnel or (B) a lead magnet to educate them? And with $500/month budget, how many ads should I test?
Matt: I would definitely do A, because people already understand what an EV charger is and why they would need it. Don't need an insane offer or lead magnet to educate them. You should just use a book a call funnel with a free inspection, but the free inspection is to sell the high ticket. If they already know what it is and why they would need it, then u just need to show them why you are the person to do it, which is a call funnel usually. Ad spend data is usually more about how many clicks do you need to decide rather than $. If you have $500 per month, that is $16 per day, I would not test more than 2-3 total ads in this campaign. Regardless you will need to go through the funnel course and the FB ads setup. There are templates for it all in there that are ideal top 1% templates.

## Webinars

### T18 (ids 878, 885, 893, 898, 925, 940, 948)
Member: I run a weekly live webinar selling a $1.9k SaaS direct to checkout, $1.2k to $1.8k/week spend, ROAS between 2.5x and 4x. When we run it to a bigger crowd the show rate drops a lot. We also have no ascension process into my agency. What should I fix?
Matt: Usually if it dies that much when targeting a bigger crowd it is a sign that your messaging is wrong. Think of it as your ad only performs when it is put in front of the top of the top/most likely to convert audience. I do a webinar to insurance agents too, usually around $6k per day spend, $2k price direct to checkout. I did the live version about 50 times until it converted 10% direct to checkout, and now we run the recording as a daily auto webinar that we pretend is live. That brought show rate from mid 20s to 40s. It is always the same day, scheduled at 7pm EST every day, not a just-in-time webinar that is obviously fake. If your webinar already converts well enough that is a real option and a high lever: maximizes audience quality, maximizes show rate. You have multiple concerns and you should focus first on the primary one. If that is show rate when you increase spend, just focus on that, then once it is ok, the next. No ascension is very bad. You should make them schedule an OB call and upsell them, preferably literally 1-2 hours after the webinar but next day at minimum. Any ascension rate benchmark would be completely made up. No two situations are the same. Mine on that webinar with a 2k price point ascends 15.5% to a 7k.

### T19 (ids 953, 954)
Member: What's a benchmark for front end ROAS?
Matt: If you have an end game such as upsell or ascension, honestly almost anything over breakeven is good. If not then you'll probably want 4x+ to make it worth it or you won't make any real money. I run my webinar at 1.17x ROAS.

## Sales

### T22 (ids 823, 825, 831)
Member: What would you say to prospects who ask to speak to your clients before signing? How do you handle that objection?
Matt: Tell them it is not your clients' job to sell your service, and that you have case studies on the website that they can review. Almost always people ask that as a nice way to get rid of you and say no. If you let them speak to clients they'd just make up a new objection anyways. 95%+ of time they are just not interested. You can easily prove this to be true: just tell them to put their credit card down and if they talk to the client and like it you can charge them. None will do it.

## Diagnosis and Unit Economics

### T25 (ids 1602, 1603)
Member: How do you figure out what the biggest constraint in a business is, and what to spend the 4-hour block on?
Matt: You would first determine if it is fulfillment, sales or marketing. If you just ask yourself "could I double the amount of customers right now and it would be fine": if yes, then not fulfillment. Will usually be obvious if it is sales or not, so will usually be marketing. Then there are four phases: clicks/traffic, calls booked, calls shown, calls closed. Usually will be obvious which is the worst one. Then you just have a list of tasks to try and fix.
